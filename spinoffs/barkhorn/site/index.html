Progress

Drawn to be made.

  1. Drawn.Atmosphere is drawn for making, part by part: more than eighty of them, each with its fabrication drawing. The horn’s profile and the cabinet’s bass were worked out by simulation before any acrylic was cut.
  2. Next, the first pair.Built as production will build it: the horn formed from the cabinet’s own tinted acrylic, the back screwed to the braces. A prototype is there to test the making.
  3. Then, measured.In a room, by ear and by microphone. The design figures on these pages give way to measured ones. The lamps and Mantle come next, the same way.

Mother EARF

Everything comes home.

Our things borrow their names from the Earth, core to atmosphere. Mother EARF is how we pay it back.

  1. Acrylic that has lived before.Cast acrylic can be broken back down to the liquid it was cast from and cast again. Recycled sheet isn’t yet made in our colours at 20 mm; when it is, we mean to use it.
  2. Made to be mended.Drivers and amplifiers any good technician knows. Screws, not glue, wherever a part may one day need to come out. Core’s battery changes with a screwdriver.
  3. Taken back at the end.When you’re done with a piece, we mean to take it back: what still works carries on, and the acrylic goes back to be cast again.

The horn

The oldest amplifier is a shape.

Long before there were amplifiers, a horn was how a small sound filled a large room: the gramophone’s, or a cupped hand’s. Ours is formed from the same tinted acrylic as its cabinet, its driver in a pod whose cap twists off by hand. Above 1 kHz the horn carries the music; below, a 15-inch woofer takes it, each on its own channel of the amplifier inside. A horn wastes little, so a little power goes a long way.

Details

The particulars

Height
1293 mm
Width
625 mm at the horn, 560 mm at the cabinet
Depth
423 mm overall, 420 mm at the cabinet
Weight
About 72 kg; 62 kg with the horn lifted off
Woofer
FaitalPRO 15PR400, 15-inch, with two ports in the front’s lower corners
Horn driver
B&C DE1085TN, 2-inch compression driver
Horn
Formed from the same tinted cast acrylic as its cabinet, a turned acrylic throat; the cabinet’s full depth
Amplifier
Hypex FusionAmp FA122, built in: two channels of 125 W, crossed at 1 kHz in its DSP
Bass
To about 51 Hz at −3 dB, before the room adds its own; the ports tuned to 38 Hz
Loudness
Up to about 115 dB at 1 m, each
Inputs
XLR (and a link out), RCA, AES, S/PDIF coaxial and optical; USB to set it up
Lining
Recycled polyester batts in white voile, 100 mm at the top and the bottom
Price
Set at launch; each pair is made to order
Care
Dust with a soft microfibre cloth; wash with water and a drop of mild soap, then dry. No glass cleaner, alcohol or solvents: they craze acrylic. Fine scratches polish out.
Figures
Design figures, from the drawings and their simulation; measured ones follow with the first pair

Lighting

The same eight, lit from within.

A pendant and a table lamp in the speakers’ acrylic, frosted inside, so the colour glows and the source never shows. Every shade comes off by hand.

CoreTable lamp

A ball of tinted acrylic sitting low in a black acrylic base. Plugged in, it stays put; unplugged, it goes where the conversation is.

See Core

Plugged in or not.

Core runs from its cable or from a battery in its base: about ten hours without the cable, and when the battery tires, you change the battery, not the lamp.
