# EARF: realistic funding paths (R2)

**As of:** 31 October 2026 (Day 0). Day 90 is 29 January 2027.
**Scope:** (a) $50,000 within 90 days; (b) a $500,000 to $1.5 million seed within 12 months.
**Method:** public web only; no forms filled, no accounts created, nobody contacted. Every factual claim carries a link. Anything marked **(est.)** is an estimate or analyst judgment, not a sourced fact. All "odds" are estimates for EARF specifically (one founder, no company, no prototypes, pre-revenue, high-ticket products).
**Caveat:** the shared web-search budget ran out part-way through. After that I only fetched official pages directly. The items I could not verify are listed in section 9.

## 0. Bottom line

1. **The $50k in 90 days is most likely to come as a stack, not as one cheque (est.).** The likely pieces are:
   - a 0% Kiva loan of up to $15k ([Kiva](https://www.kiva.org/borrow));
   - a CDFI loan from Working Solutions, which says it welcomes "first-time owners and pre-revenue businesses" ([Working Solutions](http://www.workingsolutions.org/loan-program));
   - founders'-edition deposits, taken once working prototypes exist;
   - in-kind support for the debut night from a brand partner and/or the city's Vacant to Vibrant pop-up programme ([Local News Matters, Mar 2026](https://localnewsmatters.org/2026/03/16/bcn-22-sf-pop-ups-march-15-2026/)).
2. **Equity and Kickstarter cannot realistically deliver cash by Day 90.**
   - Selling equity (a SAFE) needs a company to issue it. YC's own deal, for example, is two SAFEs ([YC deal](https://www.ycombinator.com/deal)).
   - Kickstarter requires "working prototypes" and does not allow photorealistic renderings ([Kickstarter rules](https://help.kickstarter.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005134554-What-are-the-rules-for-hardware-and-product-design-projects)).
   - Kickstarter pays out two to three weeks after a successful campaign ends ([Kickstarter Culture Fund page](https://landing.kickstarter.com/culture-fund)).
3. **A $500k to $1.5M seed in 12 months is plausible only after EARF proves demand with customers' money (est.).** The sequence would be:
   - Kickstarter for the lamps in Q2 2027, plus privately sold Tropos founders' editions;
   - then a round from collectors and customers who become angels, and/or a Reg CF community round (up to $5M in 12 months through an SEC-registered portal; [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/regulation-crowdfunding)).

   The main SF angel networks and seed funds I checked invest in tech, life science, software or deep tech. EARF does not fit any of them yet (section 4.1).
4. **The best date for the debut night is already in the window.** FOG Design+Art runs **21 to 24 January 2027** at Fort Mason ([FOG](https://fogfair.com/)). Its 2027 exhibitor list includes design-minded galleries such as Marta, Patrick Parrish Studio and Hostler Burrows ([FOG exhibitors](https://fogfair.com/exhibitors)). That week is Days 82 to 85. Hold the debut then.
5. **Two accelerator deadlines fall this weekend.**
   - a16z speedrun SR008 closes **1 Nov 2026, 11:59 pm PT** ([a16z](https://a16z.com/applications-for-a16z-speedrun-sr008-are-now-open/)).
   - YC Winter 2027 closes **2 Nov 2026, 8 pm PT** ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/apply)).

   Both are long shots for a pre-prototype hi-fi and lighting brand (est. 1 to 3%). Apply only if it costs no more than a couple of hours.

## 1. Starting point: which paths need what

| Requirement | Paths it gates | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Official business documentation** (not necessarily a company) | Kiva lends $1k to $15k. Without official documentation the cap drops to $1k to $3k. DBA/fictitious-name filings, EINs and registrations all count. | [Kiva](https://www.kiva.org/borrow) |
| **A company (entity)** | SAFEs/angels/accelerators ([YC deal](https://www.ycombinator.com/deal)); Reg CF (non-US companies ineligible; issuer files with SEC) | [SEC Reg CF guide](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/small-business-compliance-guides/regulation-crowdfunding-small-entity-compliance-guide-issuers) |
| **A working prototype** | Kickstarter hardware and product design projects | [Kickstarter rules](https://help.kickstarter.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005134554-What-are-the-rules-for-hardware-and-product-design-projects) |
| **12+ months operating and/or revenue** | Pacific Community Ventures loans (no startups under one year; must be generating revenue); Accion Opportunity Fund (12 months, per third-party reviews) | [PCV FAQ](https://pacificcommunityventures.org/get-a-loan/loan-faqs/), [Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/loans/small-business/reviews/accion-opportunity-fund) |
| **A commercial location** | SF First Year Free fee waivers | [SF Treasurer report](https://www.sftreasurer.org/file/fyf-report20250pdf/download) |
| **Stable revenue** | SF Storefront Opportunity Grant ($50k to $100k) | [sf.gov](https://www.sf.gov/apply-for-a-grant-to-open-a-new-storefront) |
| **An operating business** | SBA 7(a) loans (max $5M) | [SBA 7(a)](https://www.sba.gov/funding-programs/loans/7a-loans) |

**Implication: "form a company when the first money comes in" is too late for most paths.** Minimum this week:

- **File a fictitious business name (FBN) for "EARF".** California requires the filing within 40 days of starting to trade under the name ([B&P §17910](https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_17910)).
- **Register with the SF Treasurer & Tax Collector.** The deadline is 15 days after starting business in SF ([SF Treasurer](https://sftreasurer.org/business/register-business)).
- **Get an EIN.**

This is enough for Kiva, Working Solutions and pre-sales.

**Before taking any investor money, form a Delaware C-corp.** The Delaware minimum franchise tax is $175 (authorized-shares method) or $400 (assumed-par-value method), plus a $50 annual report due 1 March ([Delaware](https://corp.delaware.gov/frtax/)). California registration costs extra; I could not verify the amount (section 9). My recommendation (est.): if a seed round within 12 months is the plan, form the C-corp now, before taking deposits, so customer money and liability never sit with the founder personally.

### 4.3 Crowdfunding: benchmarks, price points and pitfalls

**Platform rules and fees (2026)**

- **Kickstarter**
  - Hardware projects must show "working prototypes" and may not use photorealistic renderings ([rules](https://help.kickstarter.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005134554-What-are-the-rules-for-hardware-and-product-design-projects)).
  - Fees are 5% plus about 3 to 5% processing, and only on successful projects ([fees](https://help.kickstarter.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005028634-What-are-the-fees)).
  - **Pledge Over Time** splits pledges of $125 or more into three equal payments, the last collected about two months after the deadline ([help](https://help.kickstarter.com/hc/en-us/articles/33730365935899)). In beta, projects that offered it saw average pledges 42% higher, and it made up 30% of their funding ([Kickstarter](https://start.kickstarter.com/pledge-over-time)). This matters for $2k+ tiers.
  - 2025 was the Design & Technology category's "biggest year ever" ([Kickstarter 2025 review](https://updates.kickstarter.com/a-year-in-review-2025-kickstarter-highlights/)).
  - Historically, only about one in five technology campaigns hit their goal ([The Hustle](https://thehustle.co/crowdfunding-success-rate), [Sound & Vision](https://www.soundandvision.com/content/kickstarter-flops-4-out-5-tech-campaigns-fail)).
- **Indiegogo**
  - Gamefound bought Indiegogo in 2025 ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/gamefound-buys-crowdfunding-pioneer-indiegogo/amp/)).
  - Since the platform upgrade on 16 Oct 2025, fees are a flat 5% plus 3% and $0.20 processing.
  - **Flexible Funding is no longer offered** to new campaigns, and InDemand has become "Late Pledge" ([Indiegogo help](https://help.indiegogo.com/article/645-what-changed-with-the-platform-upgrade)).
- **Kickstarter Culture Fund**
  - A $1M pool that pledges at least $1,000 to selected projects.
  - Only Art, Photography, Dance, Theater, Film, Fashion and Music projects are eligible, and campaigns must launch by 31 Dec 2026 ([Kickstarter](https://landing.kickstarter.com/culture-fund)).
  - EARF's products are not eligible.

**Pattern (est.):** at $2k to $10k, an unknown brand's campaign is a few dozen to a few hundred backers. That is enough: 50 backers at $5,000 is $250k.

- What matters is a small list of very qualified leads, not reach.

**Typical pitfalls (with evidence)**

- **Non-delivery is real.** Kickstarter's commissioned Wharton study found about 9% of funded projects fail to deliver (range 5 to 14%). Technology was among the most likely categories to fail ([Penn repository](https://repository.upenn.edu/mgmt_papers/210), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2015/12/10/9-percent-kickstarter-projects-fail-to-deliver.html)).
- **Under-capitalised manufacturing.** Ossic raised $2,708,472 from 10,263 Kickstarter backers, built only about 250 headphones, and shut down without refunds ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1642737), [Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/?p=167774), [UploadVR](https://www.uploadvr.com/vr-headphone-company-ossic-shuts/)).
- **Legal exposure.** The FTC's first crowdfunding case (2015) targeted a creator who misrepresented how funds would be used and failed to honour refunds ([FTC](https://ftc.gov/news-events/press-releases/2015/06/crowdfunding-project-creator-settles-ftc-charges-deception)).
- **Late shipping is enforced in California.** Yeezy paid about $950k under the state's shipping law ([K&L Gates](https://www.klgates.com/Not-So-Free-Shipping-Yeezy-Brand-to-Pay-US950000-Over-Late-Shipping-Under-California-Consumer-Protection-Laws-11-11-2021)).
- **Fees and payments.** Budget about 8 to 10% of pledges for platform and processing fees (est., from the official 5% plus 3 to 5% processing). Self-hosting "rewards" on Stripe is risky: Stripe lists "businesses offering a reward in return for donation" as restricted ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses)).
- **No more Flexible Funding.** Indiegogo's all-or-nothing switch removed the "keep what you raise" fallback ([Indiegogo](https://help.indiegogo.com/article/645-what-changed-with-the-platform-upgrade)).

### 4.4 Pre-sales and deposits for a made-to-order founders' edition: the rules

**FTC Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule** ([FTC business guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/business-guide-ftcs-mail-internet-or-telephone-order-merchandise-rule))

- **Scope.** It covers "most goods a customer orders" online, by mail or by phone, including prepaid sales. The guide does not exempt custom or made-to-order goods. Its exemptions are things like magazine subscriptions after the first issue, seeds and plants, cash-on-delivery orders and negative-option plans.
- **Shipping claims.** You must have a reasonable basis for any shipping date you state. If you state none, the default is 30 days from a properly completed order.
- **Delays.** You must offer the buyer a choice to agree to the delay or cancel for a prompt refund.
  - If the new date is 30 days late or less, silence counts as consent, but only if you told the customer that.
  - Silence never counts as consent to later delays.
  - Indefinite delays need the buyer's express consent.
- **Refunds.** Within seven working days for most payment methods, including third-party credit cards. No store credit or vouchers.
- **"Dry-testing."** For products that are not yet available, you must not suggest they will ship, must disclose that the product is only planned, and must offer cancellation with a refund. This is the template for **refundable reservations before prototypes exist**.
- **Penalties.** Up to **$53,088 per violation**, plus consumer redress.

**California**

- **B&P §17538 (internet and mail sellers)** ([text](https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_17538)):
  - Allow 30 days "unless otherwise conspicuously stated", then ship, refund, send a delay notice or substitute goods.
  - **Before accepting payment**, disclose your return and refund policy, legal business name and street address.
  - A violation is a misdemeanour.
- **Civil Code §1723 (refund-policy posting).** The duty to display a refund policy does not apply to "customized goods received as ordered" ([text](https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_1723)). This does not override the FTC delay and refund duties above.
- **Civil Code §1671(c) to (d) (penalty clauses).** In consumer retail purchases, a clause that fixes damages in advance is void unless actual damages would be "impracticable or extremely difficult to fix" ([text](https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_1671)). **Do not rely on "non-refundable deposits".** Have counsel draft cancellation terms that track actual, documented costs once production starts.
- **Registration.** File the FBN within 40 days of trading under the name ([§17910](https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_17910)). Register with SF within 15 days of starting business ([SF Treasurer](https://sftreasurer.org/business/register-business)). A seller's permit and the timing of sales tax on deposits are not verified here (section 9).

**Recommended founders'-edition structure (est.; not legal advice)**

1. **Stage 1, now: refundable reservation.**
   - Takes a small amount, for example $100 to $250 for lamps and $1,000 for Tropos.
   - Labelled "planned product; no ship date yet; refundable any time".
   - Follows the FTC dry-testing conditions.
2. **Stage 2, after a working prototype: order with deposit.**
   - A 30 to 50% deposit.
   - A conspicuous, reasonably based delivery window, for example "made to order in your colour; ships in 16 to 20 weeks".
   - Disclosures under B&P §17538(d).
   - FTC-compliant delay notices if the window slips.
   - Refundable until production starts; after that, cancellation terms tied to actual costs.
3. **Stage 3: balance due before shipping.**
4. **Hold deposits in a separate account.** Spend them only on that customer's order.
