**Products:**

## 0. Bottom line

1. **Editions and commissions, not equity [E].** I found no 2024–2026 case of an audio or lighting company investing in a pre-revenue design start-up [U]. I found about 20 partner editions, mostly 5–1,000 units; wherever the builder was named, it was the established maker (section A).
2. **The template is Klipsch × OJAS.** Klipsch builds in Hope, Arkansas; OJAS sells direct in the US and Klipsch dealers sell abroad. Runs went from 100 pairs ([ecoustics](https://www.ecoustics.com/products/klipsch-ojas-ko-r1/)) to 600 ([Klipsch](https://www.klipsch.com/news/vienna-showcase-klipsch-and-ojas-world-tour-continues/)). No split has been published [U].
3. **Top three:** Klipsch (a numbered horn edition); Ace Hotel Group (a commissioned listening corner plus an Ace edition of Core, via business@acehotel.com); Pro-Ject (a licensed tinted-acrylic deck; it already makes editions "under license").
4. **Speed versus size [E].** Hotel commissions and retail exclusives (Ace, MoMA) bring deposits or orders soonest. Maker-built editions are worth the most but take about 12–18 months.
5. **Lighting doors.** Flos and Artemide (web forms), Audo (designproposals@audocph.com) and probably &Tradition [U] take outside designs. HAY and Muuto do not (MillerKnoll policy), apart from Muuto's contest, which closed on 12 Oct 2026.
6. **Typical terms (ranges, not promises).** Royalties of 2–5% of factory price for furniture and lighting, or 3–7% of wholesale for consumer products (5% common). Advances small or none; minimum guarantees common in brand licences; co-brand licences 1–3 years.
7. **Timing.** Pitch nobody before 19 Jan. Send letters late Jan–Feb 2027 with real photos, then meet at Milan Design Week and Euroluce (Salone 13–18 Apr 2027) and HIGH END Vienna (6–9 May 2027).
8. **Keep Tropos, Mantle and the tint system EARF's own [E].** License or co-brand around them: partner-built editions, colourways and room commissions.
9. **California caveat.** A licensor cannot set its licensee's resale prices; use a suggested retail price and channel limits ([Lieberstein & Kumar](https://ktslaw.com/-/media/files/articles/2016/liebersteinkumararticle.pdf)).
10. **Main gaps.** No co-branded audio edition has published its split; Edition, 1 Hotels and Hoxton showed no public listening programme; about 30 pages were blocked or missing (section G).

**What the pattern says:**
- **Who builds:** the established maker, in every case found.
  - Klipsch builds in Hope.
  - Denon "built" the cartridge Turnbull "styled".
  - Pro-Ject manufactures "under license".
  - Linn, Wrensilva, Technics and B&O build their own editions.
- **What the partner brings:** design, colour or styling (Turnbull, Conran, Fujiwara, Fritz Hansen), or an audience or IP (Metallica, Harley-Davidson, Sun Records).
- **Who sells:** sales are split by channel.
  - The designer sells direct at home and the maker's dealers sell abroad (kO-R2).
  - Partner stores carry the edition (Fritz Hansen partner stores, the Roland-Garros Megastore).
  - Some editions go to a retail exclusive (B&O × Lynch at MoMA).
- **Run sizes** are mostly 10–600; mass collaborations run into thousands.
- **Premiums:** colourway editions cost about $500 or 6% more (McIntosh, Devialet Opéra). Material or limited pieces command multiples, e.g. Flos Arco K at 3.1× the standard Arco [R4].
- **Money:** nobody published a royalty or split [U]. The Linn × LoveFrom report of a pro bono designer shows that some top designers trade fees for profile.

### B2. Typical royalties for licensed furniture and lighting designs

These are ranges, not promises.

| Figure | Who says it | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Royalty-only contracts at **2–5% of the factory price**, about a quarter of retail; "typically offer no up-front payment"; Moooi: "we don't say that we will pay a minimum amount" | ICON magazine, Kieran Long (2006), quoting Pearson Lloyd and Moooi | [ICON](https://www.iconeye.com/?p=2517) |
| **3–5% of the factory gate price**; a €500 chair earns the designer about €11. Designers should ask for development payments | Michael Geldmacher (Neuland), smow (2015) | [smow](https://www.smow.com/blog/2015/04/michael-geldmacher-i-would-always-encourage-any-designer-to-at-least-attempt-to-get-paid-for-the-development-of-a-project/) |
| Industry norm "five per cent or less". Barber & Osgerby: "Sometimes we get equity, sometimes we get fees, sometimes we get a royalty" | Dezeen (2016) | [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2016/02/15/barber-osgerby-royalty-fees-furniture-product-design-stockholm-fair/) |
| **2–3% low, 7–10% high**. "A good royalty rate might be 5% of wholesale cost (around 2% of retail price)". Advances are usually small and deducted from royalties. Expect 3–4 years of strong royalties per product, and 1.5–2 years before the first cheque | Bruce M. Tharp, Core77 (2012) | [Core77](https://www.core77.com/posts/23366/product-licensing-101-so-lets-talk-money-23366) |
| An up-front payment offset against royalties, or a lump sum; an exclusive licence earns more; rights revert when the licence ends | ACID (UK design-IP body) | [ACID](https://www.acid.uk.com/?p=15787) |

**Translated for EARF [E]:**
- On a Core-like lamp licensed at $750 retail, the wholesale is about $350–375.
- A 3–5% royalty then yields about **$10–19 per lamp**.
- 1,000 lamps a year would earn **$10k–19k a year**.
- This is useful as validation and a small advance. It is not the way to fund Tropos.

## D. Deal structures: typical ranges, not promises

### D1. Licensing (EARF licenses a design to a maker)

| Term | Typical range | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Royalty, furniture and lighting | **2–5% of factory or ex-works price**; about 3–5% of wholesale; norm "five per cent or less" | [ICON](https://www.iconeye.com/?p=2517), [smow](https://www.smow.com/blog/2015/04/michael-geldmacher-i-would-always-encourage-any-designer-to-at-least-attempt-to-get-paid-for-the-development-of-a-project/), [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2016/02/15/barber-osgerby-royalty-fees-furniture-product-design-stockholm-fair/) |
| Royalty, consumer products generally | **3–7% of wholesale, 5% common.** Tharp licensed his own products at 3–7% and calls 5% of wholesale "a good royalty rate"; his rule of thumb runs from 2–3% (low) to 7–10% (high). Stephen Key uses "the standard 5% royalty rate", on wholesale | [Core77](https://www.core77.com/posts/23366/product-licensing-101-so-lets-talk-money-23366), [inventRight](https://inventright.com/innovate-this/how-to-negotiate-minimum-guarantees) |
| Royalty, brand or creator co-brands | **5–10% of net**, depending on category and leverage | [Make Influence](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/co-branded-product-collaborations-royalty-deals-explained) |
| Royalty base | Wholesale or "net sales". Define net precisely; some makers' "net" is close to their manufacturing cost | [Core77](https://www.core77.com/posts/23366/product-licensing-101-so-lets-talk-money-23366) |
| Advance | Furniture and lighting: often **none**. Otherwise **small and recoupable** against royalties, or a one-off lump sum. Ask for a development fee | [ICON](https://www.iconeye.com/?p=2517), [Core77](https://www.core77.com/posts/23366/product-licensing-101-so-lets-talk-money-23366), [ACID](https://www.acid.uk.com/?p=15787), [smow](https://www.smow.com/blog/2015/04/michael-geldmacher-i-would-always-encourage-any-designer-to-at-least-attempt-to-get-paid-for-the-development-of-a-project/) |
| Minimum guarantee | **Common in brand licences.** Licensors may terminate if it is missed, and the licensee may owe the shortfall. Key's heuristic: year 1 at about 25% of projected first-year revenue from the licence (he also writes "projected sales", so the base is ambiguous), +50% in year 2; minimum guarantees typically start about 18 months after signing | [Lieberstein & Kumar](https://ktslaw.com/-/media/files/articles/2016/liebersteinkumararticle.pdf), [inventRight](https://inventright.com/innovate-this/how-to-negotiate-minimum-guarantees) |
| Term | Co-brand licences **1–3 years** with renewal; sell-off 30–180 days after expiry | [Make Influence](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/co-branded-product-collaborations-royalty-deals-explained) |
| End of licence | Rights revert at the end. Add a clause that triggers reversion if production stops for N months [E] | [ACID](https://www.acid.uk.com/?p=15787) |
| Payments | Usually quarterly, about 3 months in arrears | [Core77](https://www.core77.com/posts/23366/product-licensing-101-so-lets-talk-money-23366) |

### D2. Co-branded limited editions (who builds, who sells, the split)

- **Who builds:** the established maker handles factory, quality control, warranty and certification. Examples: Klipsch in Hope; Denon "built" the cartridge Turnbull "styled"; Pro-Ject "under license"; Technics (sources in section A).
- **Who sells:** two common patterns.
  - The designer sells direct in its home market and the maker's dealers sell elsewhere (kO-R2: "sold direct from Ojas" in the US; authorised retailers in Europe).
  - Each partner sells through its own stores (Technics plus Fritz Hansen partner stores; Devialet plus the Roland-Garros Megastore).
- **The split:** not published for any audio edition found [U]. Three structures are plausible [E]:
  - **(a)** The designer buys finished units at a transfer price and keeps the retail margin. The kO-R2's direct sales by OJAS fit this, but it is an inference.
  - **(b)** The maker pays the designer a design fee plus a royalty on its own channel's sales (ranges in D1).
  - **(c)** A flat fee only, or pro bono for profile (Linn × LoveFrom).
- **Edition sizes in this niche:** 25–800, mostly 100–600 for $5k–$20k objects (section A).

### D3. Commissions (hotels, members' clubs, showrooms) [E]

- **Typical structure:**
  - Deposit on order (often 50%), balance on delivery or installation.
  - EARF keeps the design IP.
  - Credit line or naming, e.g. "The Listening Room by OJAS" at Patina.
  - Optional service contract.
- **Retail exclusives:**
  - Wholesale is about 40–50% of retail; Tharp cites wholesale at about 40% of retail with large retailers ([Core77](https://www.core77.com/posts/23366/product-licensing-101-so-lets-talk-money-23366)).
  - Galleries usually work on about 50% consignment [E].

### D4. Strategic investment

- I found **no 2024–2026 example** of an audio or lighting company investing in a pre-revenue design start-up [U].
- Older structures include a convertible preferred stake with an option to buy (Polk and Genesis, late 1990s; the Stereophile page returned 403) [U].
- Designers sometimes take **equity instead of fees** (Barber & Osgerby, [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2016/02/15/barber-osgerby-royalty-fees-furniture-product-design-stockholm-fair/)).
- If a partner offers equity [E]:
  - Prefer a SAFE.
  - Refuse a right of first refusal (ROFR) on the whole company, or blanket exclusivity, which would block other partners.

### D5. Clauses EARF should ask for [E, except where linked]

1. Royalty base defined as net wholesale, with listed deductions only.
2. A development fee or advance, recoupable.
3. A minimum guarantee from year 2.
4. Reversion if not launched within 12–18 months, or if production stops.
5. Approval of colour, material and finish, so EARF controls its tints.
6. Name-use and credit clause (ACID's template flags this).
7. Channel restrictions and a published suggested retail price. **Do not set resale prices**: vertical price restraints "may still be considered per se illegal" in California and New York ([Lieberstein & Kumar](https://ktslaw.com/-/media/files/articles/2016/liebersteinkumararticle.pdf)).
8. Audit rights and quarterly statements.
9. A limited territory and term for any exclusivity.
