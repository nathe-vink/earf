# R11 — EARF: what can still be protected after the October 2026 web disclosure

*Research memo, 31 Oct 2026. This is research, not legal advice.*
*Tags: **[E]** = estimate · **[U]** = could not verify live (search snippet only, or page blocked) · **[Atty]** = an attorney should confirm before anyone relies on it.*

## Bottom line

1. **Almost everything is still protectable in the US, EU, UK, Japan and Korea**, because each has a 12-month grace period for the designer's own disclosure. They share one **hard stop: Fri 15 Oct 2027**. That is the last business day before the first anniversary of the 17 Oct 2026 Tropos posting, which falls on Sun 17 Oct 2027.
2. **In China and India, protection for the designs already shown is gone.** Both require novelty worldwide, and neither has an exception that covers a brand's own website. **Taiwan (6 months) and Brazil (180 days) close around 15 Apr 2027** [U].
3. **Before the 19 Jan 2027 debut, file these** (target the week of 7 Dec 2026, last safe day Fri 15 Jan 2027):
   - 5 US design applications, one per product, drawn as formal line drawings from the CAD rather than the marketing renders;
   - 1 EU multiple-design application (€850 for 5 designs, €1,475 for 10);
   - 1 UK application (£85 for up to 10 designs).

   About **$13k–$28k all-in [E]**.
4. **The US "rocket docket" is gone** (final rule effective 14 Aug 2025). First office actions now come about 14 months after filing, so grants are likely in about 2028 [E]. For the debut and for investors, "patent pending" is what counts.
5. **Before the Feb 2027 licensing talks, commission a quick patentability screen of the horn construction.** File a US provisional ($130 small entity plus $2k–6k attorney) only if something non-obvious and *not visible* survives the prior art. That prior art is strong:
   - Tannoy US 8,213,658 claims a horn whose block is made of layers joined together;
   - Flare Audio US 9,398,356 describes a horn built from stacked plates;
   - Moser US 5,661,271 forms speaker chambers from stacked layers.

   The cordless lamp's "cell switching" looks weak.
6. **Don't count on the EU unregistered design.**
   - German case law holds that a first disclosure outside the EU can stop it arising at all.
   - The EU grace period protects only registered designs.
   - In the Crocs case, a website that search engines would not turn up still destroyed novelty, so "noindex" protects nothing.
7. **Copyright: register the published renders by Fri 15 Jan 2027**, three months after publication, to keep statutory damages (GR2D group: $85 for up to 20 works, if eligible [Atty]).
   - In the US, copyright gives the product shapes themselves little protection.
   - In the EU, copyright in applied art is real (Mio/konektra, CJEU, 4 Dec 2025).
8. **Trade dress for the tints and the overall look requires "secondary meaning"** (the public linking the look to EARF). Build it through sales and marketing; it is not available at launch.
9. **Japan and Korea can wait for one Hague filing**: ideally within 6 months of the US filing, claiming its priority, and in any case before mid-Sep 2027. WIPO fees are about **3,800 CHF for 5 designs**, plus attorney fees.
10. **Seed investors will check four things**: chain of title first (founder and contractor IP assigned in writing to the new company), then a filing schedule with the Oct 2027 plan, evidence of the disclosure dates, and NDA/confidentiality hygiene with fabricators and partners.

## A. US design patents

### A1. Grace period and deadline

- **The rule.** Under [35 USC 102(a)(1) and (b)(1)(A)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/35/102), a disclosure made **one year or less** before the effective filing date is not prior art if it was made by the inventor, or by someone who got the subject matter from the inventor.
- **The deadline.** The Tropos posting was on Sat 17 Oct 2026, so the anniversary is Sun 17 Oct 2027. **File no later than Fri 15 Oct 2027**, and much earlier in practice.
  - [Atty] Whether a weekend anniversary rolls over to Monday 18 Oct is not worth testing.
- **Why to file before the debut rather than at the deadline.** The second exception, 102(b)(1)(B), only shields EARF from *intervening* third-party disclosures of the same subject matter. [MPEP 2153.02](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2153.html) says it does not apply even where the only differences are "insubstantial changes, or only trivial or obvious variations." So copies or near-variants that appear after the debut could become prior art against a late filing.
- **About "noindex".** In *Acceleration Bay v. Activision* (Fed. Cir. 2018), a report that was not meaningfully indexed was held not "publicly accessible" ([summary](https://patentdocs.org/2018/11/21/acceleration-bay-llc-v-activision-blizzard-inc-fed-cir-2018/)) [U]. **Do not rely on this.** The renders were openly reachable, and EU law treats the same facts the other way (B2).

Design patents carry no maintenance fees ([IP Boutique Law](https://ipboutiquelaw.com/how-much-design-patent-cost-2026-usa-dc/)).

**Entity status traps [Atty]:**
- **Micro entity eligibility.** The gross-income limit is **$262,380** (effective 15 Sep 2026). Every inventor and every party with an ownership interest must qualify ([USPTO micro entity page](https://www.uspto.gov/patents/laws/micro-entity-status)).
- **Micro entity quota.** The gross-income route covers only an inventor's **first five** non-provisional applications, and design applications count toward the five. Filing 5 designs would use up each designer's quota, so later filings (utility, partial designs) would pay small-entity rates.
- **USPTO scrutiny.** The USPTO has publicly flagged erroneous micro-entity certifications ([OG notice](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/com/sol/og/2025/week52/TOCCN/item-190.htm)). **Default to small entity** unless micro status is clearly met.
- **Licensing can end small-entity status.** Small-entity status is lost once EARF licenses, or is *obligated* to license, rights to a company that is not small (500 or more employees). EARF must then notify the USPTO before paying the next fee ([37 CFR 1.27(a)(2), (g)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/1.27)). This is directly relevant to the Feb 2027 talks with audio companies.

### A4. Pendency, and whether to pay for speed

- **Pendency now.**
  - First-action pendency fell from **16.9 to 14 months**, the lowest since FY2018 ([USPTO alert, 19 May 2026](https://www.uspto.gov/subscription-center/2026/strong-progress-continues-design-patent-examination)).
  - 62,166 design applications were awaiting a first action.
  - Total pendency was 23.0 months in FY2024 ([USPTO Design Day 2024](https://uspto.gov/sites/default/files/documents/DesignDay2024.pdf); the file was too large to open, so this comes from a search snippet [U]). It is probably about 17–20 months now [E].
  - **Expect grants around mid-2028 for December 2026 filings [E].**
- **The "rocket docket" is gone.**
  - It was suspended on 17 Apr 2025.
  - A final rule **eliminated expedited examination of design applications effective 14 Aug 2025** ([USPTO](https://www.uspto.gov/about-us/news-updates/uspto-issues-final-rule-eliminate-expedited-examination-design-applications)).
- **What remains.**
  - **Accelerated Examination**: a petition to make special, with a pre-examination search and an examination support document ([Patently-O](https://patentlyo.com/patent/2025/06/discontinues-accelerated-applications.html)). The fee and the attorney effort needed were not verified [U].
  - Free petitions are available only on age or health grounds.
- **Verdict: not worth it now.** US design applications are not published before grant ([35 USC 122(b)(2)(A)(iv)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/35/122)), and "patent pending" serves the debut and investors. Revisit Accelerated Examination only if a US copy appears and EARF needs an enforceable patent quickly [E].

### A5. How many designs to file

- **Each application covers one design.** A US design application may contain **one claim only**. Independent and distinct designs need separate applications. Several embodiments can share an application only if their "appearance and shape are similar" ([USPTO design guide](https://www.uspto.gov/patents/basics/apply/design-patent)).
- **The cost of mixing designs.** Otherwise the examiner forces EARF to elect one design, and the others must go into new, fully priced applications ([IP Boutique Law](https://ipboutiquelaw.com/how-much-design-patent-cost-2026-usa-dc/)).
- **Minimum:** **5 applications, one per product**, each claiming the whole product in solid lines.
- **If budget allows:** 1–3 **partial-design** applications for signature features, with the rest of the product shown in broken lines:
  - Tropos: the layered horn body alone;
  - Mantle: the plinth or tonearm;
  - Core: the ball-to-base junction.

  Partial claims catch copiers who change everything else. About $1.8k–$3.3k each [E].
- **Tints need no separate US filings.** Black-and-white drawings cover every colour. If colour drawings are filed, "color will be considered an integral part of the disclosed and claimed design" ([USPTO guide](https://www.uspto.gov/patents/basics/apply/design-patent)), which narrows the claim.
- **Prior art.** Hemisphere and Core are simple geometric forms in crowded lamp prior art, so expect narrower scope. Partial claims help [Atty].

### A6. Can the renders serve as the drawings? Mostly not as they are

What the USPTO requires ([USPTO guide](https://www.uspto.gov/patents/basics/apply/design-patent), last updated 21 Apr 2025; [MPEP 1503.02](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s1503.html)):

- **Enough views** to disclose the complete appearance. New matter cannot be added later, so file a full set: a perspective view plus 6 orthographic views [E].
- **No environment or background.** Photographs are accepted only if they are "the only practicable medium."
- **No mixing.** Photographs and ink drawings must not be used for the same embodiment.
- **Transparent and translucent surfaces must be shown with oblique line shading.** "Elements visible behind transparent surfaces should be shown in light, full lines, not broken lines." With through-tinted acrylic, any visible drivers, LEDs or cells therefore become part of what is drawn. **[Atty]** Decide which visible internal parts appear in the claim and how the tint is depicted.
- **Colour is now allowed without a petition**, but it is then claimed.

**Conclusion:** photorealistic marketing renders (backgrounds, glow, tint, reflections) are likely to draw objections or narrow the claim. Have a draftsperson produce formal views from the CAD (**$300–$800 per design**, [IP Boutique Law](https://ipboutiquelaw.com/how-much-design-patent-cost-2026-usa-dc/)). Use the **same view set** in every filing, so that later Japan and Korea filings can claim the US priority cleanly [E].

### A7. Other US mechanics

- **No provisional applications for designs.** Design priority is **6 months** ([35 USC 172](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/35/172)).
- **Term:** 15 years from grant ([35 USC 173](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/35/173)).
- **Who can file:**
  - A **company** applicant *must* be represented by a registered patent practitioner ([37 CFR 1.31](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/1.31)).
  - If the founders file in their own names before the company is incorporated, they should later assign in writing and record the assignment within 3 months ([35 USC 261](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/35/261)).
- **Inventors:** any contractor who contributed to the ornamental design is a co-inventor and must assign [Atty].

## B. EU and UK

### B1. Registered EU design (the former Registered Community Design)

- **The legal basis.**
  - Phase I of the reform applied from 1 May 2025 (Reg. 2024/2822).
  - Since **1 July 2026** the law is consolidated in **Regulation (EU) 2026/715**, a codification "without any change in their substance" ([European Parliament](https://data.europarl.europa.eu/distribution/doc/TA-10-2026-0032_en.pdf); text on [WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/24142), [ELI](http://data.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/715/oj)).
  - Registered and unregistered Community designs are now called **registered EU designs** and **unregistered EU designs**. Applications can be filed **only at the EUIPO** ([Mewburn Ellis](https://www.mewburn.com/forward/eu-design-changes)).
- **Grace period.** **12 months** (Art 8(2)).
  - It covers disclosures by the designer or a successor in title, and by a third person "as a result of information provided or action taken by" them.
  - It is counted back from the filing date, or from the **priority date** if one is claimed.
  - So a later EU filing that claims the US date counts back from the US date.
- **Fees (Annex I of Reg. 2026/715):**

| Item | Fee |
|---|---|
| Application, first design | €350 (one fee covering registration and publication) |
| Each additional design in a multiple application (Art 44(1): maximum 50 designs) | €125 |
| Deferment of publication (up to 30 months, Art 62) | €40, plus €20 per additional design |
| Renewals (5-year terms, maximum 25 years) | €150 / €250 / €400 / €700 |
| **Example: 5 designs** | **€850** |
| **Example: 10 designs** | **€1,475** |

  - The old rule that every design in a multiple application must share a product class is **abolished** ([Mewburn](https://www.mewburn.com/forward/eu-design-changes); fees confirmed by [Boult Wade Tennant](https://boult.com/bulletin/eu-design-reform-new-design-fees-and-changes-to-renewal-dates)).
  - Deferment is useless for designs that are already public. It is useful for **unreleased variants**.
- **Representations (from 1 July 2026).**
  - Up to **10 static views** per design, *or* one 3D file (OBJ or STL), *or* one MP4 animation. Each design uses one type only ([Dreyfus](https://www.dreyfus.fr/en/2026/08/20/eu-designs-what-has-changed-since-july-1st-2026-phase-2/)).
  - Clean renders on a neutral background can serve as static views.
  - Commentators warn that the new 3D and animation formats may not support priority claims in other countries [U].
  - "Design" expressly includes **colours and materials** (Art 4(1)). **[Atty]** Consider filing neutral versions for breadth, plus one or two tinted versions if the tints are core identity.
- **Speed and examination.**
  - There is **no novelty examination**. The office checks only the definition of a design, public policy and emblems (Art 56).
  - Fast-track registrations take **about 2 working days**, regular ones up to 10 working days ([EUIPO presentation, Sep 2024](https://internationalipcooperation.eu/sites/default/files/proj-docs/2024/Presentation%20-%20Design%20Protection%20in%20the%20European%20Union%20-%20Monika%20Tomczynska.pdf)).
- **Who files.** A non-EEA applicant may file alone, but **must use an EU professional representative in all other proceedings** (Art 115(2)). In practice, budget €500–€1,500 for a representative [E].
- **Useful extras.**
  - The right covers **creating or sharing CAD and 3D-print files** that let others make the product (Art 20(2)(d)), and goods passing through the EU in transit (Art 20(3)).
  - The registration symbol is a **D in a circle** (Art 29).

### B2. Unregistered EU design: 3 years, and does the US website count?

- **The rule.**
  - Art 12(1): protection lasts 3 years "from the date on which the design was first made available to the public within the Union."
  - Art 12(2): a design counts as made available within the Union if the disclosure could reasonably have become known to the specialist circles in the sector **operating within the Union**. Confidential disclosures do not count.
  - It protects **only against copying** (Art 20(4)).
- **Does a US web disclosure count? The answer is unsettled, and both readings hurt EARF:**
  - **Crocs (General Court, T-651/16).** Crocs' own US website, a Florida boat show and US sales destroyed the novelty of its EU *registered* design. Crocs argued its site did not appear in generic search results ("shoe", "clog"). That failed: the burden was on Crocs to show EU specialists could not reasonably have known ([Legal500 summary](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/european-union/intellectual-property/crocs-put-foot-in-it-with-prior-disclosure)). **So "noindex" does not prevent a disclosure.**
  - **German Federal Court, *Gebäckpresse II*.** The first disclosure that creates an unregistered design must take place within the EU. An earlier non-EU disclosure that was knowable in the EU destroys novelty, so **no unregistered design arises at all**. The UK court reference that might have settled the point (*Squeezamals*) was withdrawn ([Kilburn & Strode](https://www.kilburnstrode.com/knowledge/brexit/teddy-v-pms); [CITMA](https://www.citma.org.uk/resources/relying-on-unregistered-community-design-protection.html)).
  - **The 12-month grace period (Art 8(2)) protects only a *registered* EU design.**
- **Conclusion.** At best EARF gets an uncertain 3-year right against copying only, and possibly nothing. **Register.** If EARF still wants the unregistered right, make the first EU-facing disclosure deliberate: EU trade press, or an EU fair such as Milan in April 2027 [E].

### B3. UK registered design

- **Grace period.** 12 months before the "relevant date" ([Registered Designs Act 1949 s.1B(6)(c)–(d)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Geo6/12-13-14/88/section/1B)). The relevant date includes a priority date (s.1B(7) with s.14(2)).
- **Fees from 1 Apr 2026 (online)** ([GOV.UK fee table](https://www.gov.uk/csv-preview/694140e158a21370f58f2f98/Registered-Designs-list-of-current-and-new-fees.csv)):

| Item | Fee |
|---|---|
| One design | £60 |
| Up to 10 designs | **£85** |
| Up to 20 designs | £110 |
| Up to 50 designs | £185 |
| Deferred design | £50 |
| Renewals | £85 / £110 / £135 / £170 |

- **Speed and term.** A decision usually comes **within 2 weeks**; registrations last 5 years, renewable up to 25 years ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/register-a-design)).
- **No full search and examination.** The 2025 consultation said the government is "not consulting on reintroducing full search and examination" ([consultation](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/consultation-on-changes-to-the-uk-designs-framework/consultation-on-changes-to-the-uk-designs-framework)).
- **Address for service.** UK filings need an address for service in the **UK, Gibraltar or the Channel Islands** ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/address-for-service-for-intellectual-property-rights)), so use a UK agent (£300–£800 [E]).
- **UK unregistered "supplementary" design.** Whether one can arise from a first disclosure outside the UK is uncertain. The consultation (published 4 Sep 2025) is closed and no government response has been published yet. Changes are expected in 2027 ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/uk-design-law-consultation-spotlight-series-section-g-post-brexit-disclosure)) [U]. **Register.**

## C. The Hague system (WIPO): one filing for the US, EU, UK, Japan and Korea?

### C1. Do the novelty clocks still allow it?

**Yes for all five, until Fri 15 Oct 2027. No for China.**

- **US:** 1-year grace (A1).
- **EU:** 12 months (B1).
- **UK:** 12 months (B3).
- **Japan:** 1 year from the first disclosure.
  - The exception must be **claimed at filing**.
  - The proving certificate goes in with the international application, or within 30 days of international publication (through a Japanese agent).
  - Since 2024, proof of the *first* disclosure covers later similar ones.
  - The filing date is not backdated, and publications by third parties are not covered ([AIPPI](https://www.aippi.org/news/recent-revisions-to-design-law/)). The JPO's own Hague page returned 403.
- **Korea:** 1 year from the applicant's first disclosure ([LESI design study, Kim & Chang](https://lesi.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/lesi-design-study-korea.pdf)). The USPTO notes that Japan and Korea aligned with the US 12-month period ([USPTO](https://www.uspto.gov/ip-policy/industrial-design-policy/grace-period-industrial-designs)).
- **[Atty]** Assume Japan and Korea count the one year to the **actual filing date** (the international registration date), not to a Paris priority date. This could not be verified. A formal defect can also postpone the international registration date [U]. **File by mid-Sep 2027 at the latest.**

### C3. Recommendation

- **Do not use the Hague system for the US, EU or UK.** File those directly before the debut. It is faster (EU registration in days, UK in about 2 weeks) and gives more control, and US small or micro rates apply either way.
- **Use a single Hague application for Japan and Korea**, plus optional Canada, Switzerland or others, **claiming the US priority** within 6 months of the US filing. The fallback deadline is mid-Sep 2027.
- **Do not designate China** for the designs already disclosed (section D).

## D. China, and other markets where novelty is already lost

| Market | Rule | Status for the disclosed designs |
|---|---|---|
| **China** | A design must not be a "prior design", meaning one known to the public at home or abroad (Art 23). The 6-month grace period covers only four events: national emergency, government-recognised international exhibitions, prescribed academic meetings, and disclosure without consent (Art 24) ([Patent Law 2020, WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/21027)). | **Lost** |
| **India** | Disclosure anywhere in the world bars registration (s.4(b)). The only exceptions are notified exhibitions (s.21, 6 months) and confidential disclosures (s.16). A 12-month grace period proposed in Jan 2026 is not yet law ([Intepat, Aug 2026](https://www.intepat.com/blog/novelty-and-originality-of-design)). | **Lost** (unless enacted in time [U]) |
| Taiwan | 6-month design grace period. An extension to 12 months is pending ([Lee and Li](https://www.leeandli.com/EN/NewslettersDetail/7329.htm), [TIPO](https://www1.tipo.gov.tw/en/cp-896-921969-e9551-2.html)) [U]. | Open until ~15 Apr 2027 |
| Brazil | 180 days, counted to the filing or priority date ([INPI](https://www.gov.br/inpi/en/services/industrial-designs/industrial-design-further-information)) [U]. | Open until ~15 Apr 2027, or counted from the US priority date |
| Australia | 12 months, in force since 10 Mar 2022 ([IP Australia](https://www.ipaustralia.gov.au/designs/applying-for-a-design/grace-period)). | Open until 15 Oct 2027 |
| Canada | 12 months, reportedly counted to the Canadian filing date ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-gb/insights/publications/2025/06/industrial-designs-ip-protection-in-canada)) [U]. | Open until 15 Oct 2027 |

### What copiers can do in China

- **Copy and sell freely.** EARF has no design right in China for these designs.
- **Register EARF's design as their own Chinese design patent.**
  - Chinese design applications get "only a preliminary examination", with no substantive examination ([WIPO Judicial Guide, China](https://www.wipo.int/patent-judicial-guide/en/full-guide/china/4.8.2)).
  - A copier could use such a patent for takedowns on Chinese platforms, or against EARF's own suppliers.
  - China Customs can intercept *exports* on the basis of recorded design patents. This requires shipping details and a bond, and patent-based seizures are rare (about 4.3% of all seizures) ([Rouse, 2023](https://rouse.com/insights/news/2023/china-customs-patent-protection-what-are-the-benefits-protection-they-offer/)). The risk is highest if the squatter sits in EARF's own supply chain [E].
- **EARF's remedy is an invalidation action**, anyone may file one, on the ground that the design was already public (Art 23) (WIPO Judicial Guide). It succeeds only with **good, dated evidence of the web disclosure**. A reported case in which Shu Uemura invalidated a copier's design patent using prior-publication evidence could not be opened (IAM page returned 403) [U].

### Practical protection instead

1. **Evidence pack now.** Collect timestamped or notarised captures of earf.world, hosting deploy logs, and the CAD revision history. These are usable in a Chinese invalidation action and against bad-faith filings elsewhere [E].
2. **File next designs first.** File new products and *significantly different* variants **before any disclosure**, including in China: a Hague designation of China costs 497 CHF per application ([WIPO](https://www.wipo.int/hague/en/fees/individ-fee.html)). New variants must still differ significantly from EARF's own published renders (Art 23) [Atty].
3. **Chinese copyright registration** with the Copyright Protection Center of China, for renders and 2D artwork (and possibly applied art).
   - Official fees are RMB 100–2,000, and it takes about 3 months.
   - Chinese e-commerce platforms that require proof of rights for takedowns readily accept it ([Hogan Lovells, Jan 2021](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/qa-why-you-should-consider-registering-your-copyright-in-china)).
4. **NNN agreements** (non-use, non-disclosure, non-circumvention) with Chinese fabricators, written to be enforceable in China ([GWBMA/RegistrationChina, 2024](https://www.registrationchina.com/articles/china-nnn-agreement)). Own the tooling, and split part production between suppliers [E].
5. **Speed, brand and trade dress.** Ship fast. Trademarks are handled elsewhere. Monitor CNIPA design publications and marketplaces [E].

## E. Utility patents

### E1. Deadlines

- **US.** Anything the renders disclosed must be claimed in a US filing (a provisional is enough) by **Fri 15 Oct 2027** under 102(b)(1).
  - Features that were *not* disclosed start their own clock when they first become public.
  - **Note:** the transparent acrylic may reveal the construction to anyone at the debut.
  - A *confidential* sale can still put the invention "on sale" ([Helsinn v. Teva, 2019](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/17-1229)).
- **Europe and China require absolute novelty.** The European Patent Convention excuses only evident abuse and officially recognised exhibitions (6 months) ([EPC Art 55](https://www.epo.org/en/legal/epc/2020/a55.html)). Anything visible in the renders is already lost there. Only undisclosed process or structural features remain patentable abroad, and only if filed before they are disclosed.

## F. Copyright, trade dress and confidential CAD files

### F1. The renders

- **Ownership.** Copyright arises automatically and belongs to the *author*. Renders or CAD made by freelancers need a **signed written assignment** ([17 USC 204(a)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/204)).
- **Registration and timing.**
  - A US work must be registered before EARF can sue ([17 USC 411(a)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/411)).
  - Statutory damages and attorney's fees require registration before the infringement, or **within 3 months of first publication** ([17 USC 412](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/412)).
  - If the website posting counts as "publication" [Atty], **register by Fri 15 Jan 2027**.
- **Cheapest route: GR2D group registration** ([Federal Register, 19 Dec 2025](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-12-19/html/2025-23402.htm); [Copyright Office](https://copyright.gov/registration/gr2d/)), in effect since 17 Feb 2026.
  - It covers 2–20 published 2D artworks for **$85**.
  - All works must be published in the same calendar year, all by the same author, and the author must be the claimant. Works made for hire are allowed.
  - **Technical drawings and works of applied art are excluded.** Whether product renders fit is [Atty].
  - Fallback: Standard application **$65** or Single **$45** per work ([fees](https://www.copyright.gov/about/fees.html)).

### F2. The products themselves

- **US.** Only artistic features that can be separated from the useful article are protected; the shape, cut and dimensions are not ([Star Athletica, 2017](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/15-866)).
  - Sculptural elements *can* be protected: statuettes used as lamp bases were held copyrightable ([Mazer v. Stein, 1954](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/347/201)).
  - Possible candidates: Mother EARF's surface artwork, or the contour-line pattern [Atty].
- **EU.** Applied art faces **no higher threshold** than other works. Infringement means the creative elements were recognisably copied, not that the "overall impression" is similar ([CJEU Mio/konektra, 4 Dec 2025, CMS summary](https://cms.law/en/nld/legal-updates/o-sole-mio-cjeu-s-tune-on-copyright-protection-of-works-of-applied-art)). This is potentially a strong extra layer in the EU [Atty].

### F3. The tint system

- **Copyright does not cover it.** "Mere variations of ... coloring" are not copyrightable ([37 CFR 202.1(a)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/202.1)).
- **As a trademark or trade dress,** a colour can work only once it identifies EARF as the source and is not functional ([Qualitex, 1995](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/93-1577.ZS.html)).
- **EU and UK registered designs can include colour** (Reg. 2026/715 Art 4(1)), so file a tinted version as an extra design.
- **Custom tint recipes** can be kept as trade secrets. Tint *names* are trademarks, which are covered elsewhere.

### F4. Trade dress for the overall look

- **Product design is never inherently distinctive.** It always needs secondary meaning ([Wal-Mart v. Samara, 2000](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/99-150.ZS.html)).
- **So it is not available at the debut.** Build it [E]:
  - consistent presentation;
  - "look-for" advertising that points at the tinted layers;
  - a file of evidence: press coverage, sales, unsolicited mentions.

### F5. CAD files shared with fabricators

- **Trade-secret status requires "reasonable measures to keep such information secret"** ([18 USC 1839(3)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1839)).
- **Minimum practice [E]:**
  - An NDA signed **before** sharing. It should cover non-use, no reverse engineering, IP and tooling ownership, return or destruction of files, and no subcontracting without consent.
  - Every drawing and file marked "CONFIDENTIAL – Property of EARF – do not copy or distribute", in both title blocks and file names.
  - Share only part-level STEP files: no native CAD files and no full assemblies.
  - An access-controlled portal and a log of who received what.
  - For China: an NNN agreement (D, item 4).
- **Legal effect.** Under EU law, a disclosure under explicit or implicit confidentiality is not "made available to the public" (Reg. 2026/715 Arts 8(1), 12(2)). That keeps unreleased variants novel. A registered EU design also lets EARF stop people sharing CAD or 3D files that enable manufacture (Art 20(2)(d)).

### F6. Licensing talks

- **Many audio companies will not keep unsolicited material confidential.** For example, Marshall states it has "no obligation to keep the Unsolicited Submission confidential" ([Marshall](https://www.marshall.com/policies/unsolicited-submissions)). Harman's policy page returned 503.
- **Rule: file first, then show.** Use a mutual NDA where the other side will sign one. Otherwise show only material that is already public or filed [E].
