# EARF: market, pricing and channel research (R4)

Prepared 31 October 2026 for the EARF investor deck. Public web only: no forms, accounts or contact with anyone.

**How to read this report**

- Prices are as listed on the linked page when we checked it in October 2026, unless a date is given. Currencies are as listed and we did not convert them. "Each" and "pair" (or B&O's "set") are stated wherever the source states them.
- **[V]** means we opened the page and checked the figure ourselves.
- **[X]** means the figure comes from a search-engine excerpt only, because the page blocked our fetcher or returned 404. Treat these as lower confidence and re-check before they go in the deck.
- **[E]** marks an estimate, an assumption or our own arithmetic. Every EARF price recommendation is an [E].
- The shared web-search budget for this session ran out near the end of the research. Some gaps remain, and they are listed in section 6.

**Three strongest market facts**

1. US vinyl revenue reached **$1.04 billion (wholesale) in 2025**, up 9.3%, on 46.8 million units. It was the **19th straight year of vinyl growth**, and the US accounts for nearly half of global vinyl revenue. In the first half of 2026, vinyl rose another 17.7% to $544 million ([RIAA 2025](https://www.riaa.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/RIAA-Year-End-Revenue-2025.pdf), [RIAA H1 2026](https://www.riaa.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2026-Mid-Year-Music-Industry-Revenue-Report.pdf)) [V].

The Klipsch × OJAS **kO-R1 (100 pairs, $8,498) is sold out**, and the **kO-R2 is a 600-pair numbered run at $11,995** ([OJAS store](https://checkout.ojas.nyc/collections/frontpage), [Gear Patrol](https://www.gearpatrol.com/audio/klipsch-ojas-k0-r2-loudspeaker-price-release-date/)) [V]. **What this means for EARF [E]**

- **No one else sells horns in cast, through-tinted acrylic.** The nearest drivers-and-horn peers are passive and cost $11,750–$23,000 a pair: Volti Lucera, kO-R2 and JBL 4367/4369. Powered, design-led speakers run from $21,200 (Beolab 28) to $211,800 (Beolab 90).
- **Acrylic turntables already span $2,999 to €13,950.** That runs from the Pro-Ject 6 PerspeX, whose floating sub-chassis is close to Mantle's concept, up to the Transrotor Rondino nero.
- **Transparent and crystal versions command large premiums over the standard model.** Flos Arco K is 3.1× the standard Arco. Louis Poulsen's PH Artichoke Glass starts at $62,300.
- **Production lighting tends to resell below new retail.** The one example we have is Adelman's auction results, which sit below the new price; those figures are [X]. Gallery and auction premiums attach to **authorship, scarcity and age**, as with Kuramata's 56 chairs, rather than to the material alone. EARF's collectible tier therefore needs real edition limits.

## 2. Recommended retail prices and margin logic

### 2.1 Price architecture [E]

1. **Production (open edition, eight colours).** Sold direct (web and Planet EARF), and later through a small number of dealers and design retailers.
2. **Founders' Edition (first numbered run).** Priced at about 1.3–1.5× the production price, with a 50% deposit, signature, certificate and perks such as first access, in-home tuning and Planet EARF membership. We recommend a premium over list, not a discount. A discount anchors the brand low, and the scarcity is real (for example, one Tropos pair per colour).
   - Precedents for limited editions priced above standard: Devialet's Opéra de Paris edition at 1.13× ([Residential Systems](https://www.residentialsystems.com/technology/audio/devialet-unveils-phantom-ultimate-speakers)) [V]; Flos Arco K at 3.1× ([Flos](https://flos.com/en/us/arco-k/M-arco-k.html)) [V]; B&O's Beolab 90 editions at roughly 2× or more ([ChannelNews](https://www.channelnews.com.au/bang-olufsen-caps-centenary-series-with-limited-edition-beolab-90-speakers/), [B&O](https://www.bang-olufsen.com/en/us/speakers/beolab-90)) [V].
   - Counter-example: Buchardt used a **pre-order discount** for the A700 ([HiFi Trends](https://hifitrends.com/2020/05/27/buchardts-stunning-new-active-floorstanding-speaker-is-a-big-deal/)) [V]. That suits a value brand, not a collectible one.
3. **Gallery editions (unique, or 8 + 2 AP).** Special forms and finishes for galleries at about 2–3× the production price. This follows the structure of Marcelis's gallery editions ([Gallery Sally Dan-Cuthbert](https://gallerysallydancuthbert.com/artworks/2658-sabine-marcelis-slide-light-mocha-2023/)) [V].

### 2.3 Margin logic [E unless linked]

- **Rule-of-thumb multiples.** Hi-fi sold through a full distributor-and-dealer chain is commonly priced at **about 5× parts cost** ([Sound & Vision](https://www.soundandvision.com/content/it-costs-how-much-0)) [X].
  - At 5×, Tropos would be about $47,900 and Mantle about $19,100 [E]. Both are above what the comparables support.
  - **Conclusion:** EARF cannot afford a two-tier chain (importer plus dealer). Sell direct, and use single-tier dealers only after the cost-downs above.
  - We use **3× as the floor for DTC** and **4× for anything sold wholesale** [E].
- **Direct (DTC).** EARF keeps the full list price, less payment fees, white-glove delivery, returns and the cost of acquiring customers [E]. DTC brands give this as their reason for selling direct: Buchardt says it bypasses dealers and distributors to "avoid the high margins of traditional Hi-Fi retail" ([Buchardt](https://www.buchardtaudio.com/pages/about-us)) [V]. Volti's Lucera is sold direct ([Part-Time Audiophile](https://pt.audio/2024/10/15/lucera-from-volti-audio-announcements)) [V], and OJAS sells from its own store ([OJAS](https://checkout.ojas.nyc/collections/frontpage)) [V].
- **Hi-fi dealers.** Stereophile describes the industry as working "on a margin of 40% from retail", with overhead eating 25–30 points of that ([Stereophile](https://www.stereophile.com//features/548/)) [X].
  - A former high-end shop employee in New Zealand reported a cost + 50% markup, which is about a 33% margin; another poster put top-tier electronics retailers at "no more than 35%" ([StereoNET forum, 2007](https://www.stereonet.com/forums/topic/4088-retailer-margins-and-discounts/)) [V].
  - We model **40% to the dealer** [E].
- **Design retail and galleries.** Art galleries' standard split is 50/50 ([Hyperallergic, 2025](https://hyperallergic.com/1034068/its-time-rethink-the-50-50-split-with-art-galleries/) [V]; [The Art Newspaper, 2015](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2015/08/26/the-great-debate-why-galleries-could-take-even-more-money-from-their-artists) [V]). Furniture placed on consignment in shops is "usually a 60/40 split", with 60% to the maker ([WoodWeb forum, 2006](https://woodweb.com/knowledge_base/Selling_Furniture_Through_A_Retail_Store.html)) [V]. We model **50%** for both galleries and design retail (keystone) [E].
- **What follows**
  - Standard Tropos and Mantle **should not be consigned to galleries at list price**. At 1.7× and 1.2× parts, the gallery's cut leaves too little. Galleries get gallery editions instead (section 2.4).
  - The lamps work as products only once production tooling is in place. Their one-off costs include moulds and setup, which is why the list prices sit below prototype cost.

### 2.5 Sensitivities and risks [E]

- **Tropos at $28,000** would leave it at only 2.9× parts. **Tropos at $36,000** puts it above the Jubilee's MSRP, a hard sell for a new brand. $32,000 is the credible middle.
- **Mantle is the hardest product to price.** The Pro-Ject 6 PerspeX, at $2,999, already sells the "acrylic and floating" idea ([Audio Advisor](https://www.audioadvisor.com/products/pro-ject-6-perspex-balanced-turntable-acrylic-pick-it-mc-3)) [V]. Mantle has to justify about 3× that price through thick tinted cast acrylic, the lit base and a better tonearm.
- **Hold price parity across all channels** (web, Planet EARF, dealers) using a minimum-advertised-price policy, so dealers aren't undercut [E].

## 3. Channels

### 3.1 Direct to consumer: web and Planet EARF (primary, year 1) [E]

- **Tropos and Mantle should launch DTC only.** These are the only channels where prototype-cost margins hold (section 2.2). OJAS shows how a brand-owned store can carry everything from $39 books and a $60 T-shirt to a $6,000 built pair of speakers and the $11,995 kO-R2 ([OJAS store](https://checkout.ojas.nyc/collections/frontpage)) [V].
- **Founders' editions sell DTC with a 50% deposit**, which pre-funds moulds and tooling [E].

### 3.2 Hi-fi dealers: how the margins work

- **The basic arithmetic.** The manufacturer sets the MSRP and sells to the dealer at MSRP minus the dealer's margin, about 40% ([Stereophile](https://www.stereophile.com//features/548/)) [X]. A 40% margin means the dealer pays 60% of list.
- **Overhead.** That 40% is not profit: overhead commonly takes 25–30 points of it (same source) [X].
- **Smaller shops.** Some price at cost + 50%, which is about a 33% margin; top-tier electronics retailers run at no more than 35% ([StereoNET forum, 2007](https://www.stereonet.com/forums/topic/4088-retailer-margins-and-discounts/)) [V].
- **Discounting.** Shoppers report discounts of roughly 8–30% off the recommended price in that thread [V]. That erodes the dealer's margin, which is why dealers ask for territory protection and price discipline [E].
- **Heritage brands keep dealer networks.** Klipsch sells the Jubilee through "Certified Klipsch Heritage dealers" ([Klipsch](https://www.klipsch.com/news/introducing-the-klipsch-jubilee-floorstanding-speaker)) [V]. The kO-R1 collaboration was sold through both the OJAS store and selected authorised Klipsch retailers ([ecoustics](https://www.ecoustics.com/products/klipsch-ojas-ko-r1/)) [V].
- **Recommendation [E]**
  - Year 2: 2–3 "listening partner" dealers, with territory protection.
  - Demo units on consignment.
  - Only once Tropos parts are ≤ $8,000 and Mantle parts ≤ $2,375.
  - No importers or distributors until volume supports another margin layer.

### 3.3 Design galleries and shops

We found no published consignment terms for any of the four named galleries. Here is what their public pages show:

- **The Future Perfect** has showrooms in New York, Miami, Los Angeles and **San Francisco**, plus a trade programme for designers and architects ([The Future Perfect](https://www.thefutureperfect.com/about/)) [V]. It sells studio-made, made-to-order lighting at full retail with long lead times; for example, Lindsey Adelman at $19,500 with a 20–25 week lead time ([The Future Perfect](https://www.thefutureperfect.com/product/lighting/ceiling-lighting/suspension/5-globe-branching-bubble-bb-05-28/)) [V].
  - **It is the most natural first design retailer for Hemisphere and Core in San Francisco [E].**
- **R & Company** (Tribeca; founded by Zesty Meyers and Evan Snyderman) represents historical and contemporary designers, including the Haas Brothers and Katie Stout. It holds an archive of about 10,000 design items, and its stated aim is "nurturing and sustaining the markets and careers" of designers ([R & Company](https://www.r-and-company.com/about/)) [V].
- **Friedman Benda** (since 2007; New York, Los Angeles, Paris) "represents globally prominent designers and artists, as well as historically significant estates" ([Friedman Benda](https://www.friedmanbenda.com/about/)) [V].
- **Carpenters Workshop Gallery** produces work as well as selling it. It runs an 8,000 m² workshop in suburban Paris, opened in 2016, and its founders cite "ensuring rarity and provenance". In 2022 it also had a San Francisco space, housed in a church ([Nuvo, 2022](https://nuvomagazine.com/daily-edit/carpenters-workshop-gallery-design)) [V]. Its current site lists London, Paris, New York and Los Angeles only ([Carpenters Workshop Gallery](https://carpenters.art/)) [V].
- **Terms to expect [E].** Assume a consignment split of 50/50, in line with the art-world norm ([Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/1034068/its-time-rethink-the-50-50-split-with-art-galleries/)) [V].
  - Negotiate a minimum net price.
  - Payment within 30 days of the collector paying.
  - Gallery covers insurance on its premises; EARF covers shipping to the gallery.
  - A defined consignment term.
  - Price-on-request presentation for gallery editions.
  - **Only gallery editions** go to galleries, never standard production pieces (section 2.4).

  - Million Goods (Brooklyn, 2026) combines a bar, a shop and a listening room ([Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2026/09/25/gjergji-shkurti-million-goods-ft-green/)) [V].

  - Silence Please, a New York horn-speaker studio, opened a showroom and listening space with a record shop and a tea room ([Dezeen, Feb 2025](https://www.dezeen.com/2025/02/21/silence-please-giant-living-room-showroom-listening-space-in-new-york/)) [V]. **This is the closest precedent for Planet EARF.**
  - USM commissioned an OJAS listening room for its New York store in 2023 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devon_Turnbull)) [V].
- **In the Bay Area,** Bar Shiru in Uptown Oakland calls itself "The Bay Area's first hi-fi vinyl listening bar" and opened in February 2019 ([Bar Shiru](https://www.barshiru.com/)) [V].
- **Commercial terms [E].** Expect contract or trade pricing of about 10–20% off list for specified projects. Offer placement on loan, or a revenue share, for 2–3 flagship San Francisco venues in return for for-sale signage and events.

### 3.5 Planet EARF [E]

Price every object at DTC list with QR checkout, ship from stock, and run operator-led listening sessions in the format SFMOMA used for Turnbull's room ([SFMOMA](https://www.sfmoma.org/devon-turnbull-listening-room-schedule/)) [V]. The bar's margin pays for the showroom.

**Channel sequence [E]**

- **Year 1:** DTC and Planet EARF for all four products, plus Founders' Editions.
- **Year 1–2:** The Future Perfect (San Francisco) for the lamps once production tooling is in place, and gallery editions in a gallery show.
- **Year 2:** 2–3 hi-fi dealers for Tropos and Mantle after the cost-downs, plus hospitality placements.

## 5. Five things that built world-class brands, and how EARF can apply them

1. **Make the product a place people go to listen, before treating it as a catalogue item.**
   - **What they did.** OJAS built its name through rooms and installations.
     - Ace Hotel lobby, mid-2000s; 14 Supreme stores; Public Records.
     - Listening rooms at Lisson Gallery (2022–23), SFMOMA's *Art of Noise* (2024) and the Cooper Hewitt (12 Dec 2025 to 19 Jul 2026).
     - A custom system for Virgil Abloh's 2019 MCA Chicago show.
   - Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devon_Turnbull), [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2025/12/15/devon-turnbull-diy-audio-the-cooper-hewitt/) [V].
   - **EARF [E]:**
     - Make Planet EARF the permanent EARF listening room, with an operator-led session schedule in SFMOMA's format.
     - Propose a Tropos and Hemisphere listening room to a Bay Area museum or design fair.
     - Seed 2–3 San Francisco venues with on-loan systems carrying "for sale" cards.

2. **Keep one signature form in production for decades.**
   - **What they did.**
     - Louis Poulsen still sells the PH 5 and PH Artichoke, both designed in 1958 ([Louis Poulsen](https://www.louispoulsen.com/en-us/c/shop-by-collection/ph-5)) [V].
     - Flos still sells the 1962 Arco ([Flos](https://flos.com/en/us/arco-steel-incandescent-arco/FU030000.html)) [V].
     - Klipsch's Heritage line is "designed and handcrafted in the USA", and the company traces itself to Hope, Arkansas, in 1946 ([Klipsch](https://www.klipsch.com/news/introducing-the-klipsch-jubilee-floorstanding-speaker)) [V].
   - **EARF [E]:**
     - Treat the Tropos horn profile, the four forms and the eight named colours as permanent. Avoid yearly model changes.
     - Publish the colour system as a brand asset, and make "San Francisco-made, tinted all the way through" the heritage story from day one.

3. **Put numbered scarcity on top of a permanent line.**
   - **What they did.**
     - Klipsch × OJAS went from 100 pairs (sold out) to 600 numbered pairs ([OJAS](https://checkout.ojas.nyc/collections/frontpage), [Gear Patrol](https://www.gearpatrol.com/audio/klipsch-ojas-k0-r2-loudspeaker-price-release-date/)) [V].
     - B&O's Beolab 90 editions: 10 pairs of each, with a certificate ([ChannelNews](https://www.channelnews.com.au/bang-olufsen-caps-centenary-series-with-limited-edition-beolab-90-speakers/)) [V].
     - Flos's Arco K uses a crystal base ([Flos](https://flos.com/en/us/arco-k/M-arco-k.html)) [V].
     - Marcelis's resin lights come in editions of 8 + 2 AP ([Gallery Sally Dan-Cuthbert](https://gallerysallydancuthbert.com/artworks/2658-sabine-marcelis-slide-light-mocha-2023/)) [V].
     - Kuramata's acrylic chair (56 made) has sold for more than $500,000 ([Phillips](https://phillips.com/press/release/phillips-june-design-sales-realize-51-million), [6sqft](https://www.6sqft.com/iconic-plastic-armchair-sells-at-sothebys-auction-for-409000/)) [V].
   - **EARF [E]:**
     - Founders' Editions with one Tropos pair and one Mantle per colour, numbered certificates, and the lamps at 8 per colour + 2 AP.
     - Gallery editions for galleries only.
     - Never reissue a founders' colour-and-number combination.

4. **Borrow credibility from a heritage maker or an institution.**
   - **What they did.**
     - OJAS's horn designs are handcrafted by Klipsch in Hope, Arkansas ([OJAS](https://ojas.nyc/products/klipsch-ko-r2)) [V].
     - OJAS also co-branded a Denon cartridge ([OJAS store](https://checkout.ojas.nyc/collections/frontpage)) [V].
     - Devialet has partnered with the Paris Opera since 2017. Its gold-leaf edition sells for $500 more, and the partnership helps fund the Opera's artistic work ([Residential Systems](https://www.residentialsystems.com/technology/audio/devialet-unveils-phantom-ultimate-speakers)) [V].
   - **EARF [E]:** Pursue one credible audio partner, such as the makers of Tropos's drivers or amplifier modules, and one San Francisco cultural partner for a co-branded edition. These are ideas only; no partnership exists or is implied.

5. **Build a price ladder with accessible entry objects, and make variation a feature.**
   - **What they did.**
     - The OJAS store runs from $39 books and $60 merchandise to a $550 cartridge, $6,000 speakers and the $11,995 collaboration ([OJAS store](https://checkout.ojas.nyc/collections/frontpage)) [V].
     - Pesce's Fish Design vases sell for $510, and each one is unique, while his resin pieces fetch £69,850 at auction ([MOCA Store](https://mocastore.org/products/gaetano-pesce-medium-indian-summer-vase-in-matte-f-and-matte-turquoise), [Phillips](https://www.phillips.com/detail/gaetano-pesce/UK050224/112)) [V].
     - Louis Poulsen runs from $285 portable lamps to the $62,300 PH Artichoke Glass ([Louis Poulsen](https://www.louispoulsen.com/en-us/shop-by/gifting/by-price), [Louis Poulsen](https://www.louispoulsen.com/en-us/catalog/private/pendants/ph-artichoke?v=90145-5741945403-03)) [V].
   - **EARF [E]:**
     - Ladder: small acrylic objects under $200 (for example a record weight or coasters, as an idea); Core at $750; Hemisphere at $1,200; Mantle at $9,500; Tropos at $32,000; then gallery editions.
     - Records, books and merchandise at Planet EARF.
     - Batch-number each cast-acrylic run, and present the slight colour variation between batches as the point, as Pesce did.
