# Planet EARF: where

Round 42, 31 October 2026. Three San Francisco neighbourhoods and one in Oakland, for a 2,000 to 3,000 sq ft ground floor.
The rents are **asking rents from public listings and broker reports** (2025 to 2026), gathered in
`research-market.md` with their links. Nobody was contacted. Zoning is from `research-sf-rules.md`. "NNN" means the
tenant also pays the building's taxes, insurance and upkeep, about $12 per sq ft a year in SF *(inference, from the
49 Page St listing)*.

What a site has to give the house, in order:
1. **A Bar permitted outright**, or a clear route to one.
2. **No homes directly above or beside it** (the bass limit, Police Code 2909).
3. **A second exit**, and ideally sprinklers already in.
4. **Height**: 14 ft or more for the record wall and the stacks.
5. **People who buy design**, walking past in daylight, for the shop.

## 1. Hayes Valley: the shop window

**Why it fits.**
- The city's hi-fi bars are already here (Phonobar, Le Fantastique).
- The streets fill with people buying design all day, which is what the shop wall needs.
- A Bar is permitted, from 6 am to 2 am. With a Limited Live Performance permit (selectors until 10 or 11 pm), the
  house can open in about 8 months. Selectors to closing need a POE, which makes it Nighttime Entertainment: a
  Conditional Use, so about 11 to 12 months.

**Against it.**
- The tightest market in the city (about 2% vacancy west of Van Ness).
- The highest rent of the four.
- Homes are above most storefronts, so check the bass limit before anything else.
- A 2,800 sq ft space with height is rare here. A smaller house (the drawings at 2,000 sq ft: shop, bar room, a
  smaller listening room) is the likelier fit.

## 2. SoMa, the Folsom Street entertainment zone: the room

**Why it fits.**
- Folsom between 7th and 8th is a designated entertainment zone.
- The buildings are old industrial ones: high ceilings, few neighbours above, and a night-time street where bass is
  expected.
- **1539 Folsom**, a former bar at $27.25, is the cheapest rent in this note. Its building is for sale at $2.5M, so an
  SBA 504 purchase (10% down, 25-year term) would fix the occupancy cost for good *(inference)*.
- The vacancy nearby gives a tenant leverage to ask for an improvement allowance and free rent.

**Against it.**
- A Bar needs Conditional Use in the SoMa NCT: 4 to 6 months and $3k to $40k.
- Little daytime design traffic, so the shop depends on people coming on purpose.
- The street itself.

## 3. Dogpatch: the workshop

**Why it fits.**
- The bars cluster at 3rd and 20th beside The Pearl.
- Warehouse shells with height.
- A maker-and-design neighbourhood, where EARF's acrylic workshop would make sense nearby.
- **2245 3rd St** is already a second-generation taproom (2,876 sq ft): plumbing, maybe a bar, maybe exits.
  Second-generation space costs 30 to 50% less to build out.

**Against it.**
- **The zoning for a bar was not verified this round** (UMU and NCT, sec. 843).
- It is quieter in the evening than Hayes or SoMa.
- Some new homes are close.

## 4. Oakland, Uptown: the lean version

**Why it fits.**
- Rent is about half San Francisco's: 2,800 sq ft costs about $80,000 a year, against about $170,000.
- Uptown has the Fox, the Paramount, galleries and Bar Shiru, the Bay Area's first analog listening bar, so the
  audience exists.
- **1735 Telegraph** is a second-generation bar (1,966 sq ft at $27): the smallest version of the plan, near opening as
  it stands.

**Against it.**
- Bar Shiru is next door in spirit and, by its owner's account, has never made a profit.
- Oakland is not the "San Francisco" in the brief.
- Alameda County's licence market and Oakland's zoning and entertainment permits were not researched this round.

## The recommendation

**SoMa's Folsom Street, at 1539 Folsom or a space like it, if the Conditional Use is acceptable. Otherwise Hayes
Valley at a smaller size.**

The numbers (`NUMBERS.md`) show why rent decides. At the base case each $10 per sq ft is about $28,000 of EBITDA a year,
and the base case is close to break-even. SoMa gives the room the plan wants (height, few neighbours, a night street) at
a third less rent, plus a chance to own the building. Hayes gives the shop its customers, and opens three months sooner if selector nights end at 10 or 11 pm.
Either way, the five checks on page one come before the lease.

Oakland is the fallback that makes the money easiest. The owner should decide whether "San Francisco" is the brief or
the Bay is.
