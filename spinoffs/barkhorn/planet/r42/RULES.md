# Planet EARF: San Francisco's rules, and the path to opening

Round 42, 31 October 2026. This is a summary of `research-sf-rules.md`, which sits beside this file and has a source
for every number below. Prices and fees are as published in 2025 and 2026. The interpretations marked *(inference)*
are this round's, not a lawyer's. A land-use attorney and a licensing consultant should confirm them before anything is
signed.

## The answer in one paragraph

**Buy a Type 48 on the transfer market** (about $100,000, with recent deals at $70,000 to $150,000) for **a site where
Planning permits both a Bar and Nighttime Entertainment outright**, such as an NC-3 corridor. Then add a **Place of
Entertainment permit** so a selector can play records ($2,708, about 2 to 3 months).

One catch: under Planning Code 102, a bar that presents entertainment needing a POE *is* "Nighttime Entertainment". That
use needs Conditional Use in Hayes-Gough and most other neighbourhood districts. There are two ways round it:
- a **Limited Live Performance** permit, which keeps the use a Bar, but the music ends by 10 or 11 pm;
- a Conditional Use hearing. The rest is ordinary tenant-improvement permitting:
- DBI building permit, about 3 to 4% of the job in fees, 2 to 4 months of review;
- DPH plan check ($1,241);
- Fire Department Place of Assembly permit ($446).

That path opens in **about 8 months from a signed lease**. A site that needs Conditional Use adds 3 to 4 months, for
**about 11 to 12**. The Mission's alcohol district is a trap: Conditional Use, and the licence must come from another
bar inside the district.

## Which ABC licence

| Licence | What it allows | Food | Minors | Can SF get a new one? | Cost in SF |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **48**, on-sale general, public premises | beer, wine, spirits | not required | no (21 and over) | no; capped by county quota, so buy one | **~$100k** on the market; ABC transfer fee $1,565 |
| **47**, on-sale general, eating place | beer, wine, spirits | a bona fide eating place: meals at all hours open | yes | no; buy one (same market as the 48) | the same price; switching a 47 to a 48 costs $125 |
| **42**, on-sale beer and wine, public premises | beer and wine | not required | no | yes, not capped | $1,135 |
| **41**, on-sale beer and wine, eating place | beer and wine | meals | yes | yes | $1,135 |

**Why the 48.** Planet EARF is a cocktail bar with snacks, 21 and over. A 47 would need a real kitchen, which costs
$50,000 to $150,000 to build *(inference)* and changes the plan. A 47 also makes the place a Restaurant in the Planning
Code, which helps only in districts where a Bar needs Conditional Use. A 42 (beer and wine) is the fallback if no 48 can
be bought: cheap and quick, but no cocktails, and the cocktail list is half the bar's revenue *(inference)*. The city's
new cheap licences (September 2026: 10 downtown, 5 neighbourhood Type 87s, $19,840 each) are restaurant-only and cannot
be transferred, so they do not apply.

**The ABC process.**
- A 30-day posting, with notices mailed to everyone within 500 ft, and protests heard if any are filed.
- ABC investigates in 55 to 65 days at best.
- If the census tract has more licences than ABC's undue-concentration limit (most commercial corridors do), the Board
  of Supervisors must find "public convenience or necessity" for a 48. It does so within 90 days, in parallel.
- Plan for **3 to 5 months** *(inference)*.

## Music: the Entertainment Commission

| | Place of Entertainment (POE) | Limited Live Performance (LLP) |
|---|---|---|
| Covers | a DJ or selector, any hour the licence allows | music as a secondary use that ends by 10 or 11 pm, on at most 200 sq ft |
| Fee (FY 2026-27) | $2,708 (waived under First Year Free) | $612 |
| Process | 30-day posted notice, a hearing, a sound test | the same |
| Time | about 2 to 3 months | about 2 to 3 months |

A playlist or a bartender's records with no performer needs no permit. **A selector in a booth is a performer, so
Planet EARF needs a POE.** The noise limit (Police Code Article 29, section 2909) is 8 dBA over ambient at the property
line, plus 8 dBC for a permitted venue, which bounds the bass. It sets what the sound isolation must achieve: the
vestibule's sound lock, the listening room built as a room within the room, and stacks that fire away from the party
walls (A-101, notes 3 and 4). Isolation costs $20,000 to $150,000 or more *(inference)*. A site with no homes above or
beside it is worth real money.

|---|---|---|

| Valencia NCT | Conditional Use | and inside the Mission Alcoholic Beverage SUD: the licence must come from another bar in the district |
| SoMa NCT | Conditional Use | the Folsom Street entertainment zone is here; ask Planning which district covers a given address, since SoMa's districts differ |
| North Beach NCD | Conditional Use, and only where the space was last a bar | |
| Dogpatch (UMU, NCT) | **not verified**: check Planning Code sec. 843 | |

Source for the definitions: [Planning Code sec. 102](https://gocodebook.com/library/us/ca/san-francisco-planning/article-1-general-zoning-provisions/sec.-102.-definitions)
("Nighttime Entertainment ... including Restaurants and Bars which present such activities").

Conditional Use costs **$3,147** in fees ($15,000 to $40,000 with counsel, *inference*). Since 16 August 2025 PermitSF
guarantees the hearing within 90 days of a complete application, so plan on **4 to 6 months**. Prop H (2020) dropped
neighbourhood notice for permitted storefront changes and set a 30-day target.

## Health, fire, building

- **Public Health:** plan check for a bar or lounge $1,241 (about $2,208 with a kitchen), 20 working days; permit to
  operate $470. The annual health licence for bars is $0 (Prop M waiver).
- **Fire Department:** Place of Assembly permit $446, required at 50 occupants or more.
- **Building code** (2025 CBC, in force in SF from 1 January 2026):
  - a bar is A-2 at 50 or more;
  - two exits at 50 or more, with panic hardware;
  - sprinklers at 100 or more, or a fire area over 5,000 sq ft.
- **DBI building permit:** about 2.5% of a $250,000 job and 2.1% of a $500,000 job. Budget 3 to 4% for all city permit
  fees *(inference)*. Review takes 2 to 4 months.
- **Plumbing** (CPC 422): for about 99 people, roughly 1 men's WC and 1 urinal, 2 women's WCs, and a lavatory each
  *(inference from the table)*. Three or four single-user all-gender rooms meet that, since they count for either sex.
  The plan draws three. Confirm with the plumbing official's own load.
- **Accessibility** (CBC 11B):
  - a 60 in length of the counter at 28 to 34 in high;
  - 5% of seats accessible;
  - 36 in routes, with no steps (the plan is one level).

### What this means for the drawn plan

The drawing set (A-000) calculates **133 occupants** with the code's load factors, so the house is an A-2 that needs
sprinklers. Many SF shells already have them. Retrofitting runs $40,000 to $80,000 *(inference)*, which is in the
construction budget.

**The option to stay at 99 or fewer**, which drops the sprinkler requirement, is worth checking with the building
official:
- count the front room as the shop it is (mercantile, 60 sq ft gross per person): 15 occupants instead of 40;
- fix the listening room's 11 chairs to the floor, so they count as seats: 11 instead of 32.

That totals **87**. It is a real design choice: the front room is then a shop with a few chairs, not a lounge, and the
posted load holds the room to it.

## The critical path (months from a signed lease, a site where the uses are permitted)

| Month | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0 to 1 | Register the business (starts First Year Free). Architect starts the drawings. Negotiate the Type 48 and open escrow. First call to the Entertainment Commission. |
| 1 to 4 | DBI review (Planning inside it). DPH plan check in parallel. ABC transfer filed: its 30-day posting and the 500 ft mailing run. PCN letter to the Board of Supervisors. |
| 3 to 4 | File the POE, so its notice, hearing and sound test end with construction. |
| 4 to 8 | Build, isolation included. Sound test. Final inspections: DBI, Fire (Place of Assembly), DPH (permit to operate). ABC issues the licence; escrow closes. |
| **~8** | **Open.** (About 11 to 12 where Conditional Use is needed, including for late selector nights in Hayes-Gough.) |

**Before signing a lease:**
1. Confirm the zoning (Bar P).
2. Check whether the tract is over-concentrated (the PCN).
3. Find out who lives above and beside the space.
4. Find out whether the building is sprinklered.
5. Check that a second exit exists.

Those five facts move the budget and the calendar more than any design choice in this set.
