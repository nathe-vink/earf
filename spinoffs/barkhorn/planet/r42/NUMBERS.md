# Planet EARF: the numbers

Round 42, 31 October 2026. This is the money for the house drawn in `drawings/`: 2,800 sq ft, 55 seats, a bar room, a
listening room and a shop wall.

- Every input is in `numbers42.py`, marked **research** (from `research-market.md` and `research-sf-rules.md`, which
  have the sources) or **assumption** (this round's, for the owner to overturn).
- The tables below are its output.
- A run-rate year means a normal year once the house is open, not the first months.
- Nothing here has been checked by an accountant or a lender.

## What the comparables show

| Venue | What it is | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| **Bar Shiru**, Oakland (2019) | analog-only, horns on tube amps, parties of six or fewer, $13 ticketed sessions | Seven years open and, its owner told KALW in May 2026, **never profitable**; it has bought fewer records since 2022. Listening alone does not pay the rent. |
| **Public Records**, Brooklyn (2019) | 6,000 sq ft compound: a 200-capacity Sound Room on a custom OJAS system, a café and bar by day, ticketed shows | Scale and **daytime use**: the same rooms earn from morning coffee to late shows. |
| **Eavesdrop**, Brooklyn (2022) | 1,000 sq ft, 36 seats, Danley horns built into a lit cabinet | **Reserved time slots are the cover charge**: the seat is the ticket. |
| **Gold Line**, Los Angeles (2018) | a record label's bar under its offices, 7,500 records from the founder's collection | **A brand can run a bar.** It has lasted, and the team opened a second bar across the street. |
| **Tokyo's jazz kissa** (JBS, Bar Martha) | one owner, 10,000 records, no cover or about ¥800 | The model **works only at a tiny cost base**: one person, a small room, low rent. About 600 kissa at the mid-1970s peak, 100 to 500 now. |
| Le Fantastique, The Pretender | hi-fi spent as the draw: more than $150k, and more than $60k | The **system is a capital cost** the room has to earn back. |
| Sounds Good, Toronto; kwia, Berlin | closed in 2026 after about 2 and 5 years | "Licensing and capacity", and **rent**. |

**What that means for Planet EARF.** A listening bar that is only a bar sits at the edge of profit in a big-city lease.
The benchmarks agree:
- full-service restaurants' median pretax margin was 2.8% in 2024 (NRA);
- occupancy runs 6 to 10% of sales.

At 2,800 sq ft and $57 a sq ft, rent alone is 10% of sales only once sales pass $1.6M (about $570 a sq ft, a
high-performer's figure).

The house has two things the comparables do not:
1. **A shop wall selling EARF's own pieces at their own margin.** One Tropos pair a month is about $228,000 of sales,
   roughly the rent.
2. **A brand behind it** that would spend money to be seen anyway.

The model treats both plainly. The shop is revenue at a 55% margin to the house *(assumption)*. The brand's marketing
value is **not** counted.

### Break-even

- Fixed costs at the base staffing, with debt service: $1,140,431 a year.
- A guest at the bar contributes $27 after pour cost and card fees (spend $36).
- With the base door and shop: **87 guests a night**, 312 nights a year (1.6 per seat a night).
- With no shop sales at all: **114 guests a night**.
- At the low case's 60 guests a night, the shop has to sell about **46 Tropos pairs a year** (or their margin in lamps) to break even.

## How to fund it

**The base case needs about $2.0M to open (about $700 a sq ft, everything in).** The base EBITDA (about $120k) supports
an SBA 7(a) loan of about $0.6M at 1.25 times cover. So:

| Source | Amount (base) | Terms (research) | Notes |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **SBA 7(a)** | ~$0.6M | up to $5M; capped at prime (7.00%) + 3% = 10.0% over $350k; 10 years for improvements and working capital; 2% to 3.75% upfront fee on the guaranteed part, 0.55% a year | Personal guarantees from every 20% owner. California allows no lien on a liquor licence, so the licence comes from equity. |
| **Landlord's improvement allowance** | $0 to $60 a sq ft | no SF retail figure is published; $40 to $100 a sq ft nationally where a landlord wants a food anchor | Ask for the allowance **and 3 to 6 months of free rent** where vacancy is high (SoMa, Union Square at 20%). |
| **Investors, equity** | ~$0.9M | a common structure: investors take 90% of profits until repaid plus 25%, then the split reverts | Hospitality money expects to lose some of the time; show them the shop's margin. |
| **A community round** | ~$0.4M | Rikki's (Castro) raised $425k on Wefunder in 2024 to 2025 as a revenue share: 5% of revenue until investors have 1.5x, from more than 80 investors | Turns the first regulars into owners. It suits a brand with a following. Mainvest, the restaurant platform, closed in 2024. |
| **EARF itself** | in kind | the Tropos pairs, the lamps, Mother EARF and the shop's stock at cost or on consignment | The shop wall is EARF's showroom. Paying for it as marketing, not as bar capex, takes $100k to $150k off the bar's cost. |

## The levers, in order of what they are worth

1. **Sell the pieces.** One more Tropos pair a month adds about $119,000 of EBITDA, more than ten more guests a night.
   The shop is EARF's reason to be on the street and the bar's reason to make money. Give it daylight hours, a
   salesperson who knows the line, and a listening room that books demos by day.
2. **Take a second-generation space.** A space that was a bar (1539 Folsom, 1735 Telegraph, 2245 3rd St) keeps its
   plumbing, exits and maybe its sprinklers. That cuts construction 30 to 50% (the low column, about $1.4M all in).
3. **Fill the counter and the door.** Ten more guests a night, or $4 more each, is about $84,000. Two more private
   hires a month (brand launches, label listening parties, a record fair) add about $49,000.
4. **Hold the rent down.** Each $10 a sq ft is about $28,000 a year (`SITES.md`).
5. **Staff to the night.** Forty fewer hours a week, early in the week, is about $56,000.

**Conclusion.** The house works when it is **a showroom that pours drinks**, not a bar with a shop in the corner. On
bar and door alone it needs about 114 guests a night to break even. With the base shop it needs about 87, against a
base of 90. The base case is close to break-even, so the plan has to be cheap to open (a second-generation space,
EARF's pieces at cost) and very good at selling the line.
