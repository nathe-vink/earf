Why the debut is not in the storefront

Vacant to Vibrant takes applications all year, reviews them in 30 to 45 days, and then matches a business to a space in “two to six months”. An application sent in November gets a decision in December and a storefront between February and June. So 19 January moves to Fort Mason, where FOG itself is, and the pop-up opens when the match comes.

The honest gap, and the fallback

About $14,500 to $25,000 of the pop-up is spent before the first deposit (drapes, shelves, permits, insurance, printing), and nothing yet covers it until a partner pays up front. At the high end the gap is about five pairs’ deposits. The night

From the door to last call.

  1. The door

    A plain blackened-steel door, recessed, with a small etched disc for the name and no queue rope. Two doors eight feet apart make a sound lock, so the music stays in. Inside is the host stand: a dark-oak top on a steel post, and on it a bowl of EARFcoins. The host finds you a seat and puts one coin in your hand: “That’s your song.”

  2. The shop

    On the right, by the glass, is the shop wall: oiled oak on blackened steel. It holds the eight Cores in their tints, a Mantle on its shelf, records, and three Hemispheres hung low in front, with a Tropos playing on its plinth beside it. Every piece carries a card, and every piece in the house is for sale, starting here. Low chairs by the window face the street.

  3. The bar

    Ten seats at a zinc-topped racetrack counter with a fluted oak front. Its first five feet drop to table height, so a wheelchair user sits at the bar too. Six Hemispheres hang low over the zinc. At the far end, the selector’s booth is set into a floor-to-ceiling wall of records, with the request column on its desk.

  4. The tables

    On the right is a banquette with six two-tops. In the middle is a long communal table, and Mother EARF hangs over it. Each table has a Core.

  5. The listening room

    Through a lobby with two doors is a room within the room, with its own walls and ceiling. Eleven low tub chairs face the altar, with a Tropos stack on each side of it, toed in to the one seat that is exactly right. Quiet is the rule here, and a card on each table says so, once. This is where the sessions happen.

  6. Last call

    Called by the record, not by a bell. The selector plays the house’s closing side, the same track every night and announced nowhere. The pendants come down a stop over its length. Any coins left in the column are played, or handed back with the bill.

Capacity, hours and sound

Loud enough to listen, quiet enough to talk.

133 by the code, or 87

The building code counts 133 people by floor area, at 15 sq ft a person at tables. That makes the house an A-2 assembly: two exits with panic hardware, a Fire Department Place of Assembly permit, and sprinklers. Many SF shells have sprinklers already.

There is an option to stay at 87 and drop the sprinkler requirement. Count the front room as the shop it is, and fix the listening chairs to the floor. That is a question for the building official before the lease.

Hours

Tuesday to Thursday, 5 pm to midnight. Friday and Saturday, 5 pm to 1:30 am. Sunday, 3 to 10 pm. Closed Monday. The shop and the demos run Wednesday to Sunday, noon to 5.

Sound targets, est.

In the bar, 60 to 65 dBA while records play, so two people at a table can talk in an ordinary voice. In the listening room, 78 to 82 during a Session, where nobody talks. Outside, the Police Code’s limits apply: 8 dBA over the ambient at the property line, and 8 dBC in the bass. The vestibule’s two doors, the room within the room, and stacks that fire away from the party walls are what keep it there.

Five facts to know before a lease

1. Does the zoning allow the use outright? 2. Is the tract over-licensed? 3. Who lives above and beside the space? 4. Is the building sprinklered? 5. Is there a second exit? Those five move the budget and the calendar more than any design choice. Where a Conditional Use is needed, opening takes 11 to 12 months.

Break-even

87 guests a night with the base shop and door, about 1.6 a seat. Without any shop sales it is 114. At the low case’s 60 guests, the shop would have to sell about 46 Tropos pairs a year.

What moves it

  • Ten more guests a night, or $4 more each: +$84k

  • Two more private hires a month: +$49k

Mother EARF in public · a proposal

Amsterdam Light Festival, Edition 16.

The one live, paid open call that fits the globe. It closes 7 December 2026 at 23:59 CET; the festival runs on the canals from 25 November 2027 to 16 January 2028, with over a million visitors. Selected work gets a €4,000 artist fee, with production and travel covered. The entry is a three-page concept under the owner’s name, with no logos. Nothing has been submitted; the concept text and the portal checklist are the brand lane’s.

The work at 2 m

Mother EARF as signed, scaled to 2 m for the canal a proposal; the signed size stays 1.25 m. Six discs of 25 mm cast acrylic, tinted through, cut flat: green, orange and violet meridians, red, blue and yellow parallels. About 485 kg. The rims glow from a sealed 24 V LED strip set in a groove along each edge, about 175 W; one hard spot on a mast draws the globe again in coloured light on the quay.

Standing outdoors

A correction to an earlier note: flat discs catch more wind than a closed sphere, not less. At a 35 to 40 m/s gust the globe takes about 4 to 6 kN, so its steel plinth carries about 1.0 to 1.8 t of ballast to the engineer’s figure. The discs are cut flat, never formed, so the tint’s 10-year colour guarantee holds.

Install

Three crates: the discs flat in one A-frame, the plinth and armature, the lights. Ballast is hired in Amsterdam. One day with a small crane and a crew of four; half a day to take it away, leaving nothing.

Mother EARF

The globe, at room scale.

EARF’s globe made as an object: six slotted discs, three meridians and three parallels with the axis leaning 23.4°, cut from 25 mm cast acrylic, 1.25 m across. Each disc is cast in its own tint, as on the globe at earf.world, and each rim glows its colour. In the house it hangs over the communal table under one hard spot, its centre 8 ft 4 in up. Its shadow draws the globe a second time on the table and the floor, ring inside ring, in colour. It is the one place in the room where the tints meet.

It costs about $9,000 to $12,000 one-off est. At about 190 kg of acrylic it hangs from the structure on an engineered fixing, over a table and never over the aisle. Cast acrylic burns, and the Fire Code may class the globe as a hanging decoration that must pass a flame test, so it is the first question for the Fire Department. Its name also belongs to the one afternoon with talking: Mother EARF Sunday, and a punch for four called Mother.

A 2 m version for public light festivals is under study; figures to confirm. The design as signed stays 1.25 m. At 2 m the same six discs weigh about 245 kg in 12.7 mm acrylic or about 485 kg in 25 mm est., stand on a ballasted base outdoors, and catch less wind than a closed sphere because the discs are open.
