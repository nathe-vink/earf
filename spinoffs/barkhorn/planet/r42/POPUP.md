# Planet EARF, the pop-up: the bridge to the house

(Two sizes: a lean eight-week first step, then the five-month pop-up. Both use the same storefront and plan.)

Round 42, 31 October 2026, from the coordinator's brief: "a 3 to 6 month downtown listening room and shop ... the
bridge to the full bar", with the debut night in FOG Design+Art week. The rules come from `research-popup.md`,
which has a source and a date for every claim. *(Inference)* marks this round's own reasoning. Nothing has been applied
for, signed or asked of anyone.

**The order of things:**
1. The pop-up proves the room and sells pieces. Its numbers then raise the money.
2. The flagship (`NUMBERS.md`, the drawings) follows about a year later.

## The drinks: a partner pours, on its own licence

| Route | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Type 58 catering by a partner bar** (a downtown Type 47 or 48 licensee with a catering permit) | **Use this.** The partner sells and pours; EARF never sells alcohol. $100 a night (paid by the partner), filed online 5 to 90 days ahead. **36 nights a year at one premises**, so about one night a week for six months. |
| A brand's invitation-only event (B&P 25600.5) | For the debut, on top of the above: 8 a year per location, 4 hours, free to guests |
| Daily licences | Nonprofits only. No. |
| An interim or temporary ABC permit | Only for protested applications or licence transfers. No. |
| EARF's own Type 42 (beer and wine) | $1,135, 3 to 6 months, and the shop becomes 21 and over. Too slow and too narrow. |
| **Other nights** | Zero-proof drinks and coffee, no licence needed |

**The money with the partner.** EARF takes a flat venue fee a night and no cut of the alcohol *(inference: a share of
liquor sales to an unlicensed party is the risk)*. The partner keeps the bar takings. Its staff run the bar on catered
nights.

**Music.** Records with no performer need no permit. A guest selector counts as entertainment: an Entertainment
Commission one-time permit costs $586, is filed two weeks ahead, and is limited to 12 days a year per premises. That
covers the debut and the monthly guest nights.

**Planning.** Ordinance 116-25 (in force from 17 August 2025) lets a vacant space host Pop-Up Retail, entertainment or
arts for up to 3 years. The Planning Director approves it without a hearing. The Temporary Use Authorization costs $644
and takes about 2 weeks; none is needed if the space was last retail.

**Three questions for ABC's San Francisco office before money is spent:**
1. Do regular public nights at an unlicensed shop count as catered "events"?
2. Is SFPD sign-off needed?
3. Can the fee be split as above?

## Vacant to Vibrant

- **What it offers:** three months' free rent, extendable. Some reports say up to six months paid. Grants of $3,000 to
  $8,000 in earlier rounds and $8,000 to $12,000 later.
- **Scale:** about 30 storefronts so far (sf.gov, 13 March 2026).
- **Where:** Union Square, Powell, O'Farrell, the Financial District, the East Cut and Yerba Buena.
- **How to apply:** no 2026 or 2027 cohort window is published, and intake appears rolling *(inference)*. The application
  is a short form and a business plan, then 30 to 45 days of review and an interview, through vibrantsf.org.
- **What doesn't fit:** the Storefront Opportunity Grant ($50k to $100k) and the $25M Downtown Business Fund (up to
  $500k) want leases of 3 years or more. They suit the flagship, not the pop-up.

## What it proves for the raise

What investors and a lender will ask for, measured over five months:

| Measure | Target | Why it matters for the flagship |
|---|---|---|
| Tropos pairs sold in the room | 6 or more | The model's biggest lever: one pair a month is worth about $119,000 a year of EBITDA |
| Lamps sold; demo bookings that turn into a sale | 100 lamps; 1 in 5 demos | The shop wall's real conversion, which no public precedent gives |
| Sessions sold, and the waiting list | 70% or more full; 500 or more waiting | Whether the listening room earns, as Eavesdrop's reserved slots do |
| Spend a guest on catered nights | $35 or more | The P&L's bar line, measured, not assumed |
| Sound at the property line | Within ambient + 8 dBA | Proves the room can play in a city block |
| Emails and press | 3,000 names; FOG-week coverage | The community round's first investors (Rikki's raised $425k from 80+) |
