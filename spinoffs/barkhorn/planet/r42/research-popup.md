# Pop-up in downtown San Francisco: rules research

Researched 31 October 2026 from public web pages only. Nobody was contacted, no forms were filled in, and no logins were used. This is a research summary, not legal advice. Anything marked "(inference)" is my reading of the sources, not something a source states. Confirm every alcohol point with ABC's San Francisco District Office before money is committed.

The case: a 3 to 6 month hi-fi listening room and design shop downtown (speakers and lamps), with drinks on regular evenings, debuting during FOG week in January 2027.

## 1. Vacant to Vibrant and related programmes

**What it is.** Vacant to Vibrant launched in 2023 under Mayor Breed. The nonprofit SF New Deal runs it with the Mayor's Office and OEWD ([Local News Matters, 16 Mar 2026](https://localnewsmatters.org/2026/03/16/bcn-22-sf-pop-ups-march-15-2026/)). It now sits under Mayor Lurie's Heart of the City plan ([sf.gov, 5 Dec 2025](https://www.sf.gov/news-mayor-lurie-celebrates-launch-of-off-the-grids-new-vacant-to-vibrant-holiday-pop-up-in-union-square-exceeding-heart-of-the-city-goal)).

**Terms (the latest public figures):**
- **Rent.** Three months of free rent ([SF Standard, 16 Apr 2026](https://sfstandard.com/2026/04/16/downtown-san-francisco-empty-storefront-grants/)). Local News Matters says "up to six months" of rent is paid ([16 Mar 2026](https://localnewsmatters.org/2026/03/16/bcn-22-sf-pop-ups-march-15-2026/)). The standard term looks like 3 months with an option to extend by 3 more (inference, from both reports and the 2024 extensions reported by [sf.gov, 18 Jan 2024](https://www.sf.gov/news/san-franciscos-vacant-vibrant-success-existing-pop-ups-extending-leases-program-expands-fill)).
- **Grant.** $3,000 to $8,000 at launch ([SF Standard, 17 Apr 2023](https://sfstandard.com/2023/04/17/sf-will-give-you-thousands-to-open-pop-ups-in-empty-downtown-storefronts-heres-how-to-apply)). $8,000 to $12,000 for the second cohort ([KQED, May 2024](https://www.kqed.org/news/11985684/sf-wants-to-give-downtown-a-boost-with-more-pop-ups-in-empty-storefronts)). No 2026 amount is published on sf.gov or vibrantsf.org.
- **Pop-up to Permanent track.** Up to $50,000 in grants plus help negotiating the lease. Only a JPMorganChase-sponsored [Axios piece](https://www.axios.com/sponsored/inside-the-pop-up-model-thats-reviving-downtown-san-francisco) states this, so treat it as unconfirmed.
- **Where.** Downtown: Union Square, Powell Street, O'Farrell Street, the Financial District, the East Cut and Yerba Buena ([sf.gov, 21 Apr 2025](https://www.sf.gov/news-mayor-lurie-announces-new-retail-pop-ups-to-continue-san-franciscos-downtown-revitalization); [sf.gov, 13 Mar 2026](https://www.sf.gov/news-mayor-lurie-welcomes-four-new-small-businesses-to-union-square-and-downtown)). The 2025 release planned 4 to 6 storefronts on and around Powell Street and new openings "on a monthly basis".
- **Scale.** 30 storefronts activated since 2023, and more than half of eligible pop-ups have signed long-term leases ([sf.gov, 13 Mar 2026](https://www.sf.gov/news-mayor-lurie-welcomes-four-new-small-businesses-to-union-square-and-downtown)). The programme site, undated, says 47 pop-ups, 35 storefronts and 57% signing long-term leases ([vibrantsf.org](https://www.vibrantsf.org/)).
- **How businesses are chosen.** The [vibrantsf.org small-business page](https://www.vibrantsf.org/for-small-businesses) (undated, checked 31 Oct 2026) describes the process:
  - The online application takes up to 60 minutes and needs a business plan.
  - Review takes 30 to 45 days, then shortlisted applicants are interviewed.
  - The ideal applicant has 1 to 2 years of bricks-and-mortar trading or 3 to 4 years of pop-up experience, a local or online following, and "a clear vision" for downtown.
  - Property owners apply separately.
- **Application window and cohorts.** No dated window or 2026/2027 cohort call appears on any public page I found. The site shows an "Apply now!" form with no deadline ([vibrantsf.org](https://www.vibrantsf.org/)). The last dated window I found closed on 23 Feb 2024 ([sf.gov, 18 Jan 2024](https://www.sf.gov/news/san-franciscos-vacant-vibrant-success-existing-pop-ups-extending-leases-program-expands-fill)). Since 2025, intake appears to be rolling (inference). sf.gov's old programme URL returns 404.
- **Fit for us.** Applying today with a 30 to 45 day review would produce a decision in mid-December at the earliest. A storefront handover in time for 20 January is therefore possible but not something to plan on (inference). The programme gives no help with alcohol licensing (inference: no source mentions it).

## 2. Drinks

**Thresholds.** A drink under 0.5% ABV is not an "alcoholic beverage" ([B&P §23004](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_bus_and_prof_code_section_23004)). Zero-proof drinks therefore need no ABC licence. They still need a health permit if they are served open (inference; see the DPH note under zero-proof below).

### 2a. Type 58 catering by a partner bar or restaurant (the realistic route)
- **Who may hold it.** Holders of on-sale licences Types 41, 42, 47, 48 and others ([ABC, Caterer's Permit](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/license-forms/caterers-permit)). A Type 41 or 42 holder can cater only beer and wine; a 47 or 48 can cater spirits too (inference from the [licence-type definitions](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/license-types/)).
- **Each event needs an authorization:**
  - File online 5 to 90 days ahead, or on paper (ABC-218) at the district office at least 3 days ahead.
  - One application can cover up to 3 consecutive days.
  - ABC may require property-owner approval, an ABC-253 diagram and law-enforcement (SFPD) approval ([ABC, Apply for a Type 58 Catering Authorization](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/license-forms/caterers-permit/apply-for-a-type-58-catering-authorization)).
  - The regulation itself says at least 3 days ahead unless waived ([4 CCR §60.5 amendment text](https://www.abc.ca.gov/law-and-policy/regulations-rulemaking/approved-regulations/caterers-permit-amendments/text-of-regulation-amendments)).
- **Limit.** 36 catered events per year at any one premises, raised from 24. ABC can allow more "to satisfy substantial public demand" ([4 CCR §60.5](https://www.abc.ca.gov/law-and-policy/regulations-rulemaking/approved-regulations/caterers-permit-amendments/text-of-regulation-amendments); [ABC](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/license-forms/caterers-permit)). The limit attaches to the premises, so two caterers cannot double it (inference). It counts per calendar year, so nights in Nov to Dec 2026 and in 2027 count separately (inference).
- **Cost.**
  - Per authorization, per day: $100 for 0 to 999 people, $325 for 1,000 to 4,999 ([ABC fee schedule, eff. 1 Jan 2026](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/license-fees/application-fee-schedules)).
  - The caterer's annual Type 58 permit: $300 ([ABC 2027 annual schedule](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/license-fees/upcoming-license-fees/annual-fee-schedule/)).
- **Recurring nights.** Nothing in the rule text bars repeat events at the same premises below the cap ([4 CCR §60.5](https://www.abc.ca.gov/law-and-policy/regulations-rulemaking/approved-regulations/caterers-permit-amendments/text-of-regulation-amendments)). So 1 night a week for 26 weeks, or 2 nights a week for 18 weeks, fits within 36 (inference).
- **Who sells.** The bar sells and keeps the alcohol money. The shop must not take a cut of drink sales, because that could make it an unlicensed seller or give it an interest in the licence (inference: ask ABC). A flat venue fee is the usual structure (inference).

### 2b. Daily licences (form ABC-221)
- **Nonprofits only.** A general daily licence goes to charitable, civic, fraternal (5 or more years old), religious or political organizations. A beer-and-wine daily licence goes to an existing nonprofit ([ABC, Daily Licenses](https://www.abc.ca.gov/?p=4002); [ABC-221 instructions, rev. 09/23](https://www.abc.ca.gov/abc-221-instructions)).
- **Cost.** $50 a day for beer and wine, $75 for general, $100 for special daily ([ABC fee schedule](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/license-fees/application-fee-schedules)).
- **Lead time.** File 10 to 30 days ahead.
- **Yearly limit.** [B&P §24045.1](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/business-and-professions-code/bpc-sect-24045-1/) lets ABC cap uses per calendar year, but I could not find the cap in a public source.
- **Fit.** A daily licence works only for a real nonprofit's own fundraiser held at the shop, such as a design or arts nonprofit, and not for the shop's own nights (inference).

### 2c. "Event permits" for for-profit businesses
- I found no ABC event permit that lets an unlicensed for-profit business serve alcohol itself.
- The invitation-only supplier event under [B&P §25600.5](https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_25600.5) is the nearest thing. It sunsets 1 Jan 2028.
  - Only a manufacturer or winegrower may host it.
  - It must be held at a caterer-authorized location or a licensed hotel.
  - Limits: 8 events a year at one location, 4 hours each, 600 guests at most, all free to guests.
  - The permit costs $200 and must be filed 30 days ahead. ABC's schedule lists $250 ([ABC fee schedule](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/license-fees/application-fee-schedules)).
- **Fit.** A spirits or wine brand could host a launch night at the shop if a partner caterer holds the authorization for that night (inference).

### 2d. Interim and temporary permits
- **Interim operating permit, [B&P §24044.5](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_bus_and_prof_code_section_24044.5):**
  - Only for an applicant whose new licence has been protested and that ABC has decided to grant.
  - Lasts 120 days and can be extended. ABC's fee is $180 ([ABC](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/license-fees/application-fee-schedules)).
  - No help to us.
- **Temporary permit (ABC-280):**
  - Only in a person-to-person transfer, on premises licensed within the last 30 days. Lasts 120 days ([ABC, Surrender License](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/surrender-license/); [ABC-217 instructions](https://abc.ca.gov/form/ABC-217-instr)).
  - It would help only if the pop-up took over a just-closed bar and bought its licence (inference). That is not this case.
- "Type 62" and "Type 63" are not interim permits. They cover vessels and hospitals ([ABC licence types](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/license-types/)).

### 2e. Our own beer-and-wine licence
- **Type 41.** Needs a bona fide eating place with "actual and substantial" meal sales ([ABC](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/license-types/)). Not a speaker shop.
- **Type 42.** A beer-and-wine bar, on-sale; minors may not enter ([ABC](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/license-types/)). It would make the shop 21-plus.
- **Type 20.** Off-sale beer and wine, so bottles only.
- **Cost.** $1,135 application for Types 20, 41 and 42 ([ABC, eff. 1 Jan 2026](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/license-fees/application-fee-schedules)). 2027 annual fees: $515 for Type 20, $580 for Type 41, $730 for Type 42 ([ABC](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/license-fees/upcoming-license-fees/annual-fee-schedule/)).
- **Time.**
  - Commonly about 90 days for an original licence ([LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/type-41-liquor-license-requirements-in-california/), undated). Up to about 175 days if protested ([permitplace](https://permitplace.com/abc-liquor-license-pillar/), undated). These are consultant figures, not ABC's.
  - If the census tract is overconcentrated, a "public convenience or necessity" finding is needed. The Board of Supervisors has 90 days to act, after Planning and SFPD review ([SF BOS fact sheet, rev. 10 May 2018](https://sfbos.org/sites/default/files/PC_or_N_Request_Fact_Sheet.pdf)).
  - Downtown tracts are commonly overconcentrated (inference).
- **Tastings with a Type 20.** An instructional tasting licence (Type 86) lets a supplier pour free tastings ([ABC advisory, 2010](https://www.abc.ca.gov/instructional-tasting-license-for-off-sale-licensees/)). Limits: one beverage type per event, 10 am to 9 pm, a separated area, no charge.
- **Verdict.** Realistically 4 to 7 months, which eats most of a 3 to 6 month lease, and buys a licence that dies with the lease (inference).

### 2f. Zero-proof, or "free" drinks with a brand partner
- **Zero-proof.** No ABC licence is needed (§23004 above). Sealed cans in a small store may fall in DPH's "retail market without prep" class, with the fee waived from 2026 under 5,001 sq ft ([SF Treasurer FY26-27 rate table](https://www.sftreasurer.org/sites/default/files/2026-03/FY26-27%20Unified%20License%20Rate%20Table%20FINAL%2003172026_0.pdf); mapping is inference). Mixing drinks to order is food preparation and needs a DPH permit (inference).
- **Free alcohol.**
  - The ABC Act bars any "premium, gift, or free goods" in connection with alcohol sales (§25600).
  - An ABC appeals decision rejected a "no purchase required" argument for grand-opening gift bags ([ABC Appeals Board, AB-9537](https://abcab.ca.gov/decisions/ab-9000-to-9999/pdf/ab-9537-issued-decision.pdf)).
  - A shop pouring "complimentary" wine to customers, or including it in a ticket, is in practice selling without a licence (inference).
- **Tied-house rules.** These govern dealings between licensed tiers. An unlicensed speaker shop is not a retail licensee, but a supplier still cannot pour for the public there except through a permitted route: a §25600.5 event at a caterer-authorized location, or a Type 86 tasting at a Type 20 store (inference).

### 2g. San Francisco entertainment permits
- **Recorded music, no performer, indoors.** No permit needed. The Entertainment Commission's indoor-event page says amplified sound only (music from a device, speeches, films), entirely indoors, needs no permit ([sf.gov, Get an entertainment permit for your indoor event](https://www.sf.gov/get-entertainment-permit-your-indoor-event)).
- **A DJ, band or live performer.** Needs a One Time Indoor Event permit:
  - $586 (FY 2026-27), amendments $403.
  - Apply at least 2 weeks ahead; the Commission can refuse a filing made under 7 days ahead.
  - Limit: 12 days per premises per calendar year ([sf.gov fees](https://www.sf.gov/check-your-entertainment-permit-fees); [sf.gov indoor](https://www.sf.gov/get-entertainment-permit-your-indoor-event)).
  - A Limited Live Performance permit ($612) covers ongoing low-level live performance ([sf.gov fees](https://www.sf.gov/check-your-entertainment-permit-fees)).
- **Other city permits.** Over 49 people needs a Fire Department Place of Assembly permit, and the special-event supplemental application is due 14 days ahead ([sf.gov, Guide to hosting temporary events and pop-ups](https://sf.gov/guide-hosting-temporary-events-and-pop-ups)).

## 3. Temporary uses in the Planning Code

- **Ordinance 116-25** (File 250540; sponsors Mayor Lurie, Supervisors Engardio, Sauter, Sherrill and Mandelman; approved 17 Jul 2025, effective 17 Aug 2025):
  - Rewrote §§205 to 205.7 to streamline temporary use authorizations.
  - Extended Pop-Up Retail to **up to three years**, authorized and renewed ([ordinance text](https://sfbos.org/sites/default/files/o0116-25.pdf); [codified §205.2 with history](https://gocodebook.com/library/us/ca/san-francisco-planning/article-2-use-districts/sec.-205.-temporary-uses-general/sec.-205.2.-temporary-uses-one-to-three-year-limit)).
- **§205.2(a)(2), Pop-Up Retail:**
  - A time-limited Retail, Nighttime Entertainment, General Entertainment or Arts Activities use.
  - Allowed in a vacant commercial space, or in a space occupied by a legally established Commercial Use.
  - Must keep within the district's principally permitted hours, and stay out of Residential districts.
  - Storefront transparency rules (§145.1) are waived for the first 60 days.
  - Approval is by the Planning Director, with no hearing. Appeal goes to the Board of Appeals within 15 days.
  - Under §205, Formula Retail is barred as a temporary use in Neighborhood Commercial districts ([o116-25](https://sfbos.org/sites/default/files/o0116-25.pdf)).
  - A listening room plus a shop plausibly sits within "Retail / General Entertainment / Arts Activities" (inference). A bar use for the shop itself would need its own analysis (inference).
- **Other limits:**
  - §205.1: 60 days, for exhibitions, festivals, booths and the like.
  - §205.3: 24 hours.
  - §205.4: intermittent activities ([gocodebook §205](https://gocodebook.com/library/us/ca/san-francisco-county-zoning/sec.-205.-temporary-uses-general)).
- **History.** Ord. 285-18 created the Pop-Up Retail framework ([FAQ packet, 2018](https://sfbos.org/sites/default/files/FlexRetailFAQPacket-102518.pdf)). Prop H (2020) allowed non-formula Pop-Up Retail in vacant storefronts ([SF Planning, Prop H](https://sfplanning.org/save-our-small-businesses-initiative)).
- **Fee and time.**
  - Temporary Use Authorization (TUA): **$644** ([SF Planning Fee Schedule, effective 29 Aug 2025, updated 15 Jun 2026](https://sfplanning.org/sites/default/files/forms/Fee_Schedule.pdf)). The online calculator shows $660, probably the FY26-27 rate ([feecalculator.sfplanning.org](https://feecalculator.sfplanning.org/)).
  - Review is usually within 2 weeks of a complete application. Email cpc.intake@sfgov.org ([SF Planning TUA Supplemental, upd. 10 May 2026](https://sfplanning.org/resource/tua-supplemental)).
  - The property owner, or the host as the owner's agent, applies ([sf.gov guide](https://sf.gov/guide-hosting-temporary-events-and-pop-ups)). The sf.gov guide still says 60 days for pop-ups; that is out of date after Ord. 116-25 (inference).
- **Change of use.** A TUA stands in for a change-of-use authorization from Planning. It does not waive Building, Health or Fire permits ([o116-25 §205(f)](https://sfbos.org/sites/default/files/o0116-25.pdf)). If the space was last retail and we sell retail, no TUA may be needed at all ("you don't need one if the space already allows the planned activity", [sf.gov guide](https://sf.gov/guide-hosting-temporary-events-and-pop-ups)).
- "PermitSF" and a 2023 "Small Business Recovery Act" turned up nothing specific to pop-ups. The 2020 Prop H is titled the "Small Business Recovery Act" ([SF Planning](https://sfplanning.org/save-our-small-businesses-initiative)).

## Recommended route

Combine three things:
1. A TUA for Pop-Up Retail ($644, about 2 weeks), or no TUA if the last use was retail.
2. A downtown partner bar or restaurant holding a Type 47 or 48 licence plus a Type 58 permit, catering up to 36 nights in 2027. At one night a week, that covers the whole 6 months. The fee is $100 per night, paid by the partner.
3. Zero-proof drinks on the other nights.

Keep the music recorded, so no Entertainment Commission permit is needed. Do not start our own licence unless the pop-up becomes permanent.
