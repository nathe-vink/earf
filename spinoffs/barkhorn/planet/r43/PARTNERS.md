## 1. How catering authorization works

### The rules

| Rule | What the source says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **What a Type 58 is** | A caterer's permit lets an on-sale licensee sell at "conventions, sporting events, trade exhibits, picnics, social gatherings, or similar events" away from its own premises. "Each permit type requires the licensee to obtain authorization from [ABC] for each catered event." | [B&P §23399(a)](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_bus_and_prof_code_section_23399) (leginfo.ca.gov returned 403, so this is a mirror); [ABC, Caterer's Permit](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/license-forms/caterers-permit) |
| **Who can hold one** | "Only licensees holding an on-sale beer and wine (Type 41 or 42), on-sale general (Type 47, 48, or 57), club (Type 50, 51, or 52), … museum (Type 78), general caterer's (Type 83), or designated special use (Type 71, 72, 73, 87, 88, or 99) license may apply." Seasonal and boat licences are excluded. A 41 or 42 caters beer and wine only. | [ABC, Caterer's Permit](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/license-forms/caterers-permit) |
| **Where the caterer may go** | Types 41, 42, 47, 48, 57, 71–73, 78, 83, 87, 88 and 99 may cater statewide. Clubs (50–52) only on their own premises, and those events are not open to the public. | [ABC, Apply for a Type 58 Catering Authorization](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/license-forms/caterers-permit/apply-for-a-type-58-catering-authorization) |
| **Per-event form** | Online, through ABC Online Services, by the licensee's License Administrator. On paper, form **ABC-218** at the district office. | same page |
| **Lead time, online** | "at least five days before the event, but not more than 90 days before the event." | same page |
| **Lead time, conflicting** | ABC's online-application PDF (v1.1, July 2024) says "more than five days but not more than **60** days". ABC's 2024 launch notice also says 60. The live web page says 90. Plan on 60 to be safe. | [ABC instructions PDF](https://abc.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/online_catering_authorization_applications.pdf); [ABC news, 5 Feb 2024](https://www.abc.ca.gov/abc-license-administrators-can-now-submit-catering-authorization-applications-online/) |
| **Lead time, paper and the regulation** | ABC-218 goes to the district office "at least three days before the event". The regulation: "at least three days in advance … unless waived for good cause." Within 3 to 5 days, call the district office. | ABC apply page; [4 CCR §60.5(1)](https://www.abc.ca.gov/law-and-policy/regulations-rulemaking/approved-regulations/caterers-permit-amendments/text-of-regulation-amendments) |
| **Fee per event** | **$100 a day** for 0 to 999 people ($325 for 1,000 to 4,999; $1,000 for 5,000+), effective 1 Jan 2026. It is charged "for each day of the event that alcohol is being sold." Cards carry a non-refundable 2.99% convenience fee. No refund on or after the event day. | [ABC fee schedule](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/license-fees/application-fee-schedules); §23399(c); instructions PDF |
| **Caterer's annual permit** | $300 a year (ABC's 2027 schedule, which the partner already pays) | [ABC annual fee schedule](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/license-fees/upcoming-license-fees/annual-fee-schedule/) |
| **Multi-day** | Up to 3 consecutive days go on one application if attendance and hours are the same each day. The fee is still charged per day. An event that ends by 2 a.m. counts as one day. | ABC apply page; instructions PDF |
| **Cap** | "a limit of **36 catered events per year at any given location**." The cap applies per calendar year (the form asks how many events "this calendar year"). ABC can allow more if the licensee shows they are "necessary to satisfy substantial public demand". | §23399(f); 4 CCR §60.5(4); [ABC, Caterer's Permit](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/license-forms/caterers-permit) |
| **Cap: the open point** | The statute and the regulation count the cap per premises. The online form asks how many events "*you* have catered at this event location". So it is unclear whether a second caterer starts a fresh count. Assume it does not *(inference)*. | instructions PDF p. 8 |
| **Police sign-off** | The statute makes each authorization "subject to local law enforcement approval" (§23399(c)). ABC says an event "may require operating conditions, Property Owner approval, Law Enforcement Agency approval" and a diagram. "If ABC does not receive all required approvals, the application may be denied." Approval is not automatic. | §23399(c); ABC apply page |
| **SFPD in practice** | SF's outdoor-event page: for-profit organizers "must hire a company that already has a Caterer's Liquor Permit (Type 58)". The security plan and liquor application go to SFPD "at least 3 months before your event". SF's 2019 alcohol guidance: work first with the Permit Officer at the police district station for the address, then file with ABC (quoted in search results; the PDF timed out twice). Both are written for **outdoor** special events. For an indoor shop, ABC decides whether SFPD sign-off is needed. Assume it is *(inference)*. Union Square, the FiDi and Jackson Square fall under Central Station *(inference)*. | [sf.gov, Serve alcohol at your outdoor special event](https://www.sf.gov/serve-alcohol-at-your-outdoor-special-event) (undated); [SF Entertainment Commission, Alcohol Management and Licensing, updated 25 Jan 2019](https://sfgov.org/entertainment/sites/default/files/Alcohol%20Management%20and%20Licensing%20-%20Last%20updated%20012519.pdf) |
| **What the site and host must provide** | ABC must pre-approve the location. Zoning and nearness to schools, churches and government facilities can count against it. ABC may ask for the property owner's approval and an **ABC-253** diagram. The printed approval and any signed conditions must be at the event. Food is required only near listed institutions. The form names the **sponsoring organization** that hired the caterer (EARF). | ABC apply page; instructions PDF p. 6 |
| **Stock** | Wholesalers may deliver to the site. "You may not store distilled spirits at the event site." Spirits go back to the licensed premises after each event. | ABC apply page |
| **Hours** | ABC sets no special catering hours. The state rule applies: no sale, service or drinking 2 a.m. to 6 a.m. | instructions PDF p. 5; [B&P §25631](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_bus_and_prof_code_section_25631) |
| **Open to the public** | Allowed. The online form asks "whether or not the event is open to the public". Only club-licence events must be closed to the public. | instructions PDF p. 7; ABC apply page |
| **Minors** | They may attend unless the site normally bars them. They may not buy, have or drink alcohol. | ABC apply page |
| **Ticketed** | No ABC catering page mentions tickets. A "sale" covers any transaction where title to alcohol passes "for any consideration". Only a licensee may do what a licence allows. So the caterer must take the drink money. A ticket that includes drinks and is sold by EARF would make EARF a seller *(inference)*. | [B&P §23025](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_bus_and_prof_code_section_23025); [B&P §23300](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_bus_and_prof_code_section_23300) |
| **Compliance** | A violation at the event counts against the caterer's own licence "as if it occurred on the licensed premises". | §23399(d); ABC apply page |

### Corrections to `r42/POPUP.md` ("The drinks")

1. **The lead time is not a firm "5 to 90 days".** ABC's own online instructions and its 2024 launch notice say 60 days. Plan on filing **6 to 60 days ahead**. Paper filing (ABC-218) can go as late as 3 days before.
2. **Eligible partners are not just "47 or 48".** Types 41, 42, 57, 78, 83 and others also qualify, but a 41 or 42 pours beer and wine only. This matters for Bar Part Time below.
3. **SFPD question (Q2): partly answered.** The statute makes every authorization "subject to local law enforcement approval". SF's guidance sends organizers to the district station's Permit Officer first, 3 months ahead for outdoor events. Treat SFPD sign-off as required.
4. **Q1 (do regular public nights count as events?): partly answered.** Public events are allowed, and nothing bars repeat nights below 36. ABC still pre-approves the location and can refuse it.
5. **Missing from POPUP:** spirits may not be stored at the shop between nights. ABC may require the landlord's approval and an ABC-253 diagram. The approval must be printed and kept on site. Drinks money goes to the caterer only, so a ticket must not include alcohol unless the caterer sells it.
6. **§25600.5:** the "8 a year per location, 4 hours, free" is right. Add these: a supplier must hold it, not EARF; invitations only; 30 days' notice; the section expires 1 Jan 2028.
7. **"$100 a night, 36 a year": correct** (ABC fee schedule effective 1 Jan 2026; §23399(f)).

### Fee model

- **EARF charges the partner a flat venue fee a night and takes no share of drink sales.** The partner sells, keeps the bar takings and runs its own staff, glassware and stock.
- **The rules behind it.** Only a licensee may exercise a licensee's privileges ([§23300](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_bus_and_prof_code_section_23300)). A "sale" is any passing of title to alcohol "for any consideration" ([§23025](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_bus_and_prof_code_section_23025)). Violations at the event count against the caterer's licence (§23399(d)). I found **no** ABC page that addresses a venue's percentage of a caterer's alcohol sales. A percentage tied to drinks could look like EARF sharing in the sale or holding an undisclosed interest in the licence; a flat fee avoids that *(inference)*.
- **Tied-house.** §§25500–25600 govern dealings between suppliers and retailers. EARF is neither, so they do not bind EARF directly. They apply as soon as a spirits or wine brand pays for or supplies a night, so route brand money through a §25600.5 permit or keep it out *(inference)*. Elixir's closed case under §§25500(a)(2), 25600 and 25504 is a reminder ([ABC Lookup](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/license-lookup/single-license/?RPTTYPE=12&LICENSE=405553)).
- **Tickets.** Sell entry or listening sessions only. Drinks are bought at the partner's bar, or a drinks voucher is sold by the partner on its own till *(inference from §23025)*.
- **Three questions for ABC's SF office** (replacing POPUP's): (1) Will ABC pre-approve an unlicensed retail shop for 11 public nights over 8 weeks? (2) Is SFPD sign-off needed for an indoor event, and from which station? (3) Can a flat venue fee be paid by the caterer to the host, and must it be disclosed on the application?
