# Planet EARF, round 44: four concepts for the bar's architecture

The owner, 31 October 2026: "I mostly want to nail design, make it realistic, make it cooler, etc. same with the bar. Make it well architected, novel, lasting."

These four boards went to a blind critic, who picked one (its verdict is in `critic/LOG.md`).

## Shared ground

**The shell.** All four sit in the same shell: a typical San Francisco ground-floor storefront.
- 25 ft wide and 80 ft deep (2,000 sq ft gross).
- 14 ft to the underside of a wood-joisted floor, with apartments above.
- Brick or concrete party walls, a slab on grade, a glazed front and a rear door to a yard.

**The brief.**
- A shop at the street.
- A bar for about 40.
- A listening room of about 24 seats around one pair of large floorstanding speakers (the Tropos, 0.56 x 0.43 x 1.29 m on a 0.40 m plinth).
- Mother EARF, a 1.25 m globe of six slotted, rim-lit tinted acrylic discs, on a low plinth. It cannot hang: it weighs about 200 kg.
- Two single-user restrooms; back of house.
- About 100 occupants in all.

**Four rules apply to every board.**
- Acrylic is for light and display only, never a wear surface (it scratches).
- The listening room is isolated from the apartments above.
- The noisy bar kit (the ice machine and the glasswasher) stays out of the listening zone.
- The ADA route reaches every public area.

---

## (a) The strata room

**The idea.** The listening room's floor rises in four curved terraces around the speakers, like the contour lines of a map or a small amphitheatre.
- **The focus.** Mother EARF stands on its plinth at the focus, between the speakers.
- **The terraces.** They are oak-faced timber boxes filled with mineral wool. Their riser faces are slotted, so the terraces themselves are the room's bass traps: a 1.2 m deep volume of absorber under the seats.
- **The light.** A thin lit line runs under each nosing (an LED behind a vertical acrylic strip, never trodden on), so the contours glow faintly in the dark.
- **The sweet spot.** The lowest terrace, at floor level, holds the sweet-spot seats. The accessible wheelchair space and its companion seat are the best seats in the room.

**Plan.** Shop, then bar, then a thick service band, then the strata room at the back.
- The service band holds the bar back with the ice machine and glasswasher, two restrooms and a sound-lock vestibule. It is the acoustic buffer.

**Section.**
- Terraces at 0, 0.30, 0.60 and 0.90 m.
- An isolated ceiling at 12 ft, leaving 2.7 m of headroom on the top tier.

**Risks.**
- Steps in a dark room need nosings, handrails and aisle lights.
- The terraces are fixed joinery, so they are lost if the lease ends.
- About 24 seats are fewer than loose chairs would give.

## (b) The room as a kit

**The idea.** The whole fit-out is a demountable system, so it outlasts any one lease. It moves from a 3-month Vacant to Vibrant pop-up to the permanent site, and on again.
- **The frame.** A bolted steel or glulam frame on a 1.2 m grid.
- **Infill panels** clip into it: oak acoustic slats, wool felt, lit acrylic panels and shelving.
- **The modules.** The bar, the shop wall and the listening room's walls are bolted modules.
- **The listening room.** A modular room-in-room on isolation pads, like a recording booth scaled up.
- **The move.** Everything packs into two 20 ft containers.

**Plan.** Shop, then bar, then the booth-room. The modules re-plan for any shell from about 18 to 35 ft wide.

**Risks.**
- A kit looks like a kit: exhibition-stand, not architecture.
- Modular isolation is heavy and weaker at bass than a built room-in-room.
- The grid fights each shell's quirks.
- It costs more than built-in for the first site and pays back only on the second.

## (c) Offcut terrazzo

**The idea.** The products' acrylic offcuts, crushed, become the aggregate in a poured terrazzo floor and bar top, so the room is made from the making.
- **The look.** A dark ground with the eight product tints scattered through it like a star field, deepest at the shop and thinning toward the listening room.
- **The rest.** Walls in lime plaster, joinery in oak, and the listening room a conventional room-in-room with loose chairs.

**Risks.**
- Acrylic aggregate is as soft as the epoxy around it. Marble or glass chips usually take the wear, so a floor with mostly acrylic chips would haze and scratch and need re-honing.
- A bar top would scratch under glasses.
- Volume: about 0.6 m³ of chips (about 700 kg) for the floor, far more offcut than early production makes.
- Workable as a 10 to 15% accent in a marble and glass terrazzo, but then it is a finish, not a scheme.

## (d) The lantern

**The idea.** The isolation the listening room needs anyway, a room within the room, becomes the building's one object.
- **The box.** A freestanding oak box sits inside the shell on isolation mounts, as a loudspeaker cabinet sits in a room.
- **Its front.** A slotted oak baffle with a heavy door, facing the bar.
- **Its top band.** A 450 mm band of lit tinted acrylic fins runs between the inner and outer leaves, so from the bar the box reads as a glowing lantern and the street sees it at the end of the room.
- **Inside.** A calm, dark, tuned room with loose chairs and Mother EARF on its plinth between the speakers.
- **The bar** runs along one side toward it. The shop's walls hold the products.

**Risks.**
- The box takes width: 25 ft less two leaves and a service corridor leaves a 5.5 m wide room.
- The lit band must not leak sound (the acrylic sits outside the seal).
- "A box in a room" is a known move (galleries, studios). It is novel here only in its light.
