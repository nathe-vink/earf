Hello,

I'm starting a small lighting line called EARF, and I'd like a quote for the glass-like ball of a table lamp called
Core. The ball is 160 mm across with a 9 mm wall, in tinted cast acrylic. It is made as two hemispheres formed from
10 mm sheet on one mould, joined at the equator, and the joint turned and polished.

The inside is bead-blasted to a fine, even frost. The outside is glossy all over. The joint should be as close to
invisible as you can make it: we'll judge it on the first sample. A 40 mm hole is cut at one pole, where it sits on
its base. We bond an aluminium lining into it.

**The parts, per ball:**

| Part | Material | Size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Two hemispheres | 10 mm cast acrylic, tinted through | 160 OD, 9 wall | formed on one aluminium mould |
| The joint | a polymerising cement (Acrifix 192 or Weld-On 40 kind) | equator | turned and polished to the sphere |
| Inside | bead-blasted | full inner surface | even frost, done before joining |
| Port | cut | 40 diameter at the bottom pole | edge smooth; we bond a lining in |

**Quantities:** balls for 1, 10 and 50 lamps, each order in one tint (likely Ember and Smoke first). Spare balls are
sold on their own, so a later reorder in the other six tints is likely.

**Please tell us:**
1. Unit price per finished ball at 1, 10 and 50.
2. Tooling: the hemisphere mould, and any jigs for bonding and turning the joint. Who owns them.
3. Minimum order, and the minimum per tint.
4. Lead time for the mould, for a first article, and for each quantity.
5. Sheet: can you source 10 mm cast sheet in our tints, or should we supply it? Dye-lot control (one lot per order),
   and any minimum on a custom tint.
6. Tint matching: will you make a test chip or a test half from the lot for us to approve before the run?
7. How you'd make the joint disappear: bond line, annealing, turning and polishing. What a customer will see, and at
   what distance.
8. The frost: blast medium and grit, and whether it stays even near the joint.
9. Roundness and wall tolerance you can hold.
10. Would you rather blow-form a one-piece ball and cut the port? If so, price that too.

We're not ready to order yet. This is to plan a first run. Drawings and STEP are attached.
