# EARF special editions

Status: **proposal, 2026-10-31**. Nothing here is signed by the owner. Edition sizes and premiums are proposals.
Prices stay relative because the standard products have no published price.

The owner, 2026-10-31: "Also it would be sick to do some special edition with things cast in the acrylic, like the miss
blanche chair, but not necessarily roses. Could also be cool to have some with bubbles."

**How we got here.** Three research reports, each claim cited with a URL and its access date:
- `research/prior-art.md`: Kuramata, Pesce, Hollis Jones, Lucite embedments, DRIFT, Marcelis, designed bubbles.
- `research/making.md`: how embedment and lamination are done, plus voids, annealing and heat.
- `research/sourcing.md`: specimens and artifacts, and the law in the US and California.

From those, `CONCEPTS.md` set out 18 editions in three directions. Three judges scored every edition, each without
seeing the others' scores (`judges/*.json`, summed up in `judges/README.md`):
- a collectible-design gallerist;
- EARF's brand director;
- a master acrylic fabricator.

Six editions were kept, and their designs changed where the fabricator's notes asked for it.

## The rule the editions follow

Any technique is fair: embedment, lamination, trapped air, laser points. No one owns those. What EARF must not do is
pair someone's signature subject with their signature form:
- flowers floating in a clear chair on thin legs (Kuramata, Miss Blanche, 1988; Phillips sold one for $516,600 on 7
  June 2022);
- feathers in a stool (Kuramata);
- seed heads on light (DRIFT);
- soft gradient resin cubes (Marcelis);
- a business object in a Lucite "tombstone".

What goes inside comes from EARF's own world: the earth's layers, sound, light, the factory. The editions avoid the
keepsake under clear: a pebble, a pressed plant, a burl, a drawing. All three judges scored those 3 to 4. (Sources are in
`research/prior-art.md`.)

## What the physics and the making allow

**Thick tints go dark.** A&C's TruCast chart gives the transmission of 1/8 in sheet: Smoke (2064) 29%, the light blue
(2069) 58%, the light grey (2515) 69%, the green (2092) 19% and the red (2283) 12% (A&C Plastics, *TruCast Acrylic Light
Transmissions*, https://www.acplasticsinc.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Trucast-Acrylic-Light-Transmissions-5.26.26-1.pdf,
accessed 2026-10-31). Through 40 mm, every one of them is close to black. So every edition body is **clear cast
acrylic**, with the tint in **thin laminae of 2 to 3 mm**, the strata. The object sits in clear, where it can be seen.

**EARF laminates; it does not pour.**
- Each object goes in a pocket milled to its shape and sealed dry.
- The cement goes on the flats only. ACRIFIX 2R 0190 warns that cement must never enter a closed cavity.
- Every void has a 0.3 mm square step at its rim. This is a capillary stop, so cement cannot creep in along a feathered
  edge.
- Every void is vented, flushed for 20 minutes, then plugged.
- Sheets are annealed at 80 °C before bonding. Bonded stacks wait 24 h, then anneal at 70 °C for about 1 h per mm.
- Where there are many voids, the bond is a solvent-assisted press bond instead (ethanol, 70 to 90 °C, 1 to 2 MPa,
  lapped flats).

Sources: `research/making.md`, sections 3 and 4. The fabricator judge added the rim step, the vent and the press bond.

**Air reads silver.** Inside PMMA (n = 1.49), light striking an air void beyond 42.2° reflects totally. The rim of a
round void shines like mercury in every light. A milled pocket's walls cannot be polished invisible, so each design
hides them:
- the walls are seen edge-on;
- a tinted lamina lies over them;
- the floor is made a deliberate ground;
- or the void is the subject, as in the bubble editions.

**Nothing organic goes in a lamp or near an amplifier.** Cast acrylic is rated for 82 °C continuous use. LED boards run
near 85 °C and must sit on metal with an air gap. Magnetic tape starts to erase in a field of 10 to 50 Oe, so no tape
goes near a driver's magnet. (Source: `research/making.md`, section 5.)

### 1. Stamper — "The mirror that pressed it."

- **Product and tint.** Bedrock, the record stand (proposed 300 × 80 × 72 mm, a 7 mm slot along the top for a sleeve),
  in water-clear (Fog). A 3 mm Smoke base stratum was drawn first and dropped in the renders. Seen from above, the light
  crosses the stratum twice, and the whole block read black.
- **Inside.** One sector of the nickel stamper that pressed Session 00: about 100 × 46 mm across its grooves, 0.3 to
  0.4 mm thick. It stands 9 mm behind the front face, grooves forward, the lead-in at the top. The shards come from one
  stamper, so the edition is as large as that stamper is: about 12 to 16 pieces. Each card carries a map of the
  stamper showing where its shard was cut.
- **How it's made.**
  1. After the run, the pressing plant releases the stamper; it is normally scrapped.
  2. Cut the shards by wire EDM or a fine waterjet, so the groove field is neither burred nor bent. Deburr, then clean
     ultrasonically.
  3. Mill the front sheet (9 mm) and the back block. Mill a pocket in the back block's face, shaped to the shard plus
     0.2 mm all round and 0.2 mm deeper than the shard. Give the pocket a 0.3 mm rim step.
  4. Anneal both parts at 80 °C, 1 h per mm.
  5. Set the shard grooves-forward, not touching the cover. Lay degassed ACRIFIX 2R 0190 as a bead on the flats.
     Press, vent, flush the pocket with dry air for 20 minutes, and plug.
  6. Wait 24 h, then anneal at 70 °C.
  7. Mill the slot, then diamond-polish the faces and flame-polish the arrises.
  8. The pocket is shallow and seen face-on, so its walls barely show. A thin silver outline stays round the shard,
     and it reads as the shard's frame.
- **Sourcing, legal and ethical.** The stamper is EARF's own tooling from its own pressing, so its legality is
  straightforward. Ask the plant in writing for the stamper's release and keep the letter. The shards are cut after the
  run, so no record goes unpressed.
- **First tests.**
  1. Cheap nickel sheet engraved with fine grooves, sealed as above. Under a loupe, look for monomer or cement film on
     the grooves, which would kill the diffraction.
  2. Heat-cycle it 20 times from −10 to +50 °C. Nickel and acrylic expand at different rates, so look for cracking at
     the shard's corners.
- **Size and premium.** 12 to 16, by the stamper's yield; +150%. The object pressed the record. There are exactly as
  many as one stamper holds and never more. And it catches light as nothing else in the room does.

### 2. Offcuts — "Our own rock record."

- **Product and tint.** Core in its stacked form, the core sample. Clear discs alternate with one 3 mm stratum of each
  of the eight tints, in order: Ember, Dust, Acid, Poison, Ice, Haze, Fog, Smoke.
- **Inside.** Nothing foreign. The offcuts from the first eight speakers' cabinets, one tint from each. A light base
  stands apart and lights the stack from below.
- **How it's made.**
  1. Fly-cut every disc to 3.00 ± 0.05 mm and pre-anneal at 80 °C.
  2. Bond the whole stack at once with degassed ACRIFIX 2R under even platen pressure.
  3. Wait 24 h, then anneal at 70 °C, about 1 h per mm.
  4. Turn and polish the outside diameter.
  The stack is all one polymer, so nothing outgasses or fades. The fabricator scored it 9: it is what a laminating
  shop already does well.
- **Sourcing.** In-house. Keep each offcut's cabinet number and date.
- **First tests.** A step wedge of the eight tints, 3 mm each, bonded. Measure each stratum's transmission against the
  chart, and check that no bond line shows (this also tests the renders' tint values). Then a bond-line peel test.
- **Size and premium.** 8, one for each first speaker; +70%. Nothing in it costs anything; the value is the founding
  run, never repeated.

### 3. Firn — "Air, kept at every layer." *(rendered)*

- **Product and tint.** Moho, the record weight (proposed ø 80 × 36 mm). A 3 mm clear base, six 5 mm clear layers,
  and a 3 mm Ice lamina on top. An Ice base was tried in the render and dropped after the first critic round. Seen by
  total reflection along the base, it went navy and hid the lowest voids.
- **Inside.** Air. At each of the five inner bond lines and under the top lamina, lens-shaped voids 1.5 to 13 mm
  across and up to 1.4 mm deep. Each comes from two matching dished pockets in the mating faces. The voids stack in
  columns, as snow traps air when it turns to glacier ice. Firn is that snow, half-way to ice, and an ice core's air
  bubbles are the oldest air we have. Each weight holds the air of the day it was bonded, and the card gives the
  date.
  - The name changed from the concept's "Abraham" (a lake's frozen methane bubbles). The brand director: "a
    well-worn photo trope; the name should change before the object does." Firn keeps Ice's snow country and is a
    core sample, as the brand is.
- **How it's made.**
  1. Pre-dry and anneal all eight sheets at 80 °C (the base, six layers, and the Ice lamina).
  2. CNC each void's dish with a polished single-crystal diamond ball-nose at 0.01 to 0.02 mm stepover, so it is
     optical off the tool. Give each dish a 0.3 mm square rim step, the capillary stop. Mirror the dish in the mating
     face.
  3. Bond by solvent-assisted press: ethanol, 70 to 80 °C, 1 to 2 MPa, lapped flats. No flowing cement near the
     voids.
  4. Anneal at 70 °C, ramping slowly. Air at +18% pressure is no threat to voids this small.
  5. Turn the outside, bore the spindle hole (ø 8 × 12 mm) from below, and diamond-polish.
  The fabricator expects one good weight in three at twelve layers, which is why it is six. Halos (Newton's rings) at
  the dish margins are the failure to watch.
- **Sourcing.** None. Air, the sheet and the tooling.
- **First tests.** The void coupon (test 1 below). Then a bubble map for each number, drawn from its own CNC file, so
  no two weights are alike.
- **Size and premium.** 25; +120%. Only a laminator can make it, and it shows its own making. Each void is clean, and
  each number has its own map.

### 4. Groove — "The first second of Session 00."

- **Product and tint.** Moho, clear, with the 3 mm Smoke stratum **beneath** the channel. A dark ground under it makes
  the silver line read. Over it, Smoke would dull the line (the fabricator's note).
- **Inside.** One round air channel, 2 mm across, drawn as a spiral like a record's groove. Its radius swings with
  Session 00's waveform. Each number carries the next slice of the recording, so the whole edition laid out in order
  plays the opening half-minute (the gallerist's note).
- **How it's made.**
  1. Mill a one-sided D-channel in one face with a polished cutter.
  2. Leave a rim step on each side of the channel.
  3. Bond to a flat cover with cement on the flats, and vent the channel once.
  4. Anneal at 70 °C. Polish.
  Because the channel is cut in one face only, the two halves can never fail to line up.
- **Sourcing.** EARF's own recording.
- **First tests.**
  1. A 100 mm spiral coupon: check cement creep along its full length, and photograph it under a dark-field light.
  2. A listening check: render each slice's waveform back to audio and confirm the set plays.
- **Size and premium.** 33, for 33⅓; +150%. Sound made visible in the object that sits on the record, and a set to
  collect. Watch that it never reads as a "soundwave print": it must look like a groove, not a waveform.

### 5. Contour — "Shasta at 2,000 m, drawn in air."

- **Product and tint.** Relief, the lit topographic wall panel, in clear, edge-lit by an LED bar. The bar sits on
  aluminium with an air gap to the acrylic.
- **Inside.** One closed contour of a real mountain, the 2,000 m line of Mount Shasta first, traced as an air channel
  3 mm wide and 1.5 mm deep. Lit from the edge, the channel reflects totally and draws a silver line in the air.
- **How it's made.**
  1. Take the contour from USGS 3DEP elevation data (public domain).
  2. Mill the full D-section in one face with a ball-nose and a polished PCD finishing pass.
  3. Bond against a flat cover: rim step, cement on the flats, one vent to the edge, flushed and plugged.
  4. Anneal and polish.
- **Sourcing.** Public elevation data. The mountains are a series, each its own small edition.
- **First tests.**
  1. A 150 mm contour coupon on the edge-light rig. Measure the channel's brightness against the panel's face.
  2. Check the LED bar's temperature at the acrylic edge: under 60 °C after four hours.
- **Size and premium.** 8 a mountain; +100%. A series, Shasta first.

### 6. Iron — "A core, inside Core." *(rendered)*

- **Product and tint.** Core, the 160 mm ball, in water-clear (Fog). The ball holds no light, no battery and no wire;
  it rests on a separate black anodised base that carries a ring of warm LEDs and lights the ball from below.
- **Inside.** A 5 mm etched slice of the Gibeon iron meteorite (Namibia, a fine octahedrite), about 62 × 44 mm. It
  stands at the centre in a dry pocket 0.5 mm larger all round. Its Widmanstätten pattern is the crystal structure of
  iron-nickel that cooled over millions of years in the core of a broken planetesimal: the nearest thing to a
  planet's core anyone can hold. The ball magnifies it about 1.5 times.
- **How it's made** (the fabricator's construction, which replaces the concept's laminated ball):
  1. Turn two thick cast blocks, each with half the pocket. There is one bond line, at the equator.
  2. Reduce the slice's chloride with an alkaline wash, then dry it, lacquer it, and dry it again. Chloride, held in
     akaganeite, is what makes iron meteorites rust; sealants alone have failed (`research/sourcing.md`).
  3. Hide a molecular-sieve cartridge in the pocket. Bond the hemispheres with cement on an annular flat. Flush the
     pocket with dry nitrogen through a vent, then plug it.
  4. Anneal the ball at 70 °C. At 160 mm through, this is a long cycle, about a week.
  5. Turn the ball round, and polish.
- **Sourcing, legal and ethical.**
  - Buy only material that has been in the trade for years, from a long-standing dealer. The dealer should give the
    official name, the classification and an IMCA number.
  - Gibeon full slices are about $2 to $4 a gram (meteoriteguy.com; meteorites.tv; accessed 2026-10-31), so a 5 mm
    slice of about 100 g is about $200 to $400.
  - Namibia protects Gibeon as heritage. Its export rules are unconfirmed and must be checked before buying.
  - Never buy meteorites found on US federal land: a casual find on BLM land cannot be sold.
- **First tests.**
  1. A cheap Gibeon end piece, treated and sealed in a small clear block with a sieve. Hold it at 40 °C and 85% RH for
     1,000 h and look for rust blooms.
  2. Heat-cycle it 20 times from −10 to +50 °C. The iron expands about six times less than the acrylic, so look for
     cracks at the slice's edges.
  3. A 100 mm ball cut from two blocks, to prove the equator bond is invisible.
- **Size and premium.** 12; +150 to 250%, about $2,500 to $4,000 over a standard Core. It has the best fit of story to
  object in the set, and the material has its own documented market value. The fabricator scored it 4 for rust and the
  long anneal. **Test 2 below decides whether it ships.**

## The first three physical tests

1. **The void coupon** (Firn, Groove, Contour, Breath).
   - Six 100 mm squares of 5 mm clear cast sheet, holding dished lens voids, hemispheres, a D-channel and a spiral.
     Half the voids get the 0.3 mm rim step and half do not.
   - Bond half the squares with ACRIFIX 2R 0190 beads on the flats, and half by ethanol press bond. Vent, flush and
     plug every void.
   - Anneal at 70 °C, then judge under a dark-field light: cement creep, halos, void shape, and bond lines.
   - Cycle 20 times from −10 to +50 °C.
   - This settles the bubble method for three editions. Cost: sheet and machine time.
2. **The metal and meteorite pocket** (Stamper, Iron).
   - Seal an engraved nickel coupon and a Gibeon end piece (about $40 to $100), each chloride-reduced where it applies,
     in dry pockets 0.2 and 0.5 mm oversize, with a sieve and a nitrogen flush.
   - Hold at 40 °C and 85% RH for 1,000 h, with photographs every week. Then heat-cycle.
   - This settles whether Iron can ship and whether the Stamper's grooves stay bright.
3. **The tint step wedge** (Offcuts, and every edition's laminae).
   - Bond eight 3 mm strata of the eight tints into one stack. Measure each stratum's transmission and colour with a
     spectrometer against A&C's chart, and look for bond lines.
   - This settles the Offcuts edition and corrects the tint values in `render/pt.py` from measured sheet.

## Renders

Path-traced in Blender Cycles (`render/`). The physics:
- IOR 1.49.
- Tint absorption from A&C's TruCast chart (`render/pt.py`, `TINTS`), matched to transmission at 1/8 in.
- Air voids as closed inward-facing meshes, so they read silver by total internal reflection.
- Inclusions seen by refraction.
- Caustics path-traced (no MNEE).
- Clear cast PMMA given a faint cyan over a metre.

Iron and Stamper stand in the lamps lane's pass-4 Core house set: paper sweep #EAE4DA, Poly Haven `marble_01` (CC0), the
same camera and lights, and the Khronos PBR Neutral view. Its values were read from `r32-lamps-final`, not changed there.
Firn is bright-field. The CC0 textures (Poly Haven, and ambientCG `Paper005` for the sleeve) are fetched by
`studio/engine/assets.py` into the git-ignored cache.

To render: `python3.11 render/scenes.py -- EDITION SHOT OUT.png [--scale 0.3 --samples 32]` for a proof. The editions are
`iron_core`, `stamper_core` and `firn`. The shots are `core` and `core_macro`, or `hero_b` and `macro_b` for Firn.

What every critic named, and what a next look should change:
- **Clear acrylic shows what is behind it.** On a dark set the body read black; on a bright sweep it merged into the wall.
  The frames that came closest have black flags placed so that each edge refracts a dark line. More are needed, close in
  (Iron: flags at ±170 mm and a black card above and behind).
- **Iron.** The etched slice needs per-band reflectance: each kamacite family should catch the light at its own angle,
  not just differ in tone. The dry pocket's outline should be smoothed (its 90-segment offset prism shows as steps
  through the ball).
- **Firn.** The vinyl needs real grooves (a normal map at 0.1 mm pitch with an anisotropic sheen along them). The void
  cores mirror the blue lamina and read as inlaid blue, so they need a neutral white bounce below to fill them.
- **Stamper.** The mirror needs a black field with one thin strip of light in it. The sleeve needs a sheen falloff.
