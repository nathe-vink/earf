# Special editions: sourcing, legality and stability of embedded materials

Research for EARF special editions with natural specimens and music artifacts cast in tinted acrylic. Compiled 2026-10-31. This is desk research only. Nothing was bought, no forms were filled in, and no dealer was contacted. **This is not legal advice.** Before the first commercial edition ships, a California attorney or the agency named in each section should confirm the rules.

## How to read the citations

Every claim gives a URL and "accessed 2026-10-31". Each claim also carries one of three tags:

- **[O]** The page was opened and read.
- **[S]** The claim comes from a search-engine result summary. The page itself was not opened, either because the fetch failed or because it was not needed.
- **[U]** Unconfirmed. This is my inference from general chemistry or law, or the sources disagree. Check it before relying on it.

## The commercial-use problem

EARF sells its editions, so every specimen it embeds is used commercially. Most "you may keep a reasonable amount" rules on public land cover noncommercial personal use only. BLM's general collecting rule allows "reasonable amounts … for noncommercial purposes". Collecting "for sale or barter to commercial dealers" needs a contract or permit under 43 CFR parts 3600 or 5400. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/43/8365.1-5, accessed 2026-10-31 [O].

In practice:

- **Never field-collect for an edition, even where a hobbyist could legally pick the same rock up.** Buy from a business that holds the permit, the land, or the quarry lease, and keep the invoice.

## What happens to an object in cast acrylic

These general points apply to every material in this report.

- **Monomer.** Cell casting surrounds the object with liquid methyl methacrylate (MMA). MMA is an ester and a solvent. Anything soluble in esters can swell, dissolve or bleed. That includes many lacquers, binders, inks, waxes and plasticised plastics [U].
- **Heat.** Industrial cell casting uses a slow first stage at about 23–30 °C for 16–36 hours. A hotter finishing stage near 105–125 °C follows. Source: https://brevets-patents.ic.gc.ca/opic-cipo/cpd/eng/patent/1089611/summary.html and https://www.scribd.com/document/463536347/PMMA, accessed 2026-10-31 [S]. Any material that melts, cracks or outgases below about 125 °C needs a different method: a lower-temperature cure or a pre-cast pocket.
- **Mitsubishi Chemical's embedment guidance:**
  - Clean and dry objects first to prevent haze.
  - Pre-dip them in MMA monomer to prevent voids.
  - Avoid copper, because it interferes with curing.
  - Colour objects that yellow before embedding.

  Source: https://us.mitsubishi-chemical.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/TDS_ACRYCON_AC_Embedment_2020.pdf, accessed 2026-10-31 [S]. The PDF returned 404 when fetched. Its content came through the search engine.
- **Oxygen** inhibits MMA polymerisation and leaves soft spots. Source: https://www.emsdiasum.com/docstechnicaltechtipsacrylic_resins, accessed 2026-10-31 [S]. Porous objects bring trapped air with them.
- **Water** in organic material turns to vapour under curing heat and makes streams of bubbles. Source: https://incurelab.com/wp/?p=10418, accessed 2026-10-31 [S]. That source is about epoxy, but the physics is the same [U].
- **No hermetic seal.** PMMA takes up a small amount of water over time. Acrylic slows corrosion and fading but does not stop them [U].

Working rule: anything that is wet, oily, copper-bearing, solvent-sensitive or heat-sensitive goes into a **sealed pocket** rather than being cast directly in syrup. The pocket is machined into cured acrylic, the object is placed in it, and a lid is bonded on. A desiccant or inert-gas fill can go in with the object [U].

## 1. Volcanic cinder, lava rock, obsidian, Pele's hair

### Legality

- **National parks.** 36 CFR 2.1(a)(1)(iv) prohibits possessing, removing or disturbing "a mineral resource or cave formation or the parts thereof" in any NPS unit. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/36/2.1 and https://www.nps.gov/articles/recreational-collection-of-rocks-and-minerals-legal-instruments.htm, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
  - The NPS lists only narrow exceptions: gold panning at Whiskeytown under 36 CFR 7.91, and some Alaska parks under 36 CFR 13.35. Same NPS source [S].
  - Hawaiʻi Volcanoes' compendium reportedly names "lava of any description, Pele's hair, Pele's tears, dirt, sand". Source: https://www.hawaii-guide.com/why-you-should-never-take-lava-rocks-from-hawaii, accessed 2026-10-31 [S, secondary; compendium not opened].
  - Reported penalties conflict, from $500 to $5,000. Treat them as unconfirmed [U].
- **Hawaiʻi state land.**
  - HRS 205A-44 bars taking "sand, dead coral, coral rubble, rocks, soil, or other beach deposits" from the shoreline area.
  - DLNR rule HAR 15-150-23 implements it.
  - In 2018 DLNR issued cease-and-desist orders over tons of lava rock removed from the Mauna Loa Forest Reserve.

  Sources: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/hawaii/Haw-Code-R-SS-15-150-23 and https://dlnr.hawaii.gov/blog/2018/05/12/nr18-078/, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
- **California state parks.** 14 CCR 4307 prohibits removing "earth, sand, gravel, oil, minerals, rocks, paleontological features". That covers Lava Beds-adjacent state land and every state park cinder cone. Source: https://regulations.justia.com/states/california/title-14/division-3/chapter-1/section-4307, accessed 2026-10-31 [S]. Lava Beds National Monument itself is NPS land, so 36 CFR 2.1 applies there [U].
- **BLM land.** Casual noncommercial collection is allowed (43 CFR 8365.1-5). Commercial collection needs a permit (see above).
  - Glass Buttes, Oregon, is a BLM set-aside obsidian area. Collection becomes permit-dependent above a reasonable daily amount, above 250 lb, or for commercial use. Source: https://blm.gov/sites/default/files/docs/2024-07/orwa-rock-collect-guide.pdf, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
  - A 2009 BLM handout says no commercial collecting is allowed except by the holders of three mining claims. Source: https://pugetsoundknappers.com/interesting_stuff/Regulations/BLM_Glass_Buttes_Handout_4-09.html, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].

### Legitimate sources

- **Cinder and red lava rock** come from landscape-supply yards, which buy from commercial quarries. One listing shows red lava rock at **$145.25 per cubic yard** (material only), about 900 lb per yard. Source: https://hellogravel.com/products/red-lava-rock/?zip=29482, accessed 2026-10-31 [S]. That listing is not a California yard. Bay Area yards should be quoted directly.
- **Obsidian.** Buy rough from claim holders or dealers who state their claim or permit.
  - Asking prices for common black obsidian are about **$2–8/lb**. Source: https://plisio.net/education/how-much-is-obsidian-worth, accessed 2026-10-31 [S, low-authority guide].
  - Glass Buttes rainbow obsidian rough sells for about **$165 per lb lot**. Source: https://www.ebay.de/itm/406120516962, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
- **Pele's hair.** I found no licensed dealer.
  - Retail listings exist: a 10 ml vial at **$34** (mislabelled as olivine), and a tephra collection with Pele's hair at **$124**. Sources: https://www.ebay.com/itm/156972019985 and https://napoleonexhibit.com/store/Volcano-Village-Handpicked-Large-Pele-Tears-Hair-Unique-Features-Exceptional-_158097570393.html, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
  - These listings state no provenance. Unless a seller can document private land, I recommend **not using Pele's hair** [U].

### Stability in acrylic

- Obsidian and dense basalt are chemically inert and stable [U].
- Vesicular cinder and pumice hold air. Vacuum pre-dip them in monomer, as Mitsubishi recommends, or they will vent bubbles [U].
- Pele's hair is volcanic glass, "a filamentous variety of brown sideromelane glass". Source: https://www.usgs.gov/media/images/peles-hair, accessed 2026-10-31 [S]. It is chemically stable but too fragile to survive syrup flow. It also sheds glass fibres, a skin and inhalation hazard for workers [U].

### Ethics

Hawaiian lava has cultural weight. One source calls the "Pele's curse" story offensive to many Native Hawaiians. Source: https://www.hawaii-guide.com/why-you-should-never-take-lava-rocks-from-hawaii, accessed 2026-10-31 [S]. Avoid Hawaiian material altogether. Use mainland quarry cinder and say so on the provenance card.

## 2. Desert sand

### Legality

- **NPS units.** Death Valley National Park and Kelso Dunes, which sit inside Mojave National Preserve (an NPS unit), fall under 36 CFR 2.1. Sand is a mineral resource and cannot be removed. Sources: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/36/2.1 [S] and https://www.nps.gov/moja/kelso-dunes.htm, accessed 2026-10-31 [S]. The Kelso Dunes page does not mention sand collection specifically.
- **BLM land.** A personal handful is casual use. A product line is commercial and needs a permit under 43 CFR 8365.1-5(c) [O, see the commercial-use section].
- **California state parks.** 14 CCR 4307 names "sand" explicitly [S, URL above].
- **California beaches.** Coastal sand is also covered by 14 CCR 4307 in state parks [S]. Other coastal land may fall under local and Coastal Commission rules [U, not researched].

### Legitimate sources

- Commercial sand and gravel yards, such as Arizona Sand and Gravel at Quartzsite. Source: https://arizonasandandgravel.com/Service-Area/Quartzsite-Arizona, accessed 2026-10-31 [S, no price shown].
- Generic bulk sand runs about **$10–50/ton**. Source: https://hellogravel.com/?p=26789, accessed 2026-10-31 [S, low authority].
- Retail "desert sand" costs about **$4.50–7.30/lb**, for example a 4.4 lb bag at $19.99 [S, Walmart listing via search].
- For an edition, the best source is a named quarry with a lot number on the invoice [U].

### Stability in acrylic

- Quartz and feldspar sand is inert [U].
- Wash out salts and fines, then oven-dry. Dust clouds the casting and salts bloom [U].
- Respirable crystalline silica is a known workshop hazard. Wet-handle the sand and use a respirator [U].

### Ethics

- Sand is the world's most-extracted solid resource after water, at about 50 billion tonnes a year, faster than it replenishes. Source: https://www.unep.org/news-and-stories/press-release/rising-demand-sand-calls-resource-governance and https://www.stockholmresilience.org/research/research-stories/2026-05-12-use-sand-wisely-warns-un-in-new-report.html, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
- EARF's grams are trivial against that figure. Still, buy from a permitted domestic quarry, never from beach or riverbed dredging [U].
- "Sand from Death Valley" would be an illegal claim. Do not use place names you cannot document.

## 3. Sulfur crystals

### Legality

Native sulfur specimens are an ordinary mineral-trade item. US law does not restrict them [U].

- **Dallol, Ethiopia.**
  - Exports need a Ministry of Mines permit, royalty clearance and inspection. Source: https://wikiprocedure.com/index.php/Ethiopia_-_Apply_for_Precious_Minerals_Export_Permit_or_License and https://www.thereporterethiopia.com/1473/, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
  - The gemstone trade is moving to an ECX model. Source: https://capitalethiopia.com/2025/11/23/gemstone-trade-to-be-regulated-through-ecx-to-combat-smuggling/, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
  - Dallol sulfur crusts are mostly soft, ephemeral deposits rather than crystals [U]. I found no dealer offering documented Dallol material. Treat any "Dallol sulfur" offer as unverified.
- **Sicily (Agrigento, Cozzo Disi).** Classic specimens come from old mines and are traded as legacy material [S, dealer listings below].

### Legitimate sources and price

- **Earth Wonders.** Asking prices:
  - About **$160** for a single thumbnail Agrigento crystal.
  - **$1,750** for a cabinet piece with crystals up to 3 cm.
  - **$5,000** for an 800 g aggregate.

  Source: https://earthwonders.com/specimens/83646 and https://earthwonders.com/guides/sulfur, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
- **Weinrich Minerals** lists Agrigento and Cozzo Disi pieces. Source: https://www.weinrichmineralsinc.com/products/sulfur-2453650.php, accessed 2026-10-31 [S, price not shown].
- **FossilEra** sells about 1–2 inch Italian crystals. Source: https://www.fossilera.com/minerals/1-2-yellow-sulfur-crystals-italy, accessed 2026-10-31 [S, price not shown].
- For a run of editions, US commercial or Gulf Coast salt-dome sulfur would be far cheaper than Sicilian specimens [U, not priced].

### Stability in acrylic: poor for direct casting

- Sulfur is soft and brittle, and "can fracture if subjected to sudden temperature changes". Source: https://earthwonders.com/guides/sulfur, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
- Sulfur melts near 115 °C [U, standard handbook value]. That is inside the 105–125 °C finishing range above, and the exotherm adds thermal shock.
- Sulfur compounds are a known class of radical-polymerisation inhibitors. Source: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9920456/, accessed 2026-10-31 [S]. I could not confirm that elemental sulfur retards MMA cure [U]. A test pour is needed.
- Recommendation: **sealed pocket only, never direct casting.** Keep it out of strong light and heat [U].

### Ethics

Old Sicilian mines used child labour, the *carusi* [U, general history, not sourced here]. A provenance card that names the mine should be honest about that.

## 4. Redwood (bark, cones, burl), ferns, grasses

### Legality

- **Redwood National and State Parks**, and every NPS unit, falls under 36 CFR 2.1. It covers plants and "dead wood on the ground" as well as minerals [U, 2.1(a)(1)(ii) not opened].
- **California state parks.** 14 CCR 4306 covers plants and driftwood. Source: https://law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/14-CCR-4306, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
- **Burl poaching** is prosecuted as felony grand theft (PC 487(a)), vandalism (PC 594(a)) and receiving stolen property (PC 496(a)). There have been more than 100 old-growth burl thefts from North Coast parks. Source: https://www.nps.gov/redw/learn/news/arrest-made-in-burl-poaching-case.htm and https://www.northcoastjournal.com/news-2/park-service-burl-poacher-pinched-2562691/, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
- **Receiving stolen property is a risk for buyers.** An undocumented burl is a liability.
- **BLM and Forest Service land.** Cones and leaves are allowed as noncommercial "common renewable resources" under 43 CFR 8365.1-5(b) [O]. Commercial collection needs a forest-products permit (part 5400) [O].
- **Private land.** Collection is legal with written owner permission. CAL FIRE timber-harvest rules govern logging, not the gathering of cones [U].

### Legitimate sources and price

- **Far West Forest Products** sells salvaged coastal redwood and burl slabs.
  - A 48 bf salvaged slab is listed at **$1,367.84**, about $28/bf.
  - A 94 × 48 × 2.5 in burl slab is listed at **$1,880**, about $24/bf.
  - A roughly 25 in burl piece is listed at **$92**.

  Source: https://farwestforest.com/product/redwood-burl-slab-fw12820-3/ and https://farwestforest.com/product/coastal-redwood-burl-fw6030-5/, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
- High-grade redwood burl can "easily exceed $50 per board foot". Source: https://biologyinsights.com/how-much-are-tree-burls-worth/, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
- **Burl offcuts and pen blanks** are the right scale for EARF. Ask the dealer for the salvage source on each piece.
- **Cones, ferns and grasses:** use a California nursery or your own garden, or a private ranch with a signed letter of permission. They were not priced [U].

### Stability in acrylic

- **Wood** must be kiln-dried and ideally resin-stabilised first. Cactus Juice is a heat-cured resin that "hardens and stabilizes most porous material, especially wood" under vacuum. Source: https://www.metalclay.co.uk/content/Using%20Cactus%20Juice%20-%20Directions2.pdf, accessed 2026-10-31 [S]. Redwood tannins may bleed into the monomer [U].
- **Pressed plants fade.** UV "initiates photochemical reactions that break down pigments". UV glass "will slow down the fading, but nothing will prevent" it. Expect visible fading within a few years in light. Source: https://cottagelife.com/design-diy/how-do-i-preserve-the-colour-in-my-pressed-flowers/ and https://www.thegrumble.com/threads/help-please-framing-pressed-flowers.72395, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
- **Dry plants in silica gel.** Wet material bubbles. Source: https://nunndesign.com/?p=66297, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
- **Tint:** a UV-absorbing tint helps [U]. Design for a faded look, such as ferns that age to a straw colour, rather than promising green.

### Ethics

- Never sell anything "from the redwoods" without a chain of custody.
- Fallen redwood is habitat. "Fallen" is not the same as "free".

## 5. Feathers

### Legality

- **Federal.** The MBTA (16 USC 703) makes it unlawful to possess feathers of native North American birds without a permit. "There is no exemption for molted feathers or those taken from road- or window-killed birds." Exceptions exist for legally hunted waterfowl and migratory game birds, and for Native American use. Source: https://www.fws.gov/lab/featheratlas/feathers-law.html, accessed 2026-10-31 [S; the Feather Atlas page 404'd when fetched].
  - Hunted migratory-bird feathers may be possessed but not sold [U]. They are useless to a retailer.
- **California.**
  - FGC 3801.6(a): "it is unlawful to possess the carcass, skin, or other part of any nongame bird". (b)(2): "Nothing in this section allows any person to sell the feathers…of a nongame bird." Source: https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/fish-and-game-code/fgc-sect-3801-6/, accessed 2026-10-31 [O].
  - FGC 3039(a) bans selling any bird "found in the wild in California". 3039(c) adds: "inedible parts of domestically raised game birds may be sold or purchased at any time." Source: https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/fish-and-game-code/fgc-sect-3039/, accessed 2026-10-31 [O].
  - FGC 3503.5 protects raptors (Falconiformes, Strigiformes). Source: https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/fish-and-game-code/fgc-sect-3503-5/, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
- **What that leaves:**
  - **Domestic poultry:** chicken, domestic duck, turkey, guinea fowl.
  - **Farm-raised game birds:** pheasant and quail, under FGC 3039(c).
  - **Farmed exotics:** peafowl and ostrich.

  Non-native House Sparrow, European Starling and Rock Pigeon fall outside the MBTA. Source: https://www.featherfolio.com/blog/guide-to-legal-and-illegal-feathers-in-the-usa, accessed 2026-10-31 [S, secondary].
  - Peacock and pheasant status was confirmed only by secondary sources. Ostrich is CITES-listed only for the North African subspecies [U].
  - **Get a supplier statement that every feather is from domestic or farmed stock.**

### Legitimate sources and price

- **Zucker Feather Products** (Missouri), a wholesale feather house:
  - Natural guinea hen rounds, 6–8 in, about **$7.43 per 12 pieces** wholesale, with a $137.78 minimum.
  - Ostrich fringe from **$27 per yard** MSRP.
  - Natural pheasant tails, 14–18 in, **$33.24 per 10** at retail.

  Sources: https://faire.com/product/p_j9uvmhxt9h and https://www.walmart.com/c/kp/feather-supplier, accessed 2026-10-31 [S]. The Faire figures are ambiguous, so confirm them on the live page.
- **The Feather Place** (California, Missouri). Source: https://missourilife.com/the-feather-place-in-california-missouri-2/, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].

### Stability in acrylic

- Feathers are keratin and fairly stable once degreased and dry [U].
- **Caution on iridescence.** Peacock and pheasant sheen is structural colour. It comes from keratin and melanin layers with air gaps. Infiltrating monomer may fill the gaps and dull the colour [U, test it]. Pigmented feathers such as guinea fowl spots and pheasant barring should survive.
- Down traps air. Pre-wet it in monomer [U].

### Ethics

- Avoid anything that looks like a protected species: owl or hawk barring, jay blue. Customers cannot tell the difference, and resale could expose them.
- Use the provenance card to say "farm-raised" honestly.

## 6. Bones and fossils

### Legality

- **Domestic animal bone** (cattle, goat, sheep, pig, chicken) is legal [U].
- **Native wild birds:** covered by the MBTA and FGC 3801.6, as in section 5.
- **Wild California mammals:** FGC 3039(a) bans sale [O, URL above].
- **Marine mammals.**
  - MMPA, 16 USC 1372(a)(3)–(4): unlawful "to possess that mammal or any product from that mammal" taken in violation, and to sell such products.
  - 1372(e) exempts animals "taken before the effective date of this chapter". The Act took effect 60 days after Oct. 21, 1972.

  Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/16/1372, accessed 2026-10-31 [O]. Pre-Act claims are hard to prove, and CITES still applies (FWS answer via https://otterspecialistgroup.org/Library/QandA/QA_20061014.html, accessed 2026-10-31 [S]). **Do not use whale bone.**
  - 2026 change: P.L. 119-99 (the ARTIST Act) broadened the Alaska Native handicraft exemption. Source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ99/html/PLAW-119publ99.htm, accessed 2026-10-31 [S]. It does not cover EARF.
- **Human remains:** excluded.
- **Fossils.**
  - The Paleontological Resources Preservation Act, 16 USC 470aaa-5(a)(3), bars selling or buying a fossil if the person "knew or should have known such resource to have been excavated, removed" from Federal land. Penalties go up to 5 years. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/16/470aaa-5, accessed 2026-10-31 [O].
  - Casual collecting covers only common invertebrate and plant fossils for noncommercial use. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/16/chapter-1C, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
  - California Public Resources Code 5097.5 makes removing paleontological features from state or local public land a misdemeanour. Source: https://www.legiscan.com/CA/text/SB1034/2009, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
  - **Fossils from documented private land are legal to sell** [U, the PRPA reaches federal land only].

### Legitimate sources and price

- **Skulls Unlimited** (Oklahoma):
  - Real domestic goat skull: **$65**.
  - "Bag-O-Canine Bones": **$49**.
  - Coyote skull: **$75**.

  Source: https://www.skullsunlimited.com/collections/unique-offerings-at-skulls-unlimited?page=6, accessed 2026-10-31 [S]. The results did not say how the company sources its specimens. Ask for species and origin on each piece.
- For bone slices, a butcher's soup bones, degreased and whitened, cost almost nothing [U].

### Stability in acrylic

- Bone must be fully degreased. Residual fat yellows and can migrate [U].
- Bone is porous. Vacuum pre-dip it [U].

### Ethics

Domestic bone tells a farm story, which is fine. Avoid anything that would need a pre-Act certificate or a provenance argument.

## 7. Mushrooms (stabilised or freeze-dried)

### Legality

- Psilocybin mushrooms are federally Schedule I [U, well known]. Exclude them, including spore-bearing caps.
- **Amanita muscaria** is not federally scheduled.
  - The FDA (December 2024) says it is not authorised as a food ingredient. Source: https://www.fda.gov/food/hfp-constituent-updates/fda-alerts-industry-and-consumers-about-use-amanita-muscaria-or-its-constituents-food, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
  - Louisiana bans it expressly. Source: https://harris-sliwoski.com/psychlawblog/whats-the-deal-with-amanita-muscaria/, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
  - I found **no California statute** banning possession of whole dried fruiting bodies [U, unconfirmed]. CDPH has investigated a muscimol candy brand. Source: https://newsletter.doubleblindmag.com/p/amanita-muscaria-comes-under-fire-in-state-and-federal-investigations, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
  - An encapsulated, non-ingestible specimen looks low-risk, but get counsel before marketing "fly agaric" [U].
- **Wild collection** in NPS units and state parks is barred by 36 CFR 2.1 and 14 CCR 4306 [S, URLs above].

### Legitimate sources and price

- Cultivated gourmet species from a grower: oyster, lion's mane, shiitake, enoki [U, not priced]. They are legal, clean and repeatable.
- Commercial freeze-dried mushrooms are another option [U].

### Stability in acrylic

- Fresh fungi are about 90% water. They must be freeze-dried, then vacuum-stabilised (the Cactus Juice workflow), before any casting [U].
- I found **no source** on stabilising mushrooms specifically. The available guidance is all about wood. Source: https://www.penturners.org/goto/post?id=1471692, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
- Expect browning [U]. This one needs test pieces.

### Ethics

Cultivated mushrooms avoid both foraging pressure and drug-culture ambiguity.

## 8. Meteorites

### Legality

- **Private land.** A meteorite belongs to the landowner [U, general US case law, not opened]. Most of the trade is legal: North African NWA finds, Argentine Campo del Cielo, Namibian Gibeon, and Russian Seymchan.
- **BLM land.** IM 2012-182 sets three tracks:
  - Casual collection, which is "for personal use only, and therefore, cannot be bartered or sold".
  - Scientific collection under an Antiquities Act permit (43 CFR 3).
  - Commercial collection under a FLPMA land-use permit (43 CFR 2920).

  Source: https://www.blm.gov/policy/im-2012-182, accessed 2026-10-31 [S; the page was blocked by Akamai when fetched].
- **Forest Service** treats meteorites as objects of scientific interest under the Antiquities Act, not collectable [S, same search].
- **NPS land:** 36 CFR 2.1 applies [S].
- **Smithsonian:** I found no rule specific to the Smithsonian [U].
- **Country of origin.** Some countries restrict export, notably Gibeon material, which Namibia protects as heritage [U, not confirmed this session]. Buy only pieces that have been in the trade for years.

### Authentication

- **Name check.** Check every named meteorite against the Meteoritical Bulletin Database, which "provides authoritative information about meteorite names". Source: https://meteoritical.org/publications/meteoritical-bulletin, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
- **IMCA membership is a weaker signal.** IMCA self-polices its members and does not verify specimens from non-members. Source: https://associationsnow.com/2013/02/want-a-piece-of-that-space-rock-theres-an-association-for-that/, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
- Buy from long-standing dealers who give an IMCA number, the official name and a classification.

### Price (asking prices, not sold prices)

- **Common NWA chondrite:** about **$4.40/g** for NWA 16794 L6 at 63 g. Source: https://www.ebay.com/str/meteoritefinder, accessed 2026-10-31 [S]. A guide puts common specimens at **$0.50–5/g**. Source: https://www.brainbound.blog/meteorite-value-guide-1, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
- **Campo del Cielo:** about **$0.70/g** for a 131.5 g piece [S, eBay store via the same search].
- **Gibeon, etched:**
  - About **$2.00/g** for 218–732 g full slices. Source: https://meteoriteguy.com/catalog/gibeon.htm [S].
  - About **$3.90/g** for a 727 g slice. Source: https://www.meteorites.tv/677-exceptional-727-g-full-slice-of-the-gibeon-iron-meteorite-with-widmanstaetten-pattern-and-large-natural-troilite-inclusion.html [S].
  - About **$7.50/g** for a 6 g pack from Otto Frei. Source: https://www.ottofrei.com/products/gibeon-etched-meteorite-6-gram-pack [S].
  - All accessed 2026-10-31.
- **Seymchan pallasite:** about **$6.40/g** for an 18.7 g piece [S, eBay store via the same search].

### Stability in acrylic: irons need treatment first

- Iron meteorites rust because of terrestrial chloride, held mostly in akaganeite. The old "lawrencite" explanation is wrong.
- Sealants and oils "have met with limited success as the issue of chloride removal was not addressed".
- Recommended storage RH for irons is 0–10%.

Sources: https://www.natsca.org/sites/default/files/publications/JoNSC-Vol7-Allington_Jones_2020_0.pdf and https://cool.culturalheritage.org/byform/mailing-lists/cdl/2001/1537.html, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].

Etched Gibeon and Seymchan should go into a **desiccated sealed pocket**, after a chloride-reduction step or at least a thorough dry-out. Do not cast them directly. A slow-rusting iron under acrylic cannot be serviced [U]. Stony chondrites also hold iron metal and rust slowly [U]. Fusion-crusted stones are the most forgiving.

### Ethics

- The NWA trade has fed smuggling and the loss of scientific specimens [U].
- Prefer classified, named meteorites, and print the Meteoritical Bulletin name on the card.

## 9. Music artifacts

### Legality

These are ordinary goods [U]. The exceptions:

- Recordings may embed copyright, but a non-playable shard is not a copy [U].
- Artist names on cards may need permission [U].

### Magnetic tape

- **Construction.** Polyester base with a polyurethane binder. Tapes from the early 1970s to early 1980s are most at risk of "sticky shed". In that condition the binder hydrolyses, urethane migrates to the surface, and oxide sheds. Baking is only a temporary fix. Source: https://psap.library.illinois.edu/collection-id-guide/softbindersyn, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
- **In acrylic.**
  - The polyester base should be fine [U].
  - The polyurethane binder and lubricants are likely to swell or bleed in MMA, and oxide may flake into the casting [U].
  - Acetate-base tapes outgas acetic acid ("vinegar syndrome"). Same source [S]. Never embed them.
- **Recommendation.** Use a pocket, or pre-coat the tape with a monomer-resistant barrier and run a test [U].
- **Source and price:** studio clear-outs and new old stock. Not priced.

### Lacquer master discs

- **Construction.** Lacquer masters are aluminium discs coated with nitrocellulose lacquer. Nitrocellulose is "highly flammable and potentially explosive when exposed to heat, friction, or static electricity". Source: https://pure-analogue.com/the-lacquer-disc/ and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acetate_disc, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
- **Supply.**
  - Apollo Masters' Banning, California plant burned on 6 Feb 2020. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Masters_Corporation_fire [S].
  - MDC (Japan) remains, and was described as the only global maker. Sources: https://www.thevinylfactory.com/features/apollo-transco-lacquer-impact-vinyl-industry and https://djmag.com/longreads/vinyl-production-really-under-threat [S].
  - No blank price was found. Used, cut lacquers come from mastering studios [U].
- **Degradation.**
  - The lacquer delaminates.
  - The castor-oil plasticiser forms palmitic and stearic acid.
  - The nitrocellulose releases NOx and nitric acid in an autocatalytic reaction.

  Sources: https://siarchives.si.edu/blog/stabilizing-lacquer-transcription-discs and https://www.canada.ca/en/conservation-institute/services/conservation-preservation-publications/canadian-conservation-institute-notes/display-storage-objects-cellulose-nitrate.html, accessed 2026-10-31 [S]. Heat accelerates all of this.
- **In acrylic: do not cast directly.**
  - Nitrocellulose dissolves in esters, so MMA is likely to soften it [U].
  - The cure exotherm is heat applied to a flammable film [U].
  - Sealed enclosure traps acidic gases. CCI says sealing worsens it, though one study found no significant gas build-up [S, as above].
- **If used at all:** a small shard in a vented or acid-scavenged pocket, labelled with a lifespan [U].

### Vinyl records (PVC)

- **Plasticizer migration.** Acrylic is "receptive". Phthalate and adipate plasticizers "migrate from the PVC to the acrylic", causing crazing and stress cracking. Source: https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6432551 and https://www.mddionline.com/materials/know-your-plasticizers-new-study-plasticizer-migration, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
- Record compound is mostly rigid PVC with little plasticiser [U], so the risk is lower but not zero. MMA may also swell PVC during casting [U].
- **Recommendation:** run test pieces and check for crazing after a heat-age.
- **Source:** EARF's own pressings or deadstock. Not priced.

### Guitar strings

- **Corrosion.** Strings are nickel-plated steel or phosphor-bronze-wound steel. Corrosion "initiated through contact with human sweat" releases iron, tin and nickel ions. Source: https://www.polsl.pl/rch-l1/en/wavelength-of-corrosion/, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
- **Copper inhibits MMA cure** (see the Mitsubishi guidance above). Phosphor and 80/20 bronze wraps and brass ball-ends contain copper, so expect soft, tacky acrylic around them [U, test it]. Prefer stainless or nickel-plated steel strings, cleaned and degreased.
- **Played strings carry sweat salts.** Clean them ultrasonically and dry them [U].
- **Price:** about $6–15 per set at retail [U, not sourced].

### Styli

- A diamond tip on an aluminium (budget) or boron (premium) cantilever. Boron is "lighter and stiffer than aluminum".
- **Price:** replacements run from about **$8** (generic) through **$37** (Stanton-type) to **$229** (Audio-Technica VMN45xML, boron).
- Source: https://www.scmsinc.com/audio-technica-at-vmn45xml-replacement-stylus-microlinear-boron.html and https://www.parts-express.com/D6807A-Stanton-Type-Replacement-Stylus-230-415, accessed 2026-10-31 [S].
- **Stability.** Diamond and boron are inert, and aluminium is fine [U]. The plastic stylus body may craze. Embed the bare cantilever and tip, or cast the whole stylus in a pocket. Retired styli from customers or repair shops are the natural source.

### EARF's own acrylic offcuts

- Same polymer, so they are perfectly stable. A clear offcut disappears into clear acrylic because the refractive index matches. Tinted offcuts read as coloured shards [U].
- Surplus can go to PMMA depolymerisation, which recovers more than 95% of MMA at lab scale. Mitsubishi and Agilyx ran a full-scale trial in 2021. Source: https://www.chemicalprocessing.com/industrynews/2021/mitsubishi-touts-breakthrough-in-plexiglass-recycling/ and https://research.chalmers.se/publication/549945/file/549945_Fulltext.pdf, accessed 2026-10-31 [S]. I found no confirmation that a commercial plant is operating.
- **Price:** zero, since it is waste.
- **Ethics:** the strongest story of the set.

### Ethics for music artifacts

- Ask the artist or the studio before using a named master.
- Don't destroy unique, unreleased recordings for décor: digitise first, then cut.

## Provenance cards

Every edition should carry a card with:

- the material and species or mineral name
- where it came from: the dealer and invoice, the quarry and lot, or the farm
- the land status, for example "private land, owner permission dated …"
- how it was treated before casting
- what will change over time: fading, iridescence loss, slow rusting

Where the honest answer is "unknown", don't use the material.
