**Kept.**
- The two that every judge scored 7 or more: Stamper and Offcuts.
- The four at a mean of 7 that the brief needs: Groove, Contour, Firn and Iron. The brief asks for bubbles and a natural specimen, and Iron is the only specimen any judge scored above 7.
- Breath, also at 7, was left out. The brand director read it as Steuben's, and Iron answers the same product better.

**Changed by the judges' notes.**
- Abraham is now Firn. The brand director asked for a new name. The fabricator cut it to six layers, with rim steps and a press bond.
- Iron is two turned blocks with one equator bond. It has a chloride-reduced slice in a nitrogen-flushed pocket with a desiccant, and no battery or light inside the ball.
- Groove has its Smoke stratum under the channel.
- Stamper is cut by wire EDM, with the grooves kept clear of the cover.

**Gallerist:** The strongest editions are the ones only a laminator could make (designed air) and the ones whose contents are finite relics of EARF's own first record; specimens bought from dealers or beaches read as paperweights and should mostly be cut. Launch small and coherent: B1, B5 and C2 together as a 'Session 00' trio on Moho and Bedrock, with A6 as the single high-ticket piece, rather than all seventeen at once, which would look like a catalogue, not a statement. Make every number in an edition different (consecutive waveform slices, bubble maps, shard positions) so collectors chase sets and the secondary market has something to talk about. Perfection of the bonds is the whole value proposition: one hazy bond line or cement-filled void and the piece is a craft fair object, so set a rejection standard and destroy failures publicly. Drop A4, A5, A7 and C5 outright; they invite comparisons to Nucleo, Rusak and the deal toy that EARF cannot win.

**Brand:** The strongest editions put the product's own logic inside it: a core in Core, a contour in Relief, the factory's tints in the core sample, the stamper in the record stand. Everything that is a foreign keepsake under clear (a pebble, oats, a burl, a drawing, a stylus) slides toward the paperweight and the tombstone, which is what the prior art warns against. Habitat specimens work only when the specimen is geology and the form does something with it (Iron, the sawn Cinder pair), not when it just displays it. Watch the sound pieces (Groove, Session 00 tape) for the soundwave-gift and commemorative-slab readings; their story is strong but their form is fragile. Fix the Dallol provenance mismatch, or rename it, before it reaches a card.

**Fabricator:** The editions that stay inside what a laminating shop already does well (same-polymer strata, inert flat or dense objects in dry pockets, one big turned void) will ship; the many-bond-line void fields and the organics will eat yield. For bubbles, the enemy is the feathered edge: a lens or dish that meets the flat at a knife edge is a capillary wedge and pulls cement in, so every void gets a 0.3 mm square step at its rim as a capillary stop, cement applied as a degassed bead on the flats sized so the squeeze-out front pins at that step, a drilled vent flushed 20 min then plugged, sheets pre-dried and annealed at 80 C before bonding, 24 h wait, anneal at 70 C with a slow ramp (air at +18 % is no threat to small voids). Where there are many voids or bond lines, drop flowing cement for a solvent-assisted press bond (ethanol, 70-90 C, 1-2 MPa, lapped flats). Axisymmetric voids should be diamond-turned on the lathe, not milled, so their walls are optical off the tool. Pocket walls cannot be hidden by polish in an air pocket; hide them by viewing them edge-on (pockets bored on the view axis), by putting a tinted lamina over them, by bead-blasting the floor as a deliberate ground, or, only for inert dense objects, by backfilling with index-matched ACRIFIX in 2 mm open lifts with a sprue, then face-milling and bonding the lid.
