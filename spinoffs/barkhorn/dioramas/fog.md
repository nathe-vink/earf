# Fog: above the fog at dawn (round 31)

The clear speaker stands knee-deep in a sea of cotton wool and low fog at first light. Stuffed felt clouds in the icon's
shape (three circles on a bar, sewn round the edge with a running stitch) hang on threads at three depths against a
slate-blue paper sky; a tissue-paper sun sinks into the cotton on the horizon beside the horn and lights everything from
behind. The joke: a balsa ladder leans up out of the fog to the nearest cloud, as if someone had just gone up to hang it.

## Why this concept

Three quick concepts were tried in proofs a1 to a3:

1. **A white sky** (a1, a2, the first sketch taken further): felt clouds over a sea of fog on a pale sweep. Soft, but the
   clear speaker vanished: clear acrylic against white has nothing to show its edges, and the horn disappeared entirely.
2. **A dawn sun behind the speaker** (a3, a4): a low tissue sun on the paper sky. It gave the frame a light source to
   believe in and something to see through the acrylic, but the horizon fog sat exactly behind the horn.
3. **Above the fog at dawn** (a7 on, the pick): the paper sky brought close behind the speaker (2.6 m) and the camera at
   16 degrees, so the fog's horizon falls at the cabinet's top: the cabinet stands against fog, the horn against a
   slate-blue sky, and the white felt clouds read against the blue. Still high-key (the fog, the cotton and the felt are
   near white), with a tonal sky to give the clear acrylic something to catch.

## The set's materials (Fog's own vocabulary)

- **Felt**, stuffed: `kit_ext/fog.py` `plush`, the icon outline as a pillow (two faces meeting at a sewn seam, plump in
  the middle), a running stitch of paper thread along the seam, and a halo of loose fibres on the crisp one.
- **Cotton wool**: `wool`, balls fused into one skin (a voxel remesh; overlapping shells trapped the subsurface light and
  rendered grey), torn into lumps by a clouds displacement, deep-scattering so it glows at its edges. Fibres teased out
  of it cost three times the render for nothing visible at frame scale, so it has none.
- **Fog**: `mist`, a scattering volume thick at the floor and gone by 0.44 m, broken into billows by noise.
- **Thread**, a felt floor, a paper sky, a tissue-paper sun, and the balsa ladder (the one warm accent with the sun).

A volumetric cotton (`tufts`) was tried and dropped: dense scattering volumes go grey with the engine's two volume
bounces, and read as smoke rather than as cotton wool.

## Light

One idea: the dawn behind. A soft lamp at the sun's place, light-linked to everything but the paper sky and the fog
(it threw hard wedges on the sky and burnt the fog out); the same dawn, gentler, on the fog alone, so it glows toward the
sun; a warm wash on the lower sky round the sun, light-linked to the sky alone from well in front (from low behind the
cotton it cast a hard-edged wedge up the sky); a broad neutral sky fill from the front left.

## Proof rounds

- a1: first build. Fog too dense, the frame a white wall; speaker square-on; clouds unlit.
- a2: thinner fog, 3/4 view, bigger speaker. Still white on white; the horn invisible.
- a3: tissue sun; volumetric cotton (grey pebbles); mist too thin.
- cotton studies: volume tufts (grey, smooth), then solid wool with hair (dark: trapped subsurface), then fused wool (white,
  lumpy, right).
- a4: wool banks, sun sinking into the horizon bank. 4 min a proof: the fibres were the cost; dropped.
- a5, a6: high-key exposure and a slate sky; the horn still lost in the bright horizon. Full-size crop: depth of field
  only about 7 cm at f/2.8 at 1:12, the horn and back corners soft.
- a7: the sky wall brought to 2.6 m, camera at 16 degrees, f/11 at 1:11 (an effective f/1: about 0.7 m in focus at 7.4
  m, the foreground and the sky still melting). The horn reads against the sky.
- a8 to a10: the dawn lamp moved in front of the wall and light-linked off it; a bright wedge on the sky traced to the
  horizon glow lamp behind the cotton.
- a11: the glow relit from in front, linked to the sky alone; the ladder added; the near fog thinned.
- b1 (mid proof): the dawn burnt the fog on the right to flat white; the near cotton was all below the frame.
- a12, a13: the fog light-linked out of the dawn and given its own gentle backlight; cotton moved into the bottom corners;
  the far cloud's shadow smudge on the sky removed by bringing it forward.
- b2 (mid proof, with a full-size crop of the speaker): the speaker crisp edge to edge at f/11 at 1:11, the horn's rim
  and the back corners included; the fog shows through the clear cabinet. Missing: a strong tilt-shift cue, nothing
  close enough to the lens to melt.
- a14, a15: a felt cloud hung close to the lens in the top left corner (about 40 px of blur at full size); the ladder's
  cloud brought 0.2 m toward the speaker so it fits the 4:5 frame too.
- p1 to p3 (4:5): the default (same camera, 58 mm) made the speaker a quarter of the frame. A closer camera put the horn
  back against the fog, so the portrait keeps the landscape's place and angle on a 96 mm lens, aimed higher; the cloud by
  the lens then falls outside it, and one more cloud hung high (above the landscape's frame) fills the tall sky.

## Composition

- Landscape: the speaker a little left of centre, 48 % of the frame's height, its front turned 16 degrees to the camera.
  The ladder and its cloud on the left third, the sun low on the right third with a cloud above it, a small cloud far
  off between. The fog's horizon falls at the cabinet's top, so the horn is seen against the sky.
- Portrait: the same, narrower and taller; the speaker 34 % of the height in the lower half, the clouds climbing the
  sky on their threads.
