# Haze: dusk, in layered paper and tulle (round 31)

Set: `haze.py`. Shots: `studio/shots/barkhorn/diorama31/haze.json`, `haze-portrait.json`. Finals:
`renders/2026-10-18/barkhorn/diorama31/haze.png` (2400 x 1600) and `haze-portrait.png` (1600 x 2000).

## The concept, and why

A paper theatre at dusk. Flat cut-paper hills stand in receding flats on a paper floor, graded from deep violet in
front through lilac and rose to peach at the horizon, with toy trees (lollipops and poplars) cut in the same sheets.
A big tissue-paper sun sits low on the last flats, lit from behind, and three bands of violet tulle drift across it,
staggered and shorter as they rise: the Haze icon (a sun behind three bars) made of fabric. The backcloth is strips of
paper pasted up the wall, peach at the horizon to violet overhead. A satin ribbon river winds out of the hills past the
speaker's feet and picks up the sun.

- **One idea of light:** the low sun behind the set. It lights the tissue disc and the backcloth round it, rims the
  speaker, shines through the clear horn and foot, and throws the speaker's shadow toward the lens with violet light in
  it (the coloured cast the brief asks for). A soft lilac light on the speaker's face stands for the dusk sky; it is
  linked to the speaker alone so the paper floor stays in the evening.
- **Materials:** card (the flats, the floor, the moon), tissue (the sun and the two far flats, so the light behind them
  shows through), tulle (the three bands and two scrims between the flats, the haze that pales each range behind the
  last), satin (the river), paper (the backcloth strips, the threads). No felt, wool or cotton, so Haze's vocabulary is
  its own.
- **The joke:** the moon is already up in the corner, a paper crescent hung on a thread, waiting for its cue; the sun
  hangs on two threads too (just visible above it in the landscape), being let down for the evening.
- **Palette:** violet, lilac and rose, with peach as the one accent (the sun, its glow, the river).

Concept proofs: the first proof was the layout (flats, sun, ribbon) and read at once as dusk, so I kept it and spent
the rounds on light, craft and the speaker rather than on alternatives. Considered and dropped: a scattering haze
volume (round 13 below) and paper grass tufts in the foreground.

## Proof rounds (scratchpad proofs, 0.3 to 0.5 scale)

1. Layout. Read as a sunset at once, but the speaker was a dark box, the sun blew out, the tulle didn't show, the
   ribbon stood on edge (a tube's width is vertical: a flat ribbon is a thin tube with `flat` > 1) and the backcloth
   ended inside the frame.
2. Sun and halo down, scrims of tulle between the flats (the flats now step paler with depth), ribbon laid flat,
   backcloth widened; a lilac face light and a glow behind the speaker.
3. More light on the speaker, exposure +0.3; the tulle bands deeper and taller so the icon reads.
4. Speaker to the left third, sun to the right; a warm rim from behind-left; the bands now read as three bars.
5. The face and glow lights linked to the speaker alone (`receivers`), so they stop lighting the floor.
6. Sky light down; the bands made sheer (faded too far: back up in 9).
7. Trees cut into the flats' outlines (trunk and oval crown in the same sheet); far flats in tissue; the moon added.
   The tissue flats went to one pink fog and lost the layering.
8. f/2.8 to f/4 so the flats keep their edges; the third flat back to card with a clear value step per flat; eased
   edges that catch the rim; the foreground flats moved into the frame; the moon placed in the frame.
9. Flats run down to the floor at their ends instead of stopping square; one foreground flat removed (a hard corner
   at the frame's edge); bands shortened to just past the disc; the floor a little deeper.
10. Paper grass tufts on the river bank: read as sea urchins; removed. A light from behind through the horn.
11. Mid proof (0.5, 64 samples): the icon, the trees and the river all read; the sky too pink at the top.
12. Backcloth regraded so the violet arrives by z 3 m, inside the frame.
13. A scattering haze volume (density 0.02): the whole frame went to pink fog and noise, at twice the time. Dropped:
    the tulle is the haze.
14. A low light from behind through the speaker's clear foot: violet cast on the floor in front, but at 14 it washed
    the floor; 8 with a 45 degree spread keeps a pool with the violet shadow in it.
15. The poplars that striped the dark near flat moved up to the lighter flat, against the rose range.

Portrait: the camera comes round to azimuth -20, where the sun stacks above the speaker, at 93 mm, elevation 16 and a
lens shift, so the speaker is about 37 % of the height with the sun's top in frame; the speaker turned to -18 to face
it. The set and light are the landscape's.

## Final settings

- Camera (landscape): elevation 17, azimuth -12, 7.8 m, 55 mm, f/4 at miniature 12, focus on the speaker's front;
  `kit_scale` 12. Portrait: elevation 16, azimuth -20, 8.6 m, 93 mm, shift_y -0.085.
- Lights: `sun` (area behind the disc, 7), `halo` (point behind the disc, 120 W), `face` (lilac, 3.8, speaker only),
  `glow` (inside the speaker, 4, speaker only), `rim` (behind-left, 8), `through` (behind, low, 10, speaker only),
  `low` (grazing from behind, 8, 45 degree spread), `sky` (lilac from above, 0.4); a dim violet gradient world.
- Finish: bloom 0.3 at 50 px over 0.75, vibrance 0.15, lift 0.04 to #3B2A55, vignette 0.22; exposure +0.3.

## What I'd still improve

- The tulle bands' ends past the sun read as small rectangles; frayed or gathered ends would read more as fabric.
- The floor is plain paper; a faint deckle or a second sheet laid on it would give the foreground more craft.
- The pool of light from behind has a visible edge to the left of the speaker.
