EARF Globe
EARF · Round 22 · the globe

A 3D Earth in six colours, and the software it lives in

Three parallels and three meridians on the Earth's own 23.4° tilt, tipped toward you so the far side shows through. Each ring is one of the six colour Clouds, and none of them is black or white: Smoke and Fog are the black-only and white-only marks. Each ring is drawn at one steady speed and takes its colour once it closes. Then the globe turns on its axis, the way browser globes did in 1998.

Notes

Why it reads as the Earth

  • The axis leans 23.4°, as the Earth's does and as a desk globe's stand holds it. It also tips 22° toward you, so the parallels open into ovals and the far halves show behind the near ones.
  • The parallels are warm, north to south: Flare, Dust, Acid. The meridians are cool: Poison, Ice, Haze. Ice is the meridian facing you, so the globe's outline is blue.
  • The rings are drawn in that spectrum order. Each one stays in the ground's ink until it closes, then takes its colour all at once, like a paint bucket filling a closed shape.
  • The far half of each ring is lighter and sits under the near halves. Even in one colour, the near side reads in front and the globe reads as round. With every line the same weight, the one-colour mark tangles (see Even lines in the player).

The rails

  • Black only: paper, print, embossing and a one-colour etch. White only: dark grounds, and the etched mark on the acrylic.
  • One colour: each Cloud's own mark, for its speaker, box and page. Avoid Acid on paper, where yellow is too faint; use it on ink.
  • Multicolour: the brand at large. Animated: screens only (the ident, the loader, the cursor, the coin).
  • Lines for most sizes. Below about 32 px the far halves fade and the near ones still read. Discs are the solid version, for fills, stamps, stickers and the smallest sizes.
  • Clear space: a quarter of the globe's width on every side. In the lockup, the globe is one and a half cap heights tall, centred on the letters.
