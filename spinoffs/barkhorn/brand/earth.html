Flush discs, all six in acrylic, a calmer arrival, and a Death Star

The same 3D Earth on the Earth's 23.4° tilt, in colour theory: the horizontal discs are the primaries, red, yellow and blue, the upright ones the colours they mix, orange, violet and green. In colour the discs are flush, with no strokes: the ball itself is violet, the atmosphere, so the globe is round without an outline. The strokes are the one-colour versions'. In acrylic every disc is tinted. Each ident ends on the whole globe, upright, and then it tilts: the Gyroscope spins on its axis, the Coin’s face is the globe, and Earthrise, new, rises over a horizon.

The globe as a Death Star — its dish a driver

A sphere, blue, with the equator’s trench in green: the outside. In its northern half a dish, a little bigger than the Death Star’s, that is a driver seen from the front: the surround red, the cone orange, the dust cap yellow, as the Earth’s core. The dish is concave, so its rings sit a little off centre. Flat colour and no strokes; in one colour, the trench and the rings’ edges are gaps. Purple can be a small cap at the centre, and it is the ring of sound the dish sends out when it kicks (below).

Theme — Atmosphere, with an icon for each speaker from the marks’ own circles, bars and triangles

Each icon is made as the cloud mark is: a few circles, bars and triangles, all filled, then every outline drawn over them, like stacked clear acrylic. Poison is two crossing bars with circles at the ends; Acid is a circle with a triangle on top.

Notes

Why it reads as the Earth

  • The axis leans 23.4°, as the Earth's does and as a desk globe's stand holds it. It also tips 22° toward you, so the parallels open into ovals and the far halves show behind the near ones.
  • The colours are colour theory (round 24): the parallels are the primaries, north to south, Flare red, Acid yellow and Ice blue; the meridians are the secondaries, Dust orange, Haze violet and Poison green. Haze faces you, so the globe's outline is violet, the colour of the atmosphere at the Earth's edge.
  • Discs in colour are flush (round 24b): no seams, no outline, no light. The ball itself is violet where no disc reaches its rim, so the globe is round and solid without a stroke. The strokes belong to the one-colour versions: there the seams are gaps in the ground, and the parts run out to the rim.
  • Acrylic: every disc tinted (round 24b). All six alike goes to mud, the violet meridian facing you lying behind everything, so the three mixed colours' discs go down first as a light wash and the primaries over them, multiplied on paper so where two primaries cross they make the colour between: red over yellow orange, yellow over blue green. On ink each is laid over the last. No strokes.
  • Lines are even, every line one weight, the far halves under the near. Round 22's lighter far halves (in brand/earth-lines-*.svg) remain for one colour at small sizes, where even lines tangle.
  • Seen from above the pole, the same rings are a speaker: rings round one centre, with the meridians as spokes. Round 23’s Kick used that view; round 24b makes the speaker a Death Star’s dish instead.
  • Round 23's tubes, the printer and the kick, and round 24's lit sphere and cut-open globe, are set aside; the first three stay in earth.py and in round 23's files.

The rails

  • Black only: paper, print, embossing and a one-colour etch. White only: dark grounds, and the etched mark on the acrylic.
  • One colour: each speaker's own mark, for the speaker, its box and its page. Avoid Acid on paper, where yellow is too faint; use it on ink.
  • Multicolour: the brand at large. Animated: screens only (the idents, the loader, the cursor).
  • Discs, flush, for colour. In one colour, the seamed discs or the even lines; below about 32 px round 22's lighter far halves read better.
  • Clear space: a quarter of the globe's width on every side. In the lockup, the globe is one and a half cap heights tall, centred on the letters.

The icons

  • Each works in the same rails as the globe: tinted, lines and black only. Fog's tint is nearly white, so on paper its outline carries it.
  • Where they could go: beside the mark on the speaker (etched as the mark is), on the end of the box, and as each speaker's tab on the site.
  • The names don't have to change to use them. Under Elements the icons read as hazard signs; under Clouds or Atmosphere, as weather.
