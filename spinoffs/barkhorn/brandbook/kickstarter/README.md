## 1. The platform rules, checked live on 31 October 2026

### Kickstarter

Source: the help article *What are the rules for hardware and product design projects?*, dated 6 August 2026
([help.kickstarter.com](https://help.kickstarter.com/en-us/articles/16236428-what-are-the-rules-for-hardware-and-product-design-projects)).

| Question | The rule | What it means for EARF |
|---|---|---|
| **Can renders be shown?** | "We do not allow photorealistic renderings." | **No.** Every EARF picture so far is a photoreal path-traced render. None of them may appear on the campaign, the pre-launch page or the film. Technical drawings, CAD views and sketches have been allowed since Kickstarter clarified the rule in 2012 ([Core77](https://www.core77.com/posts/23506/Regarding-Kickstarter-n-Product-Design-Projects)). Use them, plainly drawn and never rendered to look real. |
| **Is prototype evidence required?** | Creators must "show working prototypes of their projects." | **Yes.** A working Core and a working Hemisphere, filmed and photographed working. `first-articles.md` dates these: possible for 21 January, photographed by mid-February 2027. The campaign can't open before then. |
| **What else?** | Creators must "clearly detail for their backers how they'll produce their product", and say "whether they've produced anything like it before." | The campaign page carries a production section: who forms, turns and assembles, and in what order. It says plainly that this is EARF's first production run (`COPY.md`). |
| **Honesty** | The Design & Technology article asks that projects be "honestly and clearly presented" ([help](https://help.kickstarter.com/en-us/articles/16236513-i-m-looking-to-run-a-design-technology-project)). The prototyping guide (kickstarter.com/honest) refused the request (403) and was not retried. | Label every image for what it is: *Prototype*, *Drawing* or *Sample of the tint*. Show no feature the prototype doesn't have. |
| **Fees** | "Kickstarter collects a 5% fee from the funds collected"; Stripe takes "roughly 3-5%" (help, *What are the fees*, dated 2 September 2026). Fees apply only if the project funds. | Budgeted at **8.5%** of pledges (est.). |
| **Pledge Over Time** | Pledges of $125 or more can be split into three payments, the last about two months after the deadline (R2, citing Kickstarter's help). | Offer it. Every EARF reward is over $125. |
| **Late pledges** | Since 2024, creators can keep taking pledges after a funded campaign ends ([Kickstarter](https://updates.kickstarter.com/understanding-late-pledges-a-comprehensive-guide-for-creators/)). | **The pre-launch page** | Followers who click "Notify me on launch" get Kickstarter's email when it goes live; the follower count shows from 10 ([help](https://help.kickstarter.com/en-us/articles/16236379-setting-up-your-project-s-pre-launch-page)). Its pictures are photographs and drawings too. |

### Indiegogo, briefly

- Gamefound bought Indiegogo in 2025.
- Since the platform upgrade of 16 October 2025:
  - fees are a flat 5% plus 3% and $0.20 a transaction in processing;
  - **Flexible Funding is gone**, so it is all-or-nothing like Kickstarter;
  - InDemand became "Late Pledge" (R2 §4.3, citing [Indiegogo help](https://help.indiegogo.com/article/645-what-changed-with-the-platform-upgrade)).
- Indiegogo's own support pages refused the request today (403) and weren't retried.
- Third-party guides say Indiegogo doesn't strictly require a working prototype ([PledgeBox](https://www.pledgebox.com/post/indiegogo-vs-kickstarter)). That isn't confirmed from Indiegogo's own terms.
- **Verdict:** the same fees, the same all-or-nothing, and none of Kickstarter's design audience (est.). Its looser rule on prototypes doesn't help EARF: real prototypes are the point. **Not chosen.**

### Pre-orders on earf.world, briefly

- **The law is the same, and stricter in practice.** A paid pre-order is a sale under the FTC's Mail, Internet or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule (R2 §4.4):
  - a reasonable basis for any ship date;
  - the buyer's consent to each delay, or a refund within seven working days;
  - for a product not yet available, the rule's "dry-testing" disclosures.
- **California adds two more** (R2 §4.4): B&P §17538's disclosures, and Civil Code §1671 against non-refundable deposits.
- **Payments.** Stripe or Shopify take about 3% (est.), with no platform fee. Stripe lists "businesses offering a reward in return for donation" as restricted (R2, citing [Stripe](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses)), so it must be a plain sale or a refundable deposit, never a "reward".
- **What it lacks:**
  - Kickstarter's audience;
  - the all-or-nothing floor, which protects EARF from making 20 lamps at a loss;
  - Pledge Over Time.
- **Verdict:** use the site for **Tropos's Founding Hundred** (refundable deposits on terms counsel writes: `money/founding-terms.html`), and for the lamps **after** the campaign, as late orders. Use Kickstarter for the lamps' first run.

**On the discounts.** R4 advises against discounting the Founders' Edition: "a discount anchors the brand low". These
tiers follow that advice:
- the numbered tier sits **above** list;
- the open tiers are at most 13 to 14% under list, and only for the first backers;
- list returns after the campaign.

Kickstarter hardware often runs 25 to 35% early-bird discounts (est.). EARF doesn't need to: the margin table shows
why the deeper cut would leave Core's early bird at about a third of its pledge.

## 3. The goal: what it must raise, and why

**Proposed goal: $110,000 (est.)**: about 110 backers at an average pledge near $1,000.

**Why that number.** The pledges pay for the lamps themselves, with margin over. The goal is set where that margin
also pays the fixed costs that a first run can't avoid:

| Fixed cost | est. |
|---|---|
| Certification: FCC, DOE Appendix Y, CEC, and the UL 153 listings for both lamps (midpoint of R3's $18.5k to $47k) | $32,750 |
| A second Hemisphere shell mould, for wear over a run | $1,500 |
| Test units used up in certification, two of each | $1,480 |
| Photographs and the film, of the real prototypes | $3,000 |
| Pledge manager and survey | $1,000 |
| Launch-week advertising | $4,000 |
| Contingency, 15% | $6,559 |
| **Fixed, in all** | **about $50,300** |

At a run of 50, the margin is about 47% of every pledge (est.), so carrying $50,300 takes about **$106,000** of
pledges. That rounds up to the goal.

**Below the goal the campaign should not fund.** EARF would be making lamps it can't certify. All-or-nothing is the
protection; that is why the goal isn't set low to make the campaign look successful.

**The range:**
- Labs quoting R3's low end: about **$75,000**.
- Labs quoting R3's high end: about **$145,000**.
- The pre-seed paying for the two UL listings: about **$50,000**.

So the lab quotes (`companions/launch/lab-rfq/`) set the final goal. Ask for them before the pre-launch page goes up.

**The target, not the goal:** R2's customer-funded path aims at $150k to $500k for the lamps.

**What the prototypes cost isn't in it.** The moulds and first articles (about $6,600 for the two lamps;
`first-articles.md`) are paid from the investor deck's $50,000 before the campaign. Each unit carries its share of
the moulds, so that money comes back.

## Open questions for the owner

1. **The prices.** R4's $750 and $1,200 are recommendations; the press sheets still say "Set at launch". The tiers above are proposals.
2. **Which Hemisphere ships.** It could be the plug-in swag version, listed to UL 153 (R3's and the lamps lane's suggestion for the first sales), or the hardwired pendant under UL 1598, which the press sheet names. The economics assume the swag version.
3. **Matched tints or stock tints.** `first-articles.md` uses the stock sheet nearest each tint for January and saves the matched casts for Milan. The campaign must promise only what the run will be cast in.
4. **The figures.** Core weighs about 1.46–1.5 kg (the 2.2 kg figure is from a superseded round); its runtime is 4.7 h at full brightness at the 90% limit, 5.8 h at the bar setting. Measure the prototype.
5. **A company.** The campaign needs a payout account and someone verified to run it (check Kickstarter's current requirements before the pre-launch page). R2 advises forming the entity first; Vienna's Start-Up Area needs one too.
