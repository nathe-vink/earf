## 2. Mechanical licences: the songs, not the recording

The master belongs to the artist. The **songs** belong to their writers and publishers. Every song pressed on the LP
needs a mechanical licence, at the statutory rate or by direct agreement.

- **The rate.** The statutory rate for physical records in 2026 is "the greater of 13.1 cents or 2.52 cents per minute"
  for each song on each copy (HFA's help, checked 31 October 2026; set by the Copyright Royalty Board under 37 CFR Part
  385, Phonorecords IV, 2023 to 2027 ([Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/new-2026-us-mechanical-royalty-rates-set/))).
  - 2027's rate is adjusted for inflation each year, so expect a little more (est.).
  - A proposed settlement for 2028 on would reset the base to 12 cents ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/us-copyright-royalty-board-proposes-extending-physical-and-download-mechanical-rates-through-2032/)); it's not final.
- **The artist's own songs.** A licence from the artist (or their publisher) at the statutory rate, or waived under
  Option A. Find the publisher before the night.
- **Covers.**
  - **Route 1: HFA Songfile** (songfile.com). It licenses physical runs online, paying the statutory rate plus a
    processing fee: "$16 processing fee per song license", "$14" each beyond five in one transaction, "charged per song
    license, not per unit" (HFA help, checked 31 October 2026). A third-party guide says Songfile covers runs of 25 to
    2,500 units ([Sonicbids](https://blog.sonicbids.com/copyright-and-your-band-cover-songs-part-two)); confirm on the site.
  - **Route 2: direct.** For a song Songfile doesn't administer, ask the publisher directly (found through the PROs'
    repertory searches).
  - Instrumentals of a standard, as in jazz, are still the composer's song and need the licence.
  - **Public domain.** Works whose US copyright has expired need no licence, but an arrangement of one may be
    protected; check each.
- **The arithmetic at 300 copies** (est., at 2026's rate):

  | Case | Sum | Licence | Processing | Total |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Ten songs under five minutes, all covers | 10 × $0.131 × 300 | $393 | 10 songs at $16 / $14: $150 | **$543** |
  | One 14-minute ambient piece | 14 × $0.0252 × 300 | $106 | $16 | **$122** |

- **When.** Licences must be in place **before the records are made**, so they're obtained between the night and the
  master going to the plant (February).

## 3. Also to clear

- **The room.** The hire agreement must allow recording, filming, and the playing of music. **[COUNSEL]** whether the
  room's public-performance licences (ASCAP, BMI, SESAC, GMR) cover a private ticketless event, or EARF needs its own.
- **The engineer.** A work-for-hire agreement: the engineer is paid and owns nothing in the recording. Credit as
  agreed.
- **The jacket.** The photographer's licence for the cover photograph, the designer's for the jacket.
- **Download codes.** If they're delivered through a third-party service, check that its terms allow codes for a
  recording that EARF only licenses.
