## The price, and break-even

**Price: $40** a numbered LP (IDEAS.md (a), est.), sold at the debut, on earf.world, through the artist, and
with the Kickstarter as an add-on. 25 copies go to the artist free.

**Option B: $6 a record to the artist, from the first copy.** Each sale nets $32.80 after card fees and the royalty.

- At **$45**, option B breaks even at 290 (300) or 322 (500).
- At **$50**, option B breaks even at 257 (300) or 285 (500).

**At $40, 300 copies don't pay for themselves**: break-even is more records than there are to sell. Three levers,
in order of preference:

1. **Price the numbered LP at $50** (est.; it's an edition of 300 from one night, numbered): break-even 257 of 275.
2. **A partner pays for the pressing** (about $3,000): at $50, break-even falls to 186, and selling out leaves about $3,771.
3. **Press 500**: a record costs $24.21 instead of $36.39, but 500 is a lot of one live set to sell.

**Recommended: 300 at $50, with the record partner paying the pressing.** The cheapest single saving after that is
the mix: a live set mixed as one performance, not song by song, could cost half (ask the engineer).

## How it ties to the record-label partner

PARTNERS.md's third email asks a label or shop for "the records, the set and the Session in kind, or a contribution
toward the pressing". The pressing, freight and numbering come to about $2,896 at 300 (est.).
So:
- **A partner who pays for the pressing** (about $3,000) takes break-even at 300 from about 333 to about 241 records,
  and is named on the jacket ("pressed with [partner]").
- **A label co-release** (for example a reissue label that already distributes to shops) could take 100 copies at
  wholesale, about half of retail (est.), into record shops: less margin, far more reach, and the partner's name on the spine.
- **A shop partner** sells it over its counter and hosts a listening; it's paid as any stockist is.
