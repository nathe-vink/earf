# EARF packaging: Tropos's crate and horn case, Hemisphere's drum, Core's box

Brand lane, r47 (2026-10-31). A concept for the owner: **every size, stock and test below is a proposal** until signed.
Pictures: `out/pack-pair.png`, `out/pack-open.png`, `out/pack-lamps.png` (path-traced by `pack_render.py`), and the
printed faces `out/decal-crate-front.png`, `-inside`, `-side`, `out/decal-case.png`, `out/decal-drum.png`,

## The idea

The acrylic is tinted all the way through, so the packaging is too: the lamps' boxes are made of board dyed through
in the tint of the shade inside, and Tropos's crate is plain birch printed in two inks, black and the tint. Opening
should be a sequence the owner can follow without reading a manual, and without lifting 72 kg.

## Tropos

A pair ships as three pieces: two upright crates (one a speaker, cabinet only) and one horn case holding both horns.
The horn lifts off (the site: about 72 kg with it, 62 kg without), so the crates carry the lighter cabinet and the
most fragile, most beautiful part travels on its own, face up.

**The opening, in five steps** (printed inside the door): unlatch; lower the door; walk the speaker out on its sled;
lift the sleeve off; crown it with the horn from its case.

**Shipping.** Freight (less than a truckload), white-glove for the first pairs. Test to ISTA 3B (packaged products for
LTL shipment) with a dummy load of the cabinet's mass and centre of gravity before the first shipment; add a 300 mm
drop on each bottom edge and a tilt test on the crate. The crate is built to be used more than once: whether it is
collected back is an operations decision for the owner, not a claim to make.

**Cost.** Not quoted. A crate shop's figure for an 18 mm ply crate of this size, with foam and hardware, is the first
thing to ask for; the felt, sleeve and printing add to it. The sleeve doubles as the dust cover, so it replaces a part
that would otherwise be bought.

## Hemisphere: the drum

Each shade is sold singly, so each comes in a drum of its own: 260 mm across, 170 mm tall, through-dyed board in the
shade's tint (a dyed-through board range such as GF Smith's Colorplan is the reference; to be matched to the eight),
the globe blind-debossed on the lid, the wrap printed white: "Hemisphere", "A quarter turn off, a quarter turn on.",
the wordmark. A moulded pulp ring holds the shade by its aluminium collar, clear of every wall. The fitting (the hub,
canopy and cord) ships in a black tray under the shade's drum when sold with one. Eight drums in a row are the
spectrum: at the bar they can sit on the back shelf as the stock of spare shades.

Test to ISTA 3A (parcel).

## Core: the cube

A 220 mm cube in through-dyed board in the ball's tint, the lid lifting off. Moulded pulp: the ball on top, seated in
a cup; the base, the USB-C cable and adapter beneath. White print on one face: the wordmark, "Core", "A table lamp ·
plugged in or not". The cells ship fitted in the base and are declared to the carrier as required for lithium cells
(the lamps' certification lane owns that).

Test to ISTA 3A (parcel).

## Mantle

To be drawn from Mantle's 470 × 380 × 194 mm: a shallow birch crate, the lid printed with the tint, the platter and
the dust cover packed apart from the plinth.

## Why it raises the odds

- **Fewer damaged pairs.** One freight claim on a pair can erase the margin on several; the crate's door and sled
  keep the speaker upright and on its feet from truck to room.
- **Easier to sell through trade.** A dealer, a hotel or a bar can receive and open it without a specialist or tools.
- **The first post.** Unboxing is the first picture most owners share; the door coming down, the sleeve lifted and
  the horn crowned are three good ones.
- **Spares that sell themselves.** The eight drums on a shelf show the whole range of shades in one glance.
