**Each pack has two pages:**
- **Page 1:** what EARF is, the proposal, why it suits the partner (with its own precedent), and what each side brings.
- **Page 2:**
  - the terms in outline, every line marked **for counsel** and inside R9's ranges;
  - the timeline from the debut;
  - what EARF sends next;
  - what stays EARF's own.

**Pictures.** The packs carry renders, labelled "Render · replaced by a photograph from the debut before sending".
**Swap in the debut's photographs before any pack leaves.** R9 says to send "with real photos".

**The terms are R9's ranges, not proposals of price:**
- royalty 3–7% of net wholesale (5% common);
- development fees recoupable, advances small or none;
- a minimum guarantee from year 2 (for Pro-Ject);
- terms of 1–3 years, a sell-off of 30–180 days, and reversion if not launched in 12–18 months;
- EARF approves colour, material and finish;
- a suggested retail price only, because a licensor can't set resale prices in California (R9 D5, citing Lieberstein & Kumar);
- quarterly statements and audit.

Counsel reviews every line before a pack is sent.

## Before any of them goes

- **No earlier than the debut.**
  - Klipsch goes 1 to 4 weeks after it.
  - Ace goes 2 to 5 weeks after (February).
  - Pro-Ject goes 3 to 8 weeks after (February to March), for a meeting at Vienna, 6 to 9 May.
  - Milan is 13 to 18 April (Salone; R9).
- **Photographs in, renders out** of every pack.
- **Counsel** reads page 2 of each.
- **One at a time.** If Klipsch answers, the Pro-Ject letter should say nothing of it, and the other way round.
- **R9's odds (est.):** Klipsch 5–10%, Ace 10–20%, Pro-Ject 5–10%. These are long shots worth the postage, not a plan to depend on.
