**Drinks and the law.** California's tied-house rules bar alcohol makers and wholesalers from furnishing money or things
of value to on-sale licensees, with narrow exceptions (B&P §25500). Structure any spirits partnership with counsel; serve
through a licensed caterer (the pop-up plans Type 58 caterer nights). A non-alcoholic partner avoids the question; and if alcohol at the venue needs a
permit, the zero-proof partners (Ghia, The New Bar) are the easy path.

## Email 1: a hotel

**Subject:** A listening room for our debut, and your guests in it

Dear [Name / team at the hotel],

I'm [Your name], and I make EARF: hi-fi and light in tinted cast acrylic, in eight colors, here in San Francisco. On
[DATE] our first pair of Tropos, a horn loudspeaker, plays for the first time, at a debut night at [VENUE]. After it
comes a listening room we plan to open.

The guests are the city's design galleries, its studios and showrooms, its hi-fi shops and listening bars, record labels, and the design and hi-fi press.

We're looking for one hotel to make the night theirs, and our first ask is the room itself: an event room for the
evening of the debut, or a lent space we can fill with sound and light for a night. In return you would be named on
the invitation, host the night's Session (one album, start to finish, under your name), and have its photographs.
Beyond the room, rooms for a few visiting guests and press, or a contribution of $5,000 to $15,000, would also help
make the listening room happen. Later, if it suits you, a listening suite fitted by EARF.

The brief is attached: two pages. Could we talk for twenty minutes in the next two weeks?

With best wishes,
[Your name]
