# Big ideas: seven ways to raise the odds of funding and sales

Brand lane, r47 (2026-10-31). Each idea has a mockup (in `out/`, drawn in `scenes/` or path-traced by
`ideas_render.py`) and a short plan. **Every cost here is an estimate made from general trade knowledge, not a
quote**, and every name, date and price in a mockup is a placeholder until the owner sets it. The Brand Book's tenth
chapter shows them.

## (a) EARF Sessions

**Mockup:** `out/idea-sessions.png`: the 12-inch jacket (the listening room on the front, "[Artist] at Planet EARF"),
the record pressed in 04 Poison half out of it, the back's "One album, start to finish."

**What.** The house book already has the Session: Tuesday to Thursday at 8 pm, one album start to finish, six chairs,
no talking. Record some of them live, in the room, through the room's own pair, and press them: one release per
artist, numbered, on vinyl tinted to match the night's tint. Sold at the bar, on the site and through the artist.

**Cost (est.).** Multitrack recording in the room with a hired engineer, about $1.5k to $3k; mixing and mastering,
$1k to $2k; lacquer cutting and a short run of 300 coloured 180 g LPs with printed jackets, about $3k to $5k. About
$5k to $9k a release before the artist's share. At about $40 each, 300 records return about $12k. **Timeline.** A
pressing takes some weeks to a few months depending on the plant's queue. Session 00 does not need the bar: it can be
made in any good room the week the first pair plays.

**Why it raises the odds.** It is proof the speakers are serious, in the only form that can't be faked: a record made
through them. It gives the press and social a recurring reason to write. It is a product with a margin from day one.
And it makes Planet EARF a label as well as a bar, which is easier to fund than a bar alone.

## (b) A debut installation: Milan Design Week, April 2027 (or Design Miami, December 2027)

**Mockup:** `out/idea-milan.png`: the invitation ("Something in the air.", the uplit eight), the room in plan (eight
Tropos lit from below on a curved wall, Mother EARF overhead, a bench, Mantle and the coins by the door).

**What.** One dark room. The eight Tropos lit from below, throwing their colours up a wall, the picture on the site
made real at full size. A Session each night. The coin at the door. Every piece wears its tag, and the first run's
orders are taken there.

**Cost (est.).** A space in one of the design districts for the week, about $15k to $80k; build, light and sound,
$20k to $50k; freight both ways for eight crates, the horn cases and the globe, $10k to $25k; travel, staff and a
press agent, $20k to $45k. About $80k to $200k all in. **Timeline.** Spaces for April are taken through the winter:
book by about January 2027; ship in March. The eight speakers must exist first, which makes this the deadline that
pulls the first production run forward. If Milan is too soon, Design Miami in December 2027 gives eight months more.

**Why it raises the odds.** Design press, interior designers, hotel groups and retailers are all in one city for one
week. One room that photographs as well as the renders turns a site of renders into a brand with a public debut, and
a list of trade contacts to sell the first run to.

## (c) Mother EARF as public art

**Mockup:** `out/idea-plaza.png`: the colour globe at 4 m across on a low black plinth in a stone plaza at dusk, its
rims lit in their tints. Path-traced from the globe's own model (`out-globe-colour`).

**What.** The bar's 1.25 m Mother EARF, made at city scale for a plaza, a waterfront or a festival: six slotted
discs, each in its tint, edge-lit at night so the rims draw the globe in light.

**Cost (est.).** Cast sheet does not come 4 m wide, so each disc is built from segments on a steel or aluminium
armature: material, fabrication, structure and engineering, lighting and installation, about $150k to $300k. A 2 m
version for a design week or a festival is a fraction of that. **Timeline.** A temporary piece, 6 to 9 months; a
permanent public commission, 12 to 18 months or more with the city's arts process, engineering and permits.

**Why it raises the odds.** The brand's mark at the size of a building, photographed by everyone who passes. It is
also the most fundable idea here, because someone else can pay for it: a developer's plaza, a corporate sponsor, a
festival's arts budget. Start with a 2 m globe at the Milan room; let it travel.

## (d) Hotel listening suites, fitted by EARF

**Mockup:** `out/idea-hotel.png`: a suite in plan (Tropos 04 at the window, Mantle between, the chair on the axis,
Core, Hemisphere over the desk, 200 records), its acrylic key in Poison, and the card on the desk ("This room plays.").

**What.** A suite in a boutique hotel, fitted with a complete EARF room in one tint, with a record library chosen for
it. Everything can be bought at the front desk. A pilot with one hotel, then a programme: one suite per hotel, a
different tint in each.

**Cost (est.).** The kit at trade price (a pair, Mantle, a Core, a Hemisphere), installation and the records; the
hotel pays for it or shares it. EARF's own cost is mostly the first suite's time. **Timeline.** After the first pairs
are measured; a pilot can open within a season of a signed hotel.

**Why it raises the odds.** A night with the speaker sells it better than any picture. Hotel guests are the customer,
and a suite is a showroom that pays for itself. It is trade revenue that doesn't depend on opening the bar.

## (e) Artist editions: a ninth tint

**Mockup:** `out/idea-edition.png`: Tropos in a ninth colour beside the eight (a hue study of the Ember render, not a
cast), with the edition's card: "A ninth colour, made once."

**What.** Once a year a musician or artist chooses a tint with EARF. It is cast in one dye lot, made into a numbered
run of Tropos, Hemisphere shades and coins, paired with a Session pressed in the same colour, and never cast again.
The artist names it, from what drifts through the air.

**Cost (est.).** A custom colour means the caster's minimum batch, which can be several sheets or a full run; the
artist's share, typically a royalty on each piece. **Timeline.** 2027, after the eight are in production.

**Why it raises the odds.** Scarcity and a name the press will print. A waitlist before a sheet is cast. A premium
over the standard tints. And the artist's audience, many of whom have never heard of a horn loudspeaker.

## (f) The Tint Library (my own)

**Mockup:** `out/idea-library.png`: eight tiles of 20 mm cast acrylic standing in a birch tray in the spectrum's
order, the lid behind, "The Tint Library". Path-traced.

**What.** A trade sample kit: the eight tints as 100 × 100 × 20 mm tiles, polished on every edge, in a birch tray, with
a card of the particulars. Sent to architects, interior designers, hotel groups and retailers who ask.

**Cost (est.).** Eight tiles cut from production sheet, polished, a tray and a printed lid: about $100 to $250 a kit
at 100 kits. **Timeline.** As soon as the sheet suppliers and their colours are chosen.

**Why it raises the odds.** No render shows what a tint looks like in a client's own light; a tile on a desk does.
Designers specify what they can hold. And making the kit forces the decision manufacturing needs anyway: the eight
cast colours, matched and fixed with the suppliers.

## (g) The Founding Hundred (my own)

**Mockup:** `out/idea-founding.png`: the certificate ("Pair thirty-seven of the first hundred.") and the rating
plate marked FOUNDING 037 / 100.

**What.** The first hundred pairs of Tropos, reserved with a deposit before the first is built, numbered on both
rating plates and kept on a register at Planet EARF. Each comes with a seat at the first Session and a coin for life.

**Cost.** Counsel's time on deposit terms, refunds and California's consumer rules, and the printing. **Timeline.**
Before the first production run; it needs a price and a lead time, which the owner hasn't yet set.

**Why it raises the odds.** It is the clearest evidence of demand an investor can be shown: a list of people who paid
to wait. The deposits fund the first run's materials. And the first hundred owners are the brand's first ambassadors,
each with a number they will want to show.
