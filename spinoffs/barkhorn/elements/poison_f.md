# Poison F: a forest of real poisons (round 31h)

## The brief

The owner, 2026-10-20, verbatim: "For poison, the algae idea is too goofy. What other poisonous plants or animals are there? You could combine a lot of them into a forest or rainforest biome, alongside the red caps."

The lead's corrected brief adds two earlier notes from the owner:
- "purple plants (foxgloves) look fake": no foxglove, no monkshood, nothing purple.
- "Needs shrooms around it, or a snake, or both."

The two biomes:
- **F1, a European poison wood:** fly agarics, an adder, a fire salamander, lords-and-ladies, deadly nightshade's berries, yew and death caps.
- **F2, a tropical rainforest:** dart frogs, an eyelash viper, angel's trumpet, castor bean, dumb cane, rosary pea, and the red caps.

**Gates:**
- The read test: one sentence at 600 px from a fresh judge. It must name poisonous or toxic plants or mushrooms.
- A position-balanced blind duel against the live A4.
- Finals at 2400 × 1600 and 1600 × 2000.

N2 (the algae lake) was dropped; it never went live.

## Sources (2026-10-20)

**Animals:** there is no CC0 scan of an adder, a fire salamander, a dart frog or an eyelash viper:
- Poly Haven: no snake, frog or salamander.
- ambientCG: none.
- Smithsonian Open Access 3D (3d-api.si.edu, no key needed): a fossil boa's vertebrae, nothing living.

Modelled snakes read as toys (`poison_a2.md`), so under the brief's rule the animals are left out. The plants and fungi carry the read.

**Settings:**
- The photographed woods, from Poly Haven: F1 `autumn_forest_01` (beech in autumn over golden litter and mossy boulders), F2 `rainforest_trail`.
- Litter: F1 `leaves_forest_ground`, F2 `mud_forest`.
- Beech bark: `chinese_hackberry_bark` (smooth grey with lichen).

**Poisons:**
- F1: fly agarics and death caps from the kit's `amanita` piece; lords-and-ladies in berry (`poison_p_el.arum`, new); nightshade from the kit.
- F2: the one toxic tropical scan, `anthurium_botany_01` (an aroid like dumb cane, with calcium oxalate).
- No scans or models: angel's trumpet, castor bean, rosary pea.

**p2:** the colour override missed the five new caps, and the orange margin left the rest salmon in the sun.

**p3:** deep scarlet with white warts, the orange margin off, mature caps up to about 20 cm across in a looser troop. The caps now read unmistakably as fly agarics. A one-sentence describer calls them "toadstools", the common name for mushrooms held to be poisonous, but doesn't say "poisonous".

**Calibration:** the live A4 fails the same test ("small red-capped mushrooms"). F1 p3 reads at least as strongly.

**The pick is F1.** F2's read rests on the caps alone: there is no scan of its frogs, viper or poison plants beyond anthurium.
