# Card: the EARF mark set (round 27; after its round 1, `critic/rounds/2026-10-16/brand-mark-r1.json`)

## What it is for

EARF makes horn loudspeakers. Its Cloud line (Atmosphere 01 to 08) is cabinets of tinted cast acrylic, one colour
each: 01 Ember red (Flare in rounds 21 to 29), 02 Dust orange, 03 Acid amber, 04 Poison bottle green, 05 Ice blue, 06 Haze violet, 07 Fog clear
white, 08 Smoke black. The mark is the acrylic globe: a sphere's three parallels and three meridians as six discs,
tilted as the Earth is (its axis leaning 23.4 degrees, tipped 16 toward us, 22 until round 27f), each disc a thin slab of tinted acrylic,
see-through. This set is every version the brand prints, etches and shows: colour (the six tints together, on its
site, its look book, its boxes), black and white (one ink, flat: paper, print, an etch, an emboss; white out of dark
grounds and photographs), and one in each speaker's own tint (that speaker's page, its box, its corner). Sheet 1 is the
set, each version on the ground it shows on; sheet 2 seven of them at 300, 64, 32 and 16 px; sheet 3 the lockups with
the wordmark EARF; sheet 4 the spins, a frame every 0.4 s (round 27f: black and colour, steady; round 27k: each disc a
slab); sheet 5 (round 27e)
the angle study, the tip since chosen; sheet 6 (round 27i) the set in line; sheet 7 (round 27j) the south pole at less
tip, a study; sheet 8 (round 27l) three ways for the spin to read as one solid object, a study, none chosen yet; sheet 9
(round 28) the globe rendered as the real object, with the one-colour fill and line traced from it, a study; sheet 10
(round 28c) the render as the colour mark (round 28b's translucent acrylic) on paper and glowing on dark, a meridian
side on in the black spin (one white stroke) and the line spin (one line), and the white line globe untrimmed, with the
parallels cut to the ring (the butt kept) and with everything cut; sheet 11 (round 28c) the spins, a frame every 0.4 s:
black, black in line, the flat acrylic; sheet 12 (round 28d) the render more see-through in the speakers' colours, on
paper and on dark (the study's n4), against 28b's, then the flash and the equator's crossing before and after, and a
rim on dark in the dim studio and in the plain grey room; sheet 13
(round 28d) the acrylics tried for it, from round 28's clear tints to 28b's translucent; sheet 14 (round 28e) the
render on light grounds in 28b, 28d and now, livelier, beside the dark version (unchanged), then the colour line before
and after in one weight, the black line, and the new spin 43.5 degrees into the turn; sheet 15 (round 28e) the renders

acrylic, the owner's picks framed.

## Fixed

The globe's geometry (parallels at 40 N, 0 and 40 S; meridians 60 degrees apart; the lean 23.44 and the tip 16, the owner's pick in round 27f, from 22); the
six discs alone, nothing filling the circle; the colour version: the render (round 28c), and as it is for printing
that takes no picture (round 25's acrylic: the green meridian facing us the backing, a single disc its own flat colour,
crossings stacked a step deeper); the colours' places (red north, blue the equator, yellow south, green the meridian
facing us, orange and violet the side meridians); the speakers' colours; the wordmark EARF (since round 29 the R's
bowl a ring, its hole 70 in) and the lockup's proportions; the grounds (paper #F2EFEA, ink #121114).

Chosen in round 27f: the tip 16 (sheet 5: the tips reach 0.04 of the radius past the front disc, against 0.07 at 22
and 0.02 at 10, where the parallels close toward lines; turning the globe on its axis only makes them reach further)
and the steady spin.

## The versions, and their parameters

- Black, white and each speaker's own (after round 1): the seamed discs, flat, each speaker's on the ground where it
  holds 3:1. Every piece shrunk by half of `seam` (0.035 of the radius) on every side, the outline's too, so no band
  runs round the outside (the owner: "The stroke around the monochromatic ones looks odd. Don't think we need it");
  pieces thinner than one seam dropped (`KEEP`, round 27l: at one and a half the green disc's rim either side of the
  south pole, 1.2 seams across, was dropped, and the outline broke there). Since round 28c the parallels, fill and
  line, are cut to the outer ring (`_ring`, `_cut`): the green disc's rim at rest, tipped with the globe, 1.0 across and
  0.961 along the axis, so it falls short of the sphere at the poles. The south parallel passed it at the lower left
  by 0.018 of the radius, a sliver whose stroke stood out beside the ring's. The meridians are not cut: their caps either
  side of the butt pass it by 0.038 and stay, as cut off the bottom no longer read as three-dimensional (every piece cut
  to the ring, round 27d's trim again, was tried and undone the same day). The parallels never move in the spin, so their
  cut is the same in every frame.
  The acrylic's thickness is a crescent inside each disc's edge, its cells the discs' faces and crossings alone
  ("clipping on the multicolor one wherever there is overlap"). At the south pole, which sits on the outline at this
  tip, the side meridians' caps stand either side of it, the line between them (the axis, where they cross) drawn: round 27j drew them as one, and the owner found it
  worse (round 27k). Small cuts (round 27k): the pieces the full drawing keeps, at its own seam, drawn with a wider
  seam, `cut` 0.05 of the radius up to 64 px and 0.06 up to 32 and 16 px, so every piece is still there and the form
  never changes (round 27's cuts dropped what their wider seams would not hold, which bit holes in the outline; round
  27j drew four discs). Only a piece the cut's own seam leaves under a seam across is left out (round 27l: the two
  segments by the south pole, under a pixel wide at 64 px), where it would draw as a grey speck.
- In motion (round 27e, new): the spin. The mark turns on its axis the Earth's way (the meridians round the pole, the
  parallels still), `rate` 45 degrees a second (a turn in 8 s), each loop `turn` 180 degrees (4 s: every coloured disc
  back in its place, so it loops without a seam and starts on the mark at rest), 30 frames a second, 720 px square
  with the mark's clear space, `clear` a quarter of its width, round it. Steady (the owner, round 27f): the top's
  version, its axis wobbling round a circle `wobble` 5 degrees across, moved the lean off the Earth's and is not made.
  In motion no piece is dropped (each narrows into its seam) and the scale back to the discs' width is the seam's own,
  so nothing pops between frames. Each disc is a slab `THICK` 0.05 of the radius thick (round 27k): as it nears edge on
  its face moves toward us by half the thickness it shows and its rim shows on the near side, the rim on the globe's
  surface so nothing covers it, the thickness shown t - w / 4 (w its oval's width across; seamed, t the ink's and the
  seam), so a disc narrows to a band as wide as it is thick, never to a line; at rest nothing changes. Black and
  colour, as MP4 and WebM; sheet 4 is a frame every 0.4 s. On its own axis the parallels never appear to move and the
  meridians slide through them, which reads as discs clipping through each other. Round 27l studies three ways to
  make it read as one solid object, for the owner to choose (`spinstudy.py`, sheet 8): A, the colour discs as opaque
  slabs lit by one light from the upper left, each face and rim toned by it; B, the black discs, each seamed piece a
  tone of the ink by its light; C, A turned about the upright, a turntable, every disc moving together. Until the
  owner chooses, the spins stay as they are. A meridian side on (round 28c): in the black spin the discs have no
  thickness (`disc_shapes`, keep 0), so as a meridian turns side on its face narrows away between its two seams, and
  side on it is one white stroke across the front of the globe, one seam (0.035 of the radius), as every other. 27k's
  slab had left a strip of ink no wider than the seams either side of it, which read as a crack, and round 28b a solid
  bar between hairlines; the owner wanted the white stroke "front and center" and "just like the rest of the white
  strokes". The line version spins too (round 28c, new; `earf-globe-black-stroke-spin`): the stroke
  version's own drawing at each angle, every piece's edge once at `w`, the discs given no thickness, so every line is
  one weight and a disc side on is one line (round 28's traced line drew each ring's front bold and its back thin,
  which the owner found needless). Each piece is cut to the ring at full precision: snapped to the grid one by one,
  two pieces' shared edge stopped matching and was drawn twice for a frame.
- The real object (round 28; the colour mark since round 28c; `globe_real.py`): earth.py's six discs, each a slab 0.05 of
  the radius thick, as the Cloud cabinets' 20 mm tinted cast acrylic (the globe 800 mm across), cemented clear where
  they cross, path-traced in Cycles with the engine's acrylic (clear_resin, roughness 0.05), through earth.py's own
  camera (square on, orthographic), on the ground as a backdrop of exactly its colour, a dim studio seen only in
  reflections. Tints: the cabinets' through glow() (each passes its own colour freely, so a long edge glows instead of
  going black; Acid passes red and green, so its edge stays yellow), their colour after 20 mm, not 25. Traced from it,
  in one colour: the fill (each face a sheet of 0.2 of the colour, building where sheets cross; each edge, the outline

  behind, its edge filled). A disc side on is one band as wide as the sheet is thick, in all three: no seams either
