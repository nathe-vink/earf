## V10-9 The half-fitted shade: the study

Numbers (the v10 solids): the shade (cover, collar, clamp ring, rings) weighs 974 to 998 g (about 9.6 to 9.8 N; A with the
lock about 10.0 N). The pins bear at about r 43; the rim is about r 100. Today, let go mid-turn, the shade rests on the flat
band 8 above its seat on friction: with mu about 0.3 (anodized aluminium on stainless, dry) that is about 0.12 N m, so about
1.2 N at the rim (2.5 N with the detent's drag, the v9 critic's 0.12 to 0.25 N m) turns it back to the entries, and it falls.

A ramp in the band's ceiling (each passage's ceiling rising toward its notch, so the weight drives the turn): it self-seats
only where the slope beats the friction, slope > mu.
- Full ramp, slope 0.4 over the whole band (about 17.5 at r 43, 23 deg of the turn): rise 7.0, so only a 1.0 step is left
  into the notch. Net driving torque W r (s - mu)/(1 + mu s): about 0.04 N m at mu 0.3 (thin), 0.10 N m greased (mu 0.15). It
  weakens the seated hold: one 1.0 mm lift and the shade can be turned back down the ramp.
- Part ramp, slope 0.4 over the band's last 10 (the last 13 deg): rise 4.0, a 4.0 step left. Let go in the last 13 deg, it
  finishes on its own (same margins); let go in the first 10 deg, it rests on friction as today.
- Either changes every collar's three slots, and the margin depends on a friction I can't measure here.

Recommendation: not drawn in V10. Keep the flat band with the NOTES line, and test the part ramp on the first prototype
collar (measure mu, try it), then decide.

What the owner should know, plainly: a shade turned but let go before it drops looks nearly on (it sits 8 mm high: its clamp
ring stands up the crown's first tier), but only friction holds it, and a wipe with a cloth can turn it back so it falls.
Always turn to the stop and feel it drop and click. The lock does not cure this: it engages only once the shade has dropped.
